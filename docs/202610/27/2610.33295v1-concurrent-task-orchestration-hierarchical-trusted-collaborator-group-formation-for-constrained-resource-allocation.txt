# Concurrent Task Orchestration: Hierarchical Trusted Collaborator Group Formation for Constrained Resource Allocation 

Jiazhi Chen, Member, IEEE, Xianbin Wang, Fellow, IEEE, 

and Xuemin (Sherman) Shen, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —Effective collaboration among resource-constrained mobile intelligent machines, such as Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), is essential for the completion of complex tasks. How-ever, concurrent task demands, limited distributed resources, and dynamic collaborator availabilities make it challenging to satisfy task-specific completion goals in diverse collaborative systems. To address these challenges simultaneously, we propose a new framework of trusted collaborator group formation and the supporting resource allocation strategy for concurrent task orchestration. Specifically, a collaborative task is decomposed into subtasks subject to dynamic resource availability and inter-subtask dependencies. A task utility model is developed for col-laborator group evaluation support subtask allocation according to task completion goals and unified value-risk analysis. Based on this model, concurrent task orchestration is formulated as a long-term utility maximization problem under α-fairness. To solve the problem efficiently, we develop a hierarchical matching strategy that first clusters tasks and collaborators into coarse-grained sets and subsequently performs fine-grained collabora-tor group formation and task allocation within matched sets. Task-specific trust is predicted through spatial-temporal Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and embedded into a fairness-aware Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework to continuously update the matching policies. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed strategy improves concurrent task completion per-formance by 22% over representative baselines under resource constraints. 

Index Terms —Task orchestration, trust, collaborator group, graph neural networks, deep reinforcement learning, fairness.

A. Motivation 

Given the heterogeneity and complexity of future collabora-tive systems, achieving efficient concurrent task orchestration through effective collaboration among intelligent machines under stringent resource constraints can be extremely challeng-ing. This challenge primarily stems from the trade-off between meeting diverse requirements of concurrent tasks, possibility of involving different groups of collaborators for one same task, and drastically increased complexity to optimize overall system efficiency under fairness considerations. From the perspective of an individual task execution, both the task decomposition into subtasks and the selection of a group of collaborators jointly entail task completion value and risks. On the one hand, task completion value is ful-filled through meeting diverse task completion goals such as latency and energy consumption requirements [4], thus imposing intricate criteria on how tasks are decomposed and collaborator groups are selected. Proper task decompo-sition supports fine-grained collaboration and aligns subtask-level completion goals with available resources. However, when task completion goals are ambiguously defined and trade-offs between competing goals are improperly balanced, the effectiveness of best-effort strategies such as Quality-of-Service (QoS)-based task offloading is significantly reduced [5]. Moreover, attacks launched by malicious machines, such as illegitimate collaborators [6] or eavesdroppers [7], further degrade task completion value. On the other hand, dynamic task requirements and varying collaborator conditions intro-duce significant uncertainty for collaborator group formation and task allocation, thus increasing risks for task completion [8]. While some existing approaches such as heuristic-based task offloading select suitable collaborator groups, they often rely on static and complete system information [9], [10], making them ineffective in highly dynamic scenarios. arXiv:2610.33295v1 [cs.NI] 23 Oct 2026 2

From the perspective of system orchestration, sharing lim-ited resources of all collaborators among concurrent tasks remains a major challenge in guaranteeing the task completion value and tolerable risks for each task. Specifically, diverse task requirements induce increased dynamics in resource re-quirements, thereby intensifying uncertain contention among concurrent tasks for limited resources. This uncertainty in resource contention is eventually translated into unpredictable task completion outcomes and value. Thus, effective orchestra-tion of concurrent tasks is needed to allocate limited resources across all completing tasks, aiming to maximize the collective task completion value and minimize associated risks simulta-neously. Existing strategies typically formulate concurrent task orchestration as an optimization problem aiming to maximize the cumulative utilities of all concurrent tasks [11]. However, such objectives may lead to disproportionate outcomes, where some tasks could dominate allocated resources while others are persistently underserved. While short-term fairness is often unattainable under resource constraints, enforcing long-term fairness , i.e., fair task allocation over time, is essential to prevent persistent resource monopolization. Task-specific trust serves as a goal-achieving mechanism to direct the selection of reliable collaborators aligned with diverse completion goals of tasks [12]. We extend this concept from a single collaborator evaluation to a group evaluation, ensuring collective capability and reliability of collaborator groups in meeting the global requirements of tasks. Machine Learning (ML) supports adaptive collaborator group selection in dynamic environments by learning complex relationships between task requirements and collaborator conditions [13]. Among ML algorithms, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) particularly supports sequential decision-making under com-plexity and dynamics, making it well-suited for optimizing long-term outcomes [14], [15]. Thus, the integration of task-specific trust and DRL facilitates the automatic formation and matching of reliable collaborator groups aligned with evolving task goals. However, while DRL performs well under fully observable environments, partial observability of system states complicates such integration. Moreover, DRL typically maximizes long-term rewards, i.e., the cumulative utilities of all tasks [16], without considering long-term fairness, leading to imbalanced resource allocation among tasks. In addition, DRL model training in large-scale systems often becomes time-consuming and suffers from slow convergence [3], [17]. Based on these observations, an effective concurrent task orchestration strategy should jointly account for heteroge-neous task completion goals, dynamic collaborator conditions, scalable group formation, and long-term fairness, rather than optimizing these aspects in isolation 

B. Related Work 

Reliable task orchestration in dynamic collaborative systems requires trust-aware collaborator selection and fair resource allocation. Accordingly, we review trust-based, learning-based, and fairness-aware task orchestration. 

1) Trust-Based Task Orchestration: Trust has become in-creasingly important in orchestrating tasks, especially when security threats are involved. Beyond using trust scores derived from historical task success and responsiveness to defend against internal attacks in task orchestration [18], some studies have leveraged such scores and their resulting trust-based rankings for service provider selection and task offloading [19]–[22]. For graph-structured tasks, [12] formulated the task orchestration problem as graph matching optimization to select an optimal set of trusted collaborators for task completion. However, the trust evaluation methods in this work is sensitive to weight settings of different trust factors and depend on prior knowledge, rendering it susceptible to inaccurate or biased evaluations. ML predict trust by capturing specific patterns in trust-related data [23], enabling automatic weight learning and achieving high accuracy with sufficient data. While Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) enable end-to-end trust evaluation from raw graph data [24], [25], existing methods are typically designed for fixed graph structures and static trust, limiting their ability to model dynamically evolving, task-specific relationships and incurring high training overhead in large-scale systems, without supporting group-level, task-specific trust. 

2) Learning-Based Task Orchestration: Motivated by the capability of DRL for automatic decision-making, extensive studies have been conducted to incorporate DRL in task orchestration [3], typically categorized into value-based and policy-based methods. For instance, Deep-Q-Network (DQN) learns the control policy via value function optimization [26], whereas deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) estimates policy gradients to directly optimize the control policy [27]. However, these DRL methods rely on complete state infor-mation and neglect interactive dependencies between agents. To address these limitations, recent efforts have shifted toward state abstraction techniques, with GNN emerging as a powerful tool for modeling structural and temporal dependencies to boost training, such as adopting GNN to encode cluster topol-ogy or extract inter-subtask dependencies [11]. However, these GNN-based DRL assume fully observable and trustworthy environments, reducing adaptability to unseen task structures, thus incurring high training overhead. Some studies integrate trust into DRL [28], [29] by leveraging historical trust to guide decision-making. However, in these works, trust is treated as an auxiliary, task-agnostic variable within standard DRL focused on reward maximization, without capturing task-specific features and long-term fairness. 

3) Fairness-Aware Task Orchestration: Building on existing fairness metrics, such as Jain’s index, entropy function, and min-max ratio, fairness-aware task orchestration has been widely explored in network optimization. Recent method in [10] overlooks long-term variations in task requirements and resource conditions and simplifies fairness optimization through convex assumptions, which is unrealistic in practi-cal scenarios. To guarantee long-term fairness, [9] and [30] proposed dynamic resource allocation algorithms that rely on current system states without assuming prior distribution knowledge. However, these studies focus on specific fairness criteria (e.g., max-min or proportional fairness) rather than a general and tunable formulation such as α-fairness, which encompasses diverse system fairness objectives. Furthermore, various DRL algorithms have been applied to fair task or-3

chestration in domains such as cognitive radio networks [31], yet their heuristic design restricts their adaptability to α-fairness in optimization. Some DRL algorithms have shown potential in optimizing α-fairness [32]. However, they depend on Monte Carlo-based gradient estimation, resulting in slow convergence and remain decoupled from trust-aware and hier-archical decision-making. 

C. Contributions 

Motivated by these observations, we propose a fairness-aware concurrent task orchestration strategy that jointly per-forms trusted collaborator group formation and task allocation under dynamic and resource-constrained conditions. The con-tributions of this paper are summarized below. • We design a dynamic task utility model, rather than relying solely on static performance metrics, to guide collaborator group formation and task allocation aligned with diverse task completion goals. Tasks are decomposed into limited subtasks under resource constraints while preserving inter-subtask dependencies, and collaborator groups are evaluated via a unified value-risk task utility that captures both goal satisfaction and deviation under dynamic conditions. Based on this task utility model, concurrent task orchestration is modeled as a long-term average utility maximization problem under α-fairness, enabling joint optimization of performance and fairness beyond conventional formulations. • We develop a hierarchical matching strategy that forms and matches trusted collaborator groups in a coarse-to-fine manner for scalable and adaptive task orchestration under long-term utility optimization. Instead of selecting individual collaborators, the strategy models group-level capability and reliability for task completion. To improve scalability in large-scale systems, tasks and collaborators are first clustered into coarse-grained sets based on feature similarity, within which fine-grained group formation and subtask allocation are per-formed, reducing mismatches between dynamic task specifics and varying collaborator conditions. • To support the hierarchical matching strategy, we integrate task-specific trust prediction with fairness-aware policy learn-ing into a unified framework. Specifically, GNNs infer task-specific trust by capturing spatial-temporal dependencies be-tween tasks and collaborators across both coarse-grained and fine-grained levels. These trust representations are then em-bedded into a fairness-aware DRL framework to guide long-term matching policies. Each matching process is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where predicted trust values serve as abstracted states and fairness-aware rewards are derived by adjusting task utilities based on fairness objectives and historical reward statistics. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section II describes the system model and problem formulation. Sec-tion III details our proposed strategy. Remark 1: Solving the mixed integer programming prob-lem in (16) is extremely challenging due to 1) the difficulty of long-term average optimization, induced by inherent uncer-tainties from dynamic task requirements, varying collaborator conditions, and diverse attacks in collaborative systems; and 2) the need to maximize the total task utility of all concurrent tasks, while simultaneously ensuring long-term fairness in resource allocation and allowing short-term fairness variations due to system dynamics such as task urgency and limited resources. FORMATION AND TASK ALLOCATION 

To solve (16), we propose a hierarchical strategy for trusted collaborator group formation and task allocation under long-term fairness. As shown in Fig. 2, the framework consists of two core modules: 1) Hierarchical Task-Specific Trust predic-tion via GNNs (HTST-GNN) at coarse- and fine-grained levels, and 2) trust-embedded fairness-aware DRL for coarse-to-fine matching. Online inference is coordinated by nearby RSUs, while model training is performed offline and periodically. 

A. Hierarchical Task-Specific Trust Prediction via GNNs 

Dynamic task requirements and collaborator conditions directly affect the objective in (16), while representing all such factors cannot efficiently capture their interdependencies. We therefore use task-specific trust to abstract the value and risk of task-resource interactions. The proposed HTST-GNN dynamically coarsens tasks and resources and performs spatial-temporal aggregation to predict trust at both coarse-and fine-grained levels. B. Comparison Strategies 

We compare the proposed strategy with representative baselines for dynamic collaborator selection. Each baseline removes or simplifies one or more core components to isolate their contributions. 

1) GDTrust [12] : A strategy that selects collaborators ac-cording to weighted trust scores updated upon task completion. 

2) TrustGNN [24] : A GNN-based collaborator selection strategy using the same spatial architecture as ours but without temporal feature aggregation. 

3) SARSA [16] : An RL strategy that maximizes long-term reward without considering trust and fairness. 

4) FairPG [32] : A fairness-aware policy-gradient strategy using Monte Carlo sampling.

D. Robustness and Parameter Sensitivity Analysis (Q2) 

We evaluate robustness across problem sizes, similarity thresholds, and fairness objectives. Task completion goals are randomly generated, with results averaged over 10,000 and 15,000 steps for proportional and α-fairness utilities, respectively. TrustGNN, GDTrust, and SARSA perform worse because they do not jointly ac-count for long-term fairness, dynamic trust, and hierarchical matching. Although FairPG explicitly optimizes fairness, its Monte Carlo-based policy gradient converges more slowly. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper has proposed a fairness-aware concurrent task orchestration strategy for trusted collaborator group forma-tion and task allocation in resource-constrained collaborative systems. A unified task utility model captures the value–risk tradeoff of task completion, based on which concurrent task orchestration is formulated as a long-term average utility max-imization problem under α-fairness. To solve this problem, a hierarchical coarse-to-fine matching framework integrates task-specific trust prediction via spatial-temporal GNNs with fairness-aware DRL. The predicted trust values provide ab-stracted state representations for matching decisions, while fairness-aware rewards balance long-term task utilities under resource contention. Simulation results demonstrate improved task completion performance over representative baselines and consistent robustness under varying problem sizes, fairness objectives, and attack conditions. In particular, the proposed strategy improves concurrent task completion performance by 22% over representative baselines and maintains an average attack resistance rate of approximately 93% even when 50% of UGVs launch on-off attacks.
