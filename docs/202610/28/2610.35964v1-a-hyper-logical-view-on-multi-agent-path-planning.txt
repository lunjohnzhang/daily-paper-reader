# A Hyper-Logical View on Multi-Agent Path Planning 

## Hadar Frenkel, Dor Atzmon 

Bar-Ilan University 

Abstract 

Multi-Agent Path Planning (MAPP) requires collision-free paths for multiple agents. MAPP has numerous real-life ap-plications, including the navigation of automated vehicles, and has therefore many variants considering different constraints, requirements, and objectives. For each such variant, a new algorithmic treatment is often developed. This paper takes a complementary, specification-centered view: we show that many MAPP variants can be modeled in HyperLTL , a tempo-ral logic that reasons about multiple paths and their evolution through time, and in its ω-regular extension HyperQPTL. The contribution is not a claim that current hyperproperty model checkers replace specialized MAPP solvers. Rather, the hyper-logical view gives a unified language for comparing MAPP variants, adding cross-agent constraints, expressing optimal-ity, and connecting MAPP to verification, explanation, and causality techniques for hyperproperties. 

## 1 Introduction 

In Multi-Agent Path Planning (MAPP), we require planning a set of paths for a set of agents without conflicts (colli-sions). MAPP is derived from various real-world applica-tions in which physical agents must navigate without col-liding with other agents. These applications range from in-door environments, such as robot navigation in automated warehouses (Hönig et al. 2019; Li et al. 2020), to planning outdoor routes for autonomous vehicles (e.g., cars, trains, or drones) (Atzmon, Diei, and Rave 2019; Li et al. 2019). MAPP is general and, due to its broad applicability, has many variants addressing different real-life tasks of plan-ning conflict-free paths. A commonly known MAPP variant is Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) (Stern et al. 2019), in which each agent’s path leads to its given goal. Op-timally solving MAPF for standard objective functions is NP-hard (Surynek 2010; Yu and LaValle 2013b). Neverthe-less, efficient algorithms optimally solve MAPF for many agents (Zhang et al. 2020; Li et al. 2021; Boyarski et al. 2022; Lam et al. 2022). A prominent optimal MAPF algorithm is Conflict-Based Search (CBS) (Sharon et al. 2015). CBS plans a path for each agent separately. Then, repeatedly, it resolves conflicts between the paths by imposing constraints and replanning new paths, until conflict-free paths are found.

Besides MAPF, we define eight additional MAPP variants (nine, including MAPF) studied in the literature. For most of them, a specialized algorithm was designed, often by adapt-ing CBS or another MAPF technique to the specific variant. This paper presents a different view on MAPP problems: instead of proposing another dedicated solver, we show that many MAPP variants can be expressed modularly in the tem-poral logic HyperLTL (Clarkson et al. 2014) and its omega-regular counterpart HyperQPTL (Rabe 2016). HyperLTL is an extension of the prominent logic LTL - Linear Temporal Logic (Pnueli 1977a), that can express the behavior of an agent over time, thus allowing us to express properties such as an agent starts in vertex s and eventually reaches ver-tex g, but also much more elaborate properties, as we show later in the paper. While LTL can reason about one path at a time, HyperLTL reasons about the relations between differ-ent paths, thus, it serves as a natural specification language for MAPP problems. Moreover, it allows us to express not only the requirements posed in the various MAPP instances described below, but we also show that HyperLTL can ex-press additional constraints and behaviors that are not easily captured by CBS (see Section 3.1). In Section 5, we cover the related work regarding other connections between planning and temporal (hyper)properties. 

Contributions (1) We present a modular translation of MAPP problems in HyperLTL or HyperQPTL (Section 3.3), including examples of cross-agent constraints that are cum-bersome to express in decoupled path-planning formalisms (Section 3.1); (2) We show how to express cost functions, and in particular makespan, as hyperproperties (Section 4.1); (3) We reduce finding an optimal solution for the modeled MAPP variants to HyperQPTL (or HyperLTL) model check-ing (Section 4.3); (4) We clarify the intended role of the reduction: it is a solver-independent specification and reason-ing layer, not an empirical claim that current hyperproperty tools outperform mature MAPP solvers.

## 3 Modeling MAPP in HyperLTL 

In this section, we present simple and modular translations of the various MAPP problems into respective HyperLTL formulas. We separate between the core planning problem and optimality w.r.t. the cost function, allowing modularity both between different problems and different cost functions. We discuss translations of cost functions in Section 4. 

3.1 Motivation 

The main goal of this paper is to make the logical structure of MAPP variants explicit. A hyper-logical specification sepa-rates what is common across variants (start conditions, goal satisfaction, and collision avoidance) from what changes be-tween them. Thus, moving from one variant to another often amounts to replacing a local component of the specification rather than redesigning an algorithm from scratch. This is useful even before considering tool performance: it gives a concise way to compare variants, expose hidden assump-tions, identify redundant constraints, and state new require-ments without committing to a particular solver architecture. This scope also explains why a large empirical comparison is not the right central test for the present contribution. Cur-rent HyperLTL and HyperQPTL model checkers are general hyperproperty-verification tools, and their worst-case com-plexity is high (Mascle and Zimmermann 2020; Rabe 2016). We therefore do not claim that existing tools compete with 

> 3HyperQPTL does not add computational complexity (Rabe 2016); Since HyperLTL is a more common formalism, we choose to use it when possible.

mature MAPP solvers on standard benchmarks. Such an ex-periment would primarily measure the present maturity of hyperproperty model checking, rather than the value of the specification layer. The intended contribution is instead a for-mal bridge: once a MAPP variant is written as a decidable hyperproperty, existing and future verification procedures can be applied to it, and the formula itself can serve as a solver-independent reference specification. Another key point in our modeling is that HyperLTL di-rectly expresses relations between different paths. This obvi-ously allows us to express MAPP problems, which inherently require reasoning about the paths of all agents; but it also al-lows us to express additional requirements on the paths of the agents, which are usually not easy to express by decoupled MAPP algorithms, like CBS, which repeatedly plans paths for single agents. For example, we can specify that the path of agent a1 is always close to the path of agent a2, by defining a set of neighbor vertices N (v) = {v′ | (v, v ′) ∈ E}, and defining the following HyperLTL constraint, where πi is the path of agent ai for i = 1 , 2:

The point is not that HyperLTL replaces these algorithmic ideas, but that it provides a common language in which pairwise and beyond-pairwise path relations can be stated, compared, and eventually verified.

4.2 Sum-of-Costs 

SOC requires accumulating all path lengths rather than com-paring the latest goal-satisfaction time. Although the labels used for makespan can identify each individual path length, their sum requires asynchronous temporal reasoning over positions of different traces. This is beyond synchronous Hy-perLTL/HyperQPTL, but can be expressed in asynchronous temporal hyperlogics, e.g. (Bombardelli et al. 2025), by con-structing and comparing a trace that represents the accumu-lated path lengths.

Quantifier Alternation An important measure for the complexity of a HyperLTL or HyperQPTL formula is in the number of path-quantifier alternations: from existential to universal and vice versa. Each such alternation adds one exponent to the worst-case complexity of the model-checking problem. For example, the worst-case complexity of formu-las of the type ∃π∀π′.φ where φ is quantifier free, is ex-ponentially harder than the worst case complexity of ∀π′.φ 

formulas. Thus, we will always aim at formulas with mini-mal number of quantifier alternations. We observe that the modeling of almost all problems contains no path quanti-fiers within the formula, thus the only path quantification we introduce is the universal quantification in Section 4.1 and the existential quantification of Theorem 2, resulting in an ∃∗∀∗ formula, which is needed already for simpler prob-lems than the ones we study here (Beutner and Finkbeiner 2025). We emphasize that reducing MAPP to HyperQPTL model checking is not an efficient solution given the current tools for hyperproperty verification, such as AutoHyper and related model checkers (Beutner and Finkbeiner 2023a,c; Hsu, Sánchez, and Bonakdarpour 2021; Hsu et al. 2023). This limitation is orthogonal to the modeling contribution: the formulas identify the exact hyperproperty that a solution must satisfy, while specialized MAPP algorithms remain the right baseline for large-scale performance. At the same time, the formulas isolate where complexity enters (for example, through quantifier alternation), which can guide future tool development for the reachability-heavy fragment used here. 

## 5 Related Work 

We consider a set of one-shot MAPP variants, which end when the given tasks are fulfilled. However, online (Švancara et al. 2019) and lifelong (Wan et al. 2018) variants introduce, arguably, the more realistic cases where new agents or tasks arrive over time, and a new solution must be computed. Our modeling can also be generalized for these cases by considering a sequence of one-shot problems. In addition to search-based approaches, like CBS, there ex-ist reduction-based approaches for optimally solving MAPP. These include reducing MAPP to Integer-Linear Program (ILP) (Yu and LaValle 2013a), Answer Set Programming (ASP) (Erdem et al. 2013), and SAT (Surynek et al. 2016; Barták and Svancara 2019). Reducing MAPP to either of these problems, requires encoding temporal constraints, e.g., by adding many auxiliary variables. In contrast, HyperLTL and HyperQPTL offer a higher abstraction level, and natively express paths and the temporal constraints. Many works have studied the relation between planning and temporal properties. (Pontiggia et al. 2025) study the connection between probabilistic hyperproperties and de-centralized planning. While their work is concerned with undecidable problems in the probabilistic setting, we focus on the Boolean setting, and put more emphasis on modeling concrete MAPP problems, and, on studying a wider variety of problems. We show that we can express most problems in a modular way, as a conjunction of formulas, thus one can easily adjust only the relevant part(s) when introducing new problems. (Wang, Nalluri, and Pajic 2020) study the con-nection between HyperLTL and robotics, and (Beutner and Finkbeiner 2025) study the connection between hyperprop-erties and conformant planning. While they discuss hyper-properties and planning, our work studies multi-agent path planning. (Beutner and Finkbeiner 2021, 2023b) present a hyperlogic to reason about multi-agent systems, in which they express properties such as information-flow and secu-rity properties, over multi-agent systems. However, they do not address real-life planning problems as we study here. In contrast to all of the above, in this work, we study a different aspect of the relation between hyperlogics and multi-agent systems. We are interested in modeling existing problems from the planning community , in an existing hyper-logic, thus establishing the tight connection between the two communities. We stress that all of the above works do not ap-ply their results on actual planning problems, that are widely studied in the planning community. In addition, none of the above works concern cost functions and optimality of the solutions, as we discuss in Section 4. This distinguishes our work from empirical MAPP papers: our comparison point is not the runtime of a new solver, but the expressive match between MAPP variants and temporal hyperproperties. 

## 6 Conclusion and Future Work 

In this paper, we have established the fundamental connec-tion between Multi-Agent Path Planning (MAPP) problems and hyperlogics. Modeling the different problems in a formal language helps expose the subtleties of the variants, remove accidental differences between formulations, and specify fu-ture problems using a clean and unambiguous formalism. The value of this view is therefore conceptual and method-ological: it gives MAPP researchers a common specifica-tion language and gives the hyperproperties community a family of concrete planning problems with rich cross-trace structure. A specific domain in which our study can be ap-plicable is explainability and causality, where we plan to apply explainability techniques for hyperproperties (Horak et al. 2022; Coenen et al. 2022) to the setting of MAPP. A MAPP solution is naturally represented by several witness traces, and the formulas in this paper state which relations among those traces make the plan valid. This creates a direct interface to formal explanations: one can ask which trace re-lations caused a conflict to be avoided, a goal to be delayed, or a group constraint to hold. In addition, we plan to study further constraints on the planning problems (as mentioned briefly in Section 3.1), and combine them with verification tasks, to use the full power of hyper-temporal specifications.
