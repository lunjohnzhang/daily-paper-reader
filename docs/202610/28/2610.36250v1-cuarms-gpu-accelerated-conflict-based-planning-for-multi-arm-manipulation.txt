# cuArms: GPU-Accelerated Conflict-Based Planning for Multi-Arm Manipulation 

Philip Huang 1,2, Fabio Ramos 1,3, Jiaoyang Li 2, Caelan Garrett ∗,1, Lars Johannsmeier ∗,1

Abstract —Multi-arm manipulation has the potential to dra-matically improve the throughput, efficiency, and dexterity of many manipulation tasks. Recent work coordinates multiple robot arms in a shared workcell by allocating and scheduling tasks for greater parallelism, jointly planning multi-arm motions, or directly learning multi-arm policies. So far, these approaches are either too slow, rely on domain-specific assumptions, or scale poorly with the number of robots and task horizon. Thus, we introduce cuArms , a conflict-based planner co-designed with GPU parallelism that efficiently decomposes the search space using reusable multi-modal roadmaps for individual robots and resolves conflicts and deadlocks through a lazy search. To jointly optimize task assignment, schedules, motions, and manipulation skills, we integrate a high-level scheduler that proposes candidate skeletons for cuArms to ground into coordinated manipulation plans. Experimental results show that our method establishes a new state of the art, with real-time initial solutions and anytime improvements in both motion quality and scheduling, without days of domain-specific training required by learning approaches. Videos of both simulated and real-world industrial tasks are available on https://cuarms.github.io/. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

As robotic manipulator hardware becomes more widely available and less expensive, multi-arm robotic manipulation— using multiple arms to complete tasks in a shared work cell— becomes much more affordable and accessible. A multi-arm system improves task throughput with greater parallelization, increases space utilization on factory floors, and becomes necessary for tasks that require more dexterous solutions and bimanual coordination, or the handling of bulky parts. Realizing this promise requires tightly coordinating the motions and manipulation skills of multiple robots that share the same workspace to avoid collisions. Specifically, there are several technical challenges: task allocation (which robot is assigned to each task), scheduling (the order and duration of each robot’s actions), safety (robots must not collide), and reasoning over hybrid action spaces (e.g., contacts, grasps, kinematic modality changes, reachability). Current research in this area is often domain-specific [1], [2], time-consuming [3], [4], and cannot plan bimanual manipulation skills [3], [5]. To make these increasingly difficult problems tractable, we introduce cuArms , a new planner that tightly couples GPU ac-celeration with a conflict-based multi-agent planner [6] to find long-horizon, multi-modal, and multi-arm manipulation plans. This combination attacks the computational bottleneck from both sides: algorithmic decompositions reduce the search com-plexity, while GPU parallelism accelerates expensive geomet-ric validations. cuArms constructs a multi-modal roadmap [7] for each robot, searches the joint configuration space to resolve

Our work builds on prior work in multi-agent pathfinding (MAPF), multi-arm manipulation, and accelerated planning. 

A. Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) 

MAPF algorithms [10] coordinate thousands of robots on discrete space-time graphs to find collision-free paths. Pri-ority inheritance with backtracking (PIBT) [11] is a seminal algorithm that significantly improves speed and scalability in MAPF by resolving conflicts for single-step actions. How-ever, PIBT is incomplete and can livelock. Lazy constraints addition search for MAPF (LaCAM) [6] embeds PIBT as a lazy expansion step in a complete depth-first search of the joint configuration space. db-LaCAM [12] extends LaCAM to kinodynamic multi-robot motion planning with motion primi-tives. However, LaCAM-style search has only been shown for mobile robots, as building graphs and robot collision checking for arms is much more expensive. cuArms uses LaCAM-style search for contact-rich multi-arm manipulation tasks with GPU-accelerated search and roadmap construction. 

B. Multi-Arm Manipulation Planning 

Recent work has explored how sampling-based motion plan-ning methods [13] or MAPF techniques [3] can find efficient and collision-free motions for multi-arm systems. However, these are limited to single-query start-to-goal motion planning. Several approaches [4], [14] extend sampling-based planners to long-horizon, multi-arm pick-and-place and handover tasks. These methods are limited by collision checking as the dom-inant computational bottleneck. Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) [15] offers a general model-based hybrid planning framework for long-horizon ma-nipulation planning. ScheduleStream [16] searches over tem-poral, durative actions with a code-based domain description to find multi-arm schedule skeletons. However, ScheduleStream samples conflict-free motions with rejection sampling, which is ineffective if the paths are highly intertwined. Other ap-proaches for multi-arm TAMP also depend on postprocess-ing [17] or synchronization [18] to plan multi-arm motions. In contrast, cuArms couples individual robot roadmaps with a conflict-driven search in their composite space to find asynchronous trajectories and skills jointly. Application-driven systems exploit task structure to scale planning for multi-arm assembly [1], [2], but task-specific hierarchies and constraints make them difficult to generalize to new tasks and objectives. In contrast, cuArms is domain-agnostic, and a human or AI agent can define new problems without extending the planner. 

C. Accelerated Motion Planning 

One trend to improve motion planning is to accelerate com-putation with hardware such as CPU SIMD (e.g., VAMP [19]) or GPUs (e.g., cuRobo [20]). GPU kinematic solvers and planners often directly use optimized libraries such as Py-Torch [20] or JAX [21], making a Python-based codebase possible for better user and agentic experiences. A similar trend appears in task and motion planning: cuTAMP [22] uses a GPU to evaluate many task skeletons in parallel. For multiple robot arms, VAMP-MR [5] is a recent multi-arm motion planner that vectorizes collision checking with CPU AVX2 instructions without algorithmic modification, but collision checking remains the computational bottleneck. To reduce this bottleneck using a GPU while avoiding excessive CPU-to-GPU communication, we design cuArms to evaluate collisions early and in a few large batches rather than naively moving an existing CPU-based planner onto the GPU. CU ARMS ALGORITHM 

cuArms disaggregates the multi-arm manipulation coordi-nation problem into three phases: roadmap construction, joint coordination, and anytime refinement. The key insight is to separate the reusable single-robot geometry from inter-robot interactions and motion refinement, then co-design each algorithmic phase around GPU-batched geometric computa-tions to maximize efficiency. Additionally, cuArms jointly coordinates skills and motions for long-horizon tasks through a conflict-based multi-agent planning method. This differs from rescheduling solutions that are generated independently [16] or sequentially [1], or using composite-space sampling and prioritized planning [4]. B. GPU-Accelerated Conflict-Driven Search 

Given the per-robot roadmaps, cuArms builds on La-CAM [6] to plan multi-arm, multi-task, and multi-modal mo-tion and manipulation skills. The original LaCAM is a depth-first search algorithm that lazily expands the successor joint state guided by the single-step inter-robot conflict resolution algorithm PIBT [11]. Fig. 4 illustrates this on a toy example, where R1 must first yield for R2’s pick, then R2 retreats with B to make room for R1. Planning motion independently cannot resolve this deadlock via retiming alone, while sequential planning must explicitly order the inter-robot actions in the schedule skeleton. However, the original LaCAM does not track task-level progress for long-horizon manipulation skills. Resolving conflicts for arms with PIBT is also slow because the recursive priority inheritance repeatedly invokes expensive inter-robot collision checks. Compared to LaCAM, cuArms augments the roadmap node vi with its current symbolic action ˆa, and each search node s stores the joint roadmap and action states 

⟨v1, ˆa1, . . . v |R|, ˆa|R|⟩ (line 4). A constraint queue s.K stores the action-constraint set κ that forces certain action selection in PIBT, allowing LaCAM to lazily explore alternative joint successors without enumerating all action combinations up-front (line 16). The joint state determines the current ma-nipulation mode and whether the schedule skeleton allows each robot’s next action. At each state, cuArms expands a fixed list Ui(s) of candidate motions, waits, and manipulation skills for each robot (line 13). Skills become available when all participating robots reach their entry nodes and have no blocking precedence constraints. Completing a skill advances the robots to their exit nodes, advances action stages, and applies any mode transitions. cuArms sorts the candidate list 

Ui(s) by first prioritizing manipulation skills, then actions that lead to low cost-to-go at the resulting state s′

The key innovation that accelerates LaCAM is to com-pute the geometric conflicts upfront and in parallel on the GPU (line 15), before the recursive PIBT action selection (line 17) . In the original PIBT, robots move on a shared graph, and each candidate next vertex has at most one current robot occupant. PIBT processes robots in descending order of dynamic priority so that higher-priority robots choose actions first, and each robot selects its next vertex based on the cost-to-goal heuristic while avoiding higher-priority robots. If a robot requests a vertex occupied by another robot whose next action is undecided, the occupant temporarily inherits its priority and is recursively asked to move; if it cannot, PIBT backtracks and tries another candidate. For robot arms, a candidate edge can conflict with several arms simultaneously, and the recursive geometry check to find conflicts with other arms is expensive. Running these recursive checks on the GPU requires repeated kernel launches and synchronization overhead, which limits GPU parallelism. Instead, cuArms gathers the GPU-resident collision geometry for all candidate edges and batch-checks all inter-robot candidate pairs, using aligned robot trajectories for motions and enclosing volumes for skills. It then runs PIBT by looking up the resulting Boolean conflict table during priority inheritance and backtracking. This design separates the recur-sive CPU control flow from batched GPU computation and avoids repeated GPU launches and CPU-GPU synchronization. 

cuArms uses PIBT to generate conflict-free joint successors within the lazily constrained LaCAM search. For bimanual skills, cuArms commits and constrains participating robots’ actions in PIBT and LaCAM together. cuArms can also exploit precedence constraints from the skeleton Σ if a move is blocked by directly prioritizing yield motions with cost-to-home heuristics in candidate ranking. The search terminates when every robot completes its action sequence, and the solution path is converted into the timed plan τ ⋆.

cuArms improves its initial plan through batched multi-arm shortcutting co-designed to maximize GPU parallelism. First, for each move trajectory, cuArms enumerates a bounded set of straight-line interpolations in joint space as shortcuts and batch-evaluates whether these shortcuts are collision-free with the static environment and movable objects O under its current mode. From all valid single-robot shortcuts, cuArms randomly samples a large batch of multi-arm shortcuts. A multi-arm shortcut can be a single-arm path or prioritized shortcut, where all other arms’ trajectories remain the same [9] or a composite shortcut made of at least two concurrent single-arm shortcuts. cuArms then evaluates whether these multi-arm shortcuts have any inter-robot conflicts in parallel. After finding a set of valid multi-arm shortcuts, cuArms greedily combines non-overlapping shortcuts in descending order of cost improvement, replacing the original robot trajectory. V. The full multi-arm TAMP problem requires deciding which robots perform which tasks, in what order, and with which skill parameters, in addition to solving the multi-arm manipulation planning problem. A high-level scheduler or temporal planner often solves task allocation and scheduling and evaluates manipulation modes– such as object placements and grasps–effectively, but is less effective at finding tightly coordinated motions. We introduce a hierarchical schedule-then-coordinate ap-proach in which a task-level scheduler generates candidate schedule skeletons while cuArms grounds them into tightly coordinated motion and manipulation trajectories that allow asynchronous execution. The scheduler may be a general temporal planner such as ScheduleStream [16], a domain-specific optimization program, or derived from a fixed-order assembly plan. If cuArms fails to find a feasible plan for any candidate skeletons, the system backtracks to the scheduler to generate additional skeletons. In practice, the scheduler can generate a batch of candidate skeletons upfront, which reduces the need for backtracking. cuArms also reuses the same multi-modal roadmap stored in GPU memory when the modes are shared; when new modes are introduced, it updates only the mode-validity masks without modifying the base roadmap. The resulting TAMP hierarchy evaluates many candidate schedule skeletons to improve from an initially infeasible or suboptimal manipulation plan. VI. E XPERIMENTS 

To evaluate cuArms , we examine three questions: 1) How quickly does it find an initial solution? 2) How does it improve solution quality with additional planning time? 3) How does it scale with the number of tasks and robots? 

Environments and Baselines. We evaluate in two simulated benchmarks as well as real-world bimanual electronic assem-bly tasks. We first evaluate cuArms in a welding task proposed by RoboBallet [24], a state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL)-based method. We retrain the RL policies from scratch in about two days. Next, we analyze the performance of 

cuArms for TAMP problems in a set of tabletop manipulation tasks against ScheduleStream [16], a general temporal planner for multi-arm TAMP and scheduling with GPU acceleration. Lastly, we show how cuArms can be deployed in real-world bimanual electronics assembly tasks with integrated manipu-lation skill sampling and coordinated manipulation planning.

A. RoboBallet: Many-Arm and Long-Horizon Manipulation 

RoboBallet [24] is an RL method for scaling the number of arms and tasks for multi-arm scheduling and motion planning. The evaluation environment requires 4 or 8 arms performing 10 or 40 spot welding tasks in a shared, tight workspace with obstacles, with the goal of reaching a set of Cartesian weld targets (Fig. 1 (b)). RoboBallet learns a single policy in simulation that jointly allocates and schedules tasks and generates coordinated motions. RoboBallet provides neither completeness nor formal safety guarantees and also assumes goal-reaching tolerances up to 2.5 cm and 15 ◦.Our system combines a greedy scheduler with cuArms to schedule and plan the full multi-arm reaching task and mo-tions. The greedy scheduler repeatedly uses Hungarian match-ing to assign a target to each robot every round using relaxed travel-distance costs until all targets are assigned. The joint tar-get configurations for each Cartesian weld target are sampled in batches using GPU-based inverse kinematic solvers [20] to achieve sub-millimeter-level precision. cuArms then plans the manipulation trajectory from the given schedule skeleton and refines the initial solutions for 2s and 6s per skeleton for 10- and 40- target problems respectively. As only the weld targets are different in each evaluation episode while the workspace remains the same, cuArms precomputes a base roadmap without any episode-specific joint objectives and reuses it. During planning, cuArms first connects each target to the base roadmap using RRT-guided steering. Once the system solves the initial skeleton, it backtracks and proposes alter-native schedule skeletons with sampled targets for cuArms to solve iteratively. A total of 16 schedule skeletons are evaluated.

Videos of successful execution are available in our supplementary material and https://cuarms.github.io/. VII. C ONCLUSION 

We present cuArms , a new conflict-based multi-arm manip-ulation planning algorithm. Using a GPU to batch-evaluate geometry, cuArms efficiently searches the joint state space with decomposed and reusable per-arm roadmaps and inter-robot conflict resolution. cuArms can be integrated into a general multi-arm TAMP algorithm with a schedule-then-coordinate approach to improve both low-level coordinated manipulation and high-level scheduling. With solutions found near-instantaneously without requiring task-specific training, users can use cuArms to rapidly evaluate the feasibility of multi-arm task and workspace configurations, and integrate it with domain-specific costs and collaborative skills. 1
