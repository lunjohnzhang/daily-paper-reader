# Shared-Roadmap Generation and Evaluator for Multi-Agent Path Planning Using Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network 

Brandon Ho, Nikola Rogers, Seung-Kyum Choi 

Abstract — Multi-agent path planning (MAPP) in continuous environments often relies on roadmaps to balance safety and search efficiency. However, traditional roadmap generation methods, such as lattice grids or standard sampling-based approaches, frequently face a trade-off between graph density and the likelihood of finding feasible, high-quality solutions. In this paper, we propose a scalable heterogeneous Graph Neural Network (GNN) framework for the automated generation and evaluation of shared multi-agent roadmaps. Our model covers the representation of waypoints, agent locations, and task loca-tions as distinct nodes in a heterogeneous graph, allowing it to reason over global connectivity and inter-agent interactions. By training on occupation density maps aggregated and collected from expert solver trajectories, the GNN learns to identify critical points of interest and prune redundant nodes and edges. This process produces a compact, coordination-aware roadmap that is invariant to task permutations and is reusable for multi-agent pick and delivery tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our framework can reduce planning effort and can potentially find better solutions, reaching at least 40% 

reduction in runtime and in graph size for dense roadmaps. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multi-agent path planning (MAPP) has been a central challenge in domains such as autonomous driving [1], [2], aerial swarms [3], and warehouse automation [4], [5], where overall system performance depends critically on both safety and efficiency. In real-world deployments, safety require-ments typically take precedence over efficiency, often con-straining agents to move along predefined edges and vertices within a structured environment, commonly referred to as a shared roadmap. A simple solution is a lattice grid, where autonomous agents or automated guided vehicles (AGVs) are restricted to moving between cardinal neighboring cells. However, unlike lattice grids, roadmaps that approximate continuous state spaces allow agents to move more flexibly and can yield more practical and higher-quality solutions. Such non-lattice roadmaps better capture the underlying geometry of real environments and reduce discretization artifacts. Nevertheless, roadmap generation itself is a non-trivial problem. Poorly constructed roadmaps can signifi-cantly degrade the performance of MAPP, underscoring the importance of principled methods for a shared multi-agent roadmap generation. The main challenge of roadmap generation lies in balanc-ing graph density with the likelihood of finding a feasible solution [6]. A dense roadmap may better capture high-quality or even the optimal solution, but it enlarges the

A common approach for constructing effective roadmaps is a two-step iterative process: (1) use sampling-based motion planning (SBMP) [7] to generate multiple roadmaps and then (2) evaluate these roadmaps using a MAPP algorithm. Although this paradigm has been widely adopted for single-agent navigation [8]–[11], extending it to multi-agent set-tings is non-trivial due to the inter-agent interactions. While prior work has explored roadmap generation for multi-agent scenarios [12]–[14], existing approaches impose certain con-straints, such as generating non-shared roadmaps for each agent, limiting connectivity (e.g., non-bidirectional edges). Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown promising re-sults in scalable recommendation systems where information can be represented in a relational graph [15]. By coding indi-vidual entities as nodes and their interactions as edges, GNNs are able to learn structured representations that generalize to environments with a variable number of elements. Given an existing roadmap, we encode waypoints, agent locations, and task locations as nodes in a heterogeneous graph, while en-coding spatial relationships and task-relevant information as edge features. On this structured representation, our hetero-geneous GNN model evaluates roadmap quality and reasons over global connectivity and agent interactions. Additionally, the GNN learns to identify critical points of interest and prune less informative nodes or edges, thereby producing a refined roadmap that is both compact and better suited for efficient multi-agent coordination. II. R ELATED WORKS 

A. Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) 

Multi-agent pathfinding (MAPF), a problem of multi-agent path planning (MAPP) in discrete space, has been applied in various AI and robotics applications where the objective is to find a set of collision-free paths on a graph. Popular approaches include, but are not limited to, conflict-based search (CBS) algorithms [16], increasing cost tree search [17], and prioritized planning (e.g., HCA* [18], SIPP [19], etc. More recently, research demonstrates the potential in using machine-learning techniques for solving MAPF. These methods learns from either demonstrations collected offline for imitation learning, a sequence of observations for reinforcement learning, or using graph neural networks, leading to finding solutions in multi-agent path planning (MAPP). Despite such progress, many algorithms simplify the scope of their problem instances, typically in a grid map, where the spatial graph structure has uniform travel cost, and collisions only occur when point-particle agents occupy the same vertices or edges [18]. These assumptions restrict the algorithms’ capabilities to only discrete time and space, making their real-world applications limited. 

B. Roadmap Generation 

Roadmap generation has been a research topic of interest mainly for single-agent cases, as it allows the agents to move in continuous space, leading to more practical and better solutions. A roadmap refers to an undirected connected graph that is either shared across agents or tailored for each agent. An ideal roadmap entails that any two points on the graph can be connected by at least one path, and the graph should be sparse as possible. Popular methods in generating a roadmap include, but not are not limited to, probabilistic roadmaps (PRMs) [20], constrained Delaunay Triangulation (CDT) [21], visibility graph [22], and rapidly exploring random graphs (RRGs). CDT and visibility graph construct a roadmap by utilizing the obstacle boundary as either constraints or points of interest, while PRM and RRG iteratively sample points in free space. Research on single-agent roadmaps studies how to densify the representation of the environment to balance optimality and density [8]–[11]. For multi-agent roadmaps, most continuous MAPP meth-ods [23]–[27] rely on some roadmap to operate. Their primary challenge lies in scaling to large numbers of agents in real time, as the search space grows significantly and collision checking must be performed at high frequency. In contrast, we investigate a complementary research direction: instead of improving the planner itself, we explore what types of roadmap representations are most effective for solving MAPP in continuous space. There have been relatively few studies on multi-agent roadmap generation and refinement. [14] constructs a shared roadmap with bias-based sampling, focusing on narrow pas-sages, but is less suited for sparsely distributed obstacles. [13] employs a generative model to construct separate but not shared roadmaps per agent that incorporate inter-agent interactions. The most relevant work is [12], where they opti-mized already constructed roadmaps through online learning. However, their optimization is limited to directed edges and does not remove redundant or unnecessary vertices, thereby restricting the extent to which the roadmap can be structurally refined. In contrast, our method identifies critical points of interest within an existing roadmap and reconstructs a shared, refined graph. A shared roadmap provides constraints where safety can be better tracked as the agents follow on set guided paths. By labeling agents’ initial and task locations as landmarks, the model ensures connectivity among essential nodes, improving coverage, reusability, and generalization across varying multi-agent tasks, including pickup and de-livery scenarios. A. Roadmap Generation 

We construct our homogeneous roadmap graph G using three generation strategies, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The first strategy is a lattice grid, in which vertices are uniformly distributed throughout the free space and edges connect neighboring vertices along Cartesian directions. The second strategy is a Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM), where vertices are randomly sampled in the free space and edges are created based on a maximum connection radius and a limit on the number of nearest neighbors. The third strategy is Delaunay triangulation, in which randomly sampled vertices are connected to form a planar graph composed of non-overlapping triangles. For all three constructions, each vertex 

vi ∈ G stores its global position a, and each edge ei j ∈ G is associated with a scalar weight wi j , corresponding to the Euclidean distance between its incident vertices. 

B. Model 

In this work, we formulate roadmap evaluation and gener-ation as a binary node classification problem e.g ( φ (vi|G ) →{0, 1}). We are given a collection of MAPP demonstrations, each consisting of a problem instance and its corresponding solution obtained through computationally intensive offline MAPF solvers operating on sufficiently dense roadmaps constructed via uniform random or grid sampling. Using these demonstrations as training data, we learn a predictive model that classifies each vertex according to whether it belongs to the solution set (class 1) or not (class 0). The trained model serves as a vertex evaluator that prunes a densely generated shared roadmap based on a decision threshold, producing a sparser yet solution-preserving graph that enables more efficient downstream planning. C. Training Setup 

Our dataset comprises 1,800 problem instances on 32 × 32 maps with an obstacle density of 0.1, featuring scenarios with 1, 2, and 4 agents, where we use 1,620 problems for training and 180 for validation. The number of generated problem permutations per problem instance was at most 64 to create our occupation density map H. The roadmap generation was done with PRM, sampling 1000 waypoints with a minimum edge length of 0.1, a maximum edge length of 5.1, and a maximum limit of 13 neighbors per node. The architectural pipeline begins with two initial linear layers with 64 channels to encode the input, which consists of 4-dimensional node features and 1-dimensional edge features. This is followed by three residual Graph Attention Network (GAT) layers, each configured with 4 heads and 16 channels, using Leaky ReLU activation functions. A final linear layer with 32 channels reduces the dimensionality to a single scalar output representing the vertex probability score. The model is trained using the AdamW [32] optimizer with a learning rate of 0 .0001 and a weight decay of 5 ×10 −4. We employ a batch size of 128 over 5,000 epochs, incorporating a Laplacian regularization term ( λ ) of 0 .01 to ensure spatial smoothness across the roadmap. We utilize an Intel Ultra 7 265K (20 cores) processor and a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU with 32GB of RAM, where the dataset generation took less than 5 minutes using 20-core multiprocessing, and the training process was completed in approximately 52 minutes.

E. Limitation 

Although the proposed GNN-based roadmap evaluator is capable of identifying key regions of interest and pruning redundant waypoints to produce a sparser roadmap, it has a few limitations. First, if the initial roadmap is not sufficiently dense, the evaluator may prune vertices that are essential for connectivity, thereby eliminating feasible paths between start–goal pairs. We presented a heterogeneous Graph Neural Network (GNN) framework for roadmap generation and evaluation, introducing a novel graph representation tailored for shared-roadmap construction in multi-agent path planning. The proposed GNN-based roadmap evaluator assigns importance scores to vertices in an initially dense roadmap and prunes those deemed unnecessary via a thresholding function. Through its training procedure—leveraging multiple permu-tations of the same problem instance—and by encoding start and goal configurations as landmarks, the GNN captures structural patterns that generalize across agent assignments. As a result, the reconstructed roadmap is task-agnostic, en-abling MAPF algorithm to reuse the same map under differ-ent task assignments, making it suitable for multi-agent pick-up and delivery (MAPD) tasks. We demonstrated that our roadmap evaluator reduced the search effort of MAPF/MAPP algorithms, reaching at least 40% reduction in runtime and graph size for dense roadmaps, and potential remove points of congestion or conflict. In future work, we plan to replace the manually tuned thresholding mechanism with a learned thresholding function and extend the framework to explicitly address MAPD problems. Such an extension would enable adaptive, instance-aware pruning, reducing the likelihood of disconnecting critical regions of the roadmap and thereby minimizing the risk of infeasible solutions.
