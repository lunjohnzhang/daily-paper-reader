# Distributed Relative Localization for Homogeneous Multi-Robot Systems through UWB Ranging and Limited Communications 

Zhiqiang Cao , Ran Liu , Senior Member, IEEE , Billy Pik Lik Lau , Senior Member, IEEE ,Chau Yuen , Fellow, IEEE , U-Xuan Tan , Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —Accurate and reliable relative localization is crucial for multi-robot applications like exploration, search, and rescue missions. LiDAR-based solutions offer high accuracy in localiz-ing surrounding objects; however, distinguishing homogeneous robots with similar appearances remains challenging due to the lack of distinctive identification features. In this paper, we propose a fully distributed relative pose estimation approach by integrating LiDAR, UWB, and odometry measurements, allowing each robot to accurately and continuously localize its teammates without external infrastructure. Specifically, potential anonymous teammate robot clusters from LiDAR scans are tracked by a dynamic tracker. We then identify teammate robots from these tracked anonymous clusters using a joint matching strategy, ensuring reliable data association between robots and clusters. Finally, by combining the corresponding LiDAR observations, UWB ranging, and odometry measurements, each robot precisely localizes others while minimizing data exchange. The system requires only odometry data exchange through onboard UWB, eliminating the need for additional communication infrastruc-ture like WiFi routers or mesh networks. Extensive simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed relative localization approach. 

Index Terms —Distributed relative localization, Multi-robot system, Teammate identification, Sensor fusion 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# MULTI-ROBOT systems have gained significant inter-est in the robotics research community over the past decades due to their superior applicability and efficiency com-pared against single robot systems in various applications, such as exploration, search, and rescue missions [1]-[3]. To execute these multi-robot cooperative tasks in unknown scenarios, each robot must be capable of reliably and accurately estimating the relative position of others with respect to itself [4]-[6]. This is essential for maintaining formation, preventing collisions, and coordinating actions seamlessly. To date, the majority of multi-robot research depends on external sensing devices, such as global positioning systems (GPS) [7], motion capture systems [8], and infrastructure-based Ultra-WideBand (UWB) [9], to achieve relative local-ization between robots. Both motion capture systems and infrastructure-based UWB positioning systems require the installation of external anchors, which restrict their applicability in unknown environments. However, these sensors encounter difficulties with anonymous detection results due to the similar structure of objects (i.e., data association prob-lem) and their dependence on line-of-sight (LOS) conditions [13][14]. In contrast, wireless sensors, like UWB, demon-strate greater resilience to non-line-of-sight (NLOS) situations. Leveraging the complementary strengths of odometry, Li-DAR, and UWB, we propose a fully distributed relative local-ization approach for a homogeneous multi-robot system that operates effectively under challenging conditions, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Particularly, UWB ranging and LiDAR clusters generated by the adaptive clustering algorithm [18] are first processed to eliminate outliers. Then, a dynamic tracker is designed to track these filtered anonymous LiDAR clusters within the robot’s own global frame. Subsequently, we identify teammate robots from these tracked LiDAR clusters based on a joint matching strategy. Once the teammate robot is identified and meets the cumulative displacement criteria, a separate teammate tracker is assigned for continuous relative pose estimation. All communication demands in the proposed system are fulfilled by the tailored UWB communication net-work, without external communication devices. > •

We propose a robust teammate identification approach based on a joint matching strategy, ensuring reliable data association between teammate robots and LiDAR clus-ters. > •

We present a fully distributed relative localization system that fuses LiDAR, odometry, and UWB ranging mea-surements without requiring external infrastructure. The system is built upon the UWB communication network used to transmit odometry data among robots, with each message containing only 8 Bytes per robot, while ensur-ing reliable and efficient localization.

Relative localization for a multi-robot system operating in GPS-denied and communication-constrained environments without any external infrastructure has emerged as a significant research focus in the robotics community. This section covers the existing studies on relative pose estimation in multi-robot systems based on LiDAR, cameras, and wireless sensors. In recent years, LiDAR-based and visual-based solutions have gained popularity in the robotics community. Lajoie et al. 

[19] presented a two-stage matching approach for constructing inter-robot loop closure (i.e., relative pose between robots) by exchanging the features extracted from the image and LiDAR point cloud among robots. However, a substantial communication load is needed for exchanging features, and feature matching requires high computational resources. To relieve communication and computational load, Weinstein et al. [20] and Lusk et al. [21] acquire the relative pose between robots by sharing the robot’s ego-state generated by Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) [22]. These methods assume a known initial pose correspondence among robots and are prone to drift over time, which is an inherent limitation in VIO. In addition, LiDAR-based or visual-based object detection methods have emerged as promising alternatives for accurate relative position estimation. However, associating the detection results with specific teammate robots remains a challenge for a group of homogeneous robots due to their similar structures or appearances. Zhu et al. [13] addressed data association by implementing trajectory matching between the tracked anonymous LiDAR clusters and odometry trajectories received from other robots, in which each robot was attached with reflective taps for LiDAR detection. Xun et al. [23] used active infrared LEDs to encode drone ID, allowing for straightforward identification of each teammate drone. These methods require customized platforms, which may restrict their applications in practical deployment. Cao et al. [24] addressed the data association problem between teammate robots and LiDAR clusters through a subgraph matching approach. Although this approach requires little data exchange and performs effectively in normal scenarios, it can fail when robots form completely symmetrical structures and is computationally inefficient. P ROPOSED RELATIVE LOCALIZATION FRAMEWORK 

In this section, we introduce key notations and problem for-mulation studied in this paper. Following this, we describe the proposed relative localization framework, involving five key components: (i) Data preprocessing in Section III-A, which rejects the abnormal UWB ranging measurements and LiDAR clusters, (ii) Dynamic tracker in Section III-B, which tracks the filtered anonymous LiDAR clusters within the robot’s own global frame, (iii) Extrinsic transformation estimation

To enhance measurement reliability, we employ a low-pass filter [29] to smooth raw UWB ranging data and eliminate outliers. Initially, the mean and variance of the raw UWB ranging data within a short temporal window are computed. If the variance is below a preset threshold, the UWB ranging data is considered as valid and the mean is utilized to initialize the low-pass filter; otherwise, the data is discarded. D. Teammate Tracker 

In this subsection, we introduce the teammate tracker to track the teammate robot within the robot’s own global frame, integrating UWB ranging, LiDAR, and odometry measure-ments. Each teammate robot is assigned a separate teammate tracker, which becomes active when the teammate robot is identified and its cumulative displacement over a period of time exceeds a preset threshold of 2.5m, chosen empiri-cally. The cumulative displacement condition ensures that the rotation matrix of the extrinsic transformation estimated in Section III-C1 is proper. In this paper, we adopt a pose graph optimization-based method for the teammate tracker, as illustrated in Fig. 4, wherein the vertex indicates the robot’s pose and the edge denotes the constraints between vertices. Given the highly nonlinear nature of the underly-ing optimization problem, directly integrating the measure-ments without an appropriate initial guess is nearly infeasible. E. UWB Communication Network 

Each robot only broadcasts its odometry measurement to others solely based on a UWB communication network, with-out the need for any external communication devices, such as WiFi router. The UWB sensor utilized in our system supports Byte-level data transmission when performing ranging, with a maximum payload of approximately 100 Bytes. However, this limited bandwidth is insufficient for transmitting high-dimensional information, such as point clouds or feature descriptors. To minimize communication bandwidth usage while ensuring reliability and accuracy, we represent each odometry measurement using only 8 Bytes. A. Experimental Setup 

To validate the performance of the proposed approach, we have conducted experiments in an indoor environment with three homogeneous TurtleBot2 robots. As shown in Fig. 6, each robot was equipped with one UWB node (Nooploop LinkTrack, maximum range of 100m, ranging frequency of 50Hz, maximum data transmission of 100 Bytes at frequency of 20Hz) and LiDAR (Velodyne VLP-16, maximum range of 100m with horizontal field of view of 360 ◦ at frequency of 10Hz). Notably, since this study focuses on 2D scenarios, only a single LiDAR scan channel is used in the experiment, which aligns with the 2D nature of the state estimation and the wheel odometry employed in our system. Each robot was controlled to randomly move at a maximum speed of 0.2m/s and provide odometry measurements with a sampling frequency of 50Hz. The proposed approach is evaluated on a computing unit with an E5-2620 v4 CPU, running Robot Operating System (ROS) Noetic. During the experiments, the initial correspondence among robots is undisclosed, and odometry measurement is broadcast via the UWB communication network depicted in Section III-E. Adaptive Monte Carlo Localization 1 was applied to each robot to obtain the ground truth based on a prior occupancy grid map. For visualization and comparison purposes, all trajectories were manually transformed into a common reference frame in the offline processing stage, as illustrated in Fig. 7. Each robot’s odometry measurements experience considerable drift.

D. Comparison with Existing Approach 

We evaluate the proposed approach against two representa-tive methods. The first baseline [27] estimates relative poses by combining short-term UWB ranging and odometry, with-out requiring initial pose correspondence among robots. The second is SLAM Toolbox [33], a LiDAR–odometry SLAM framework, where relative poses can be derived by exchanging self-localization results, under the assumption of known initial pose correspondence. All baselines incur communication costs similar to our approach. Fig. 10 demonstrates a compari-son of the relative translational error and relative rotational error across the different approaches, in which the relative localization is conducted from each robot’s perspective. As can be observed from this figure, our approach achieves the average translational error of 0.126m and rotational error of 2.275 ◦. Compared with all methods, our approach attains the highest translational accuracy and exhibits a low stan-dard deviation, indicating strong robustness and reliability. In terms of rotational accuracy, the proposed method significantly outperforms pure odometry and the approach in [27]. In contrast, SLAM Toolbox [33] benefits from LiDAR’s strong angular observability, which enables more reliable orientation corrections and thus yields higher rotational accuracy than our approach. This outcome is expected, as the proposed approach lacks explicit orientation constraints. 

E. Evaluation of Communication Efficiency 

In the proposed system, broadcasting odometry measure-ment only relies on the UWB communication network, without external communication devices. In this experiment with three robots, the total communication bandwidth required is only 0.48KB/s at a communication frequency of 20Hz, i.e., message size (8 Bytes) × broadcast frequency (20Hz) × number of robots (3), resulting in a total data exchange of 115.2KB for 240s duration of our experiment. Table IV reports the packet loss rate for each robot in our experiment, using the UWB communication network presented in Section III-E. The results show that the average packet loss rate is around 0.476% across three robots during this experiment. The proposed approach enables relative localization for a group of robots with instanta-neous UWB ranging and communication, which is well-suited for the coordination and formation of miniaturized vehicles with limited onboard resources. B. Scalability Analysis 

To discuss the scalability of the proposed approach, we conducted simulations with different team sizes varying from 3 to 10 identically configured TurtleBot3 robots. All trials were performed in the same Gazebo environment to ensure fair comparison, as shown in Fig. 13. Table V lists the relative localization accuracy for four experimental trials, with team sizes of 3, 5, 7, and 10 robots, respectively. Due to space limitations, only results from Robot 1’s perspective are pre-sented. The results indicate that the proposed approach scales effectively to a 10-robot team while maintaining consistent relative localization accuracy with that observed in smaller teams. However, scaling the team size from 3 to 10 robots increases communication bandwidth usage from 0.48KB/s to 1.6KB/s and prolongs the average time consumption of team-mate identification from 6.3ms to 66.7ms. The communication bandwidth usage increases primarily due to the larger number of robots participating in the data exchange, as calculated from the formulation in Section IV-E. Similarly, the rise in teammate identification time results from the greater number of candidate associations that must be processed during the identification procedure with a larger team size. Nevertheless, these increases remain acceptable. Moreover, the performance of other modules is minimally impacted, benefiting from the system’s distributed design. VI. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS 

In this paper, we present a distributed relative localization method for a group of homogeneous robots using only on-board UWB communication network, enabling flexible and robust multi-robot cooperation in communication-limited sce-narios, such as post-disaster search and rescue or unknown-environment exploration, even without prior knowledge of initial correspondence among robots. Particularly, we identify teammate robots from the tracked anonymous LiDAR clusters by a joint matching strategy. Subsequently, a teammate tracker is employed to continuously localize the teammate robot in a distributed manner.
