Abstract. We study the optimal gathering problem over a finite set of designated meeting nodes for asynchronous, anonymous, and oblivious 

mobile robots on an infinite grid under crash faults. The robots have global visibility and strong multiplicity detection, but share neither a coordinate system nor chirality. The objective is to gather all non-faulty robots at a Weber Meeting Node , minimizing the total Manhattan distance from their initial positions. Up to n − 2 robots may crash per-manently, and such crashes are indistinguishable from arbitrary delays. Existing approaches often rely on a designated robot to break symme-try, whose crash may block the remaining robots indefinitely. Instead, our approach enables every robot to independently select the same tar-get from its snapshot, while target-dependent restricted shortest paths preserve the target as a Weber Meeting Node . We prove that, un-der strong multiplicity detection, optimal gathering is impossible from certain fully symmetric configurations. For all remaining configurations, our algorithm CrashTolerantWeberGathering() selects a unique common target, preserves its optimality throughout the execution, and allows non-faulty robots to progress without waiting for crashed robots, thereby guaranteeing gathering in finite time. 

Keywords: Swarm Robotics · Optimal gathering · Weber Meeting Nodes · Crash faults · Infinite grid · Asynchronous scheduler. 

## 1 Introduction 

Background and motivation. Swarm robotics is inspired by the collective be-haviour of social insects such as ants, bees, and termites. A robot swarm consists of several simple and inexpensive robots that work together, without centralized

control or explicit communication, to perform tasks such as exploration, pattern formation, convergence, and gathering. The main question is how robots with limited computational capabilities can coordinate to achieve a common goal. In the classical distributed model, robots are autonomous (operate without centralized coordination), homogeneous (identical in capabilities and behaviour), 

anonymous (have no unique identifiers), oblivious (have no memory of past ac-tions), and silent (do not communicate explicitly). Each robot executes the same deterministic algorithm and repeatedly performs a Look–Compute–Move (LCM )cycle: it observes the current configuration, computes a destination, and either remains stationary or moves toward that destination. The robots are disoriented and share neither a coordinate system nor chirality. We consider robots deployed on an infinite grid, where initially each robot occupies a distinct grid node and moves along grid edges. Movements are instantaneous, so robots are observed only at grid nodes. They have global visibility and strong global multiplicity detec-tion ; hence, they observe the complete configuration and know the exact number of robots at each occupied node. Every robot makes its decision only from the snapshot expressed in its local coordinate system. The grid contains a finite set of distinguished nodes, called meeting nodes , which may represent charging sta-tions, communication points, maintenance facilities, or evacuation sites. In the 

gathering over meeting nodes problem, the robots must gather at one of these designated nodes rather than at an arbitrary grid node. For optimal gathering ,the destination must be a Weber Meeting Node , that is, a meeting node minimizing the total Manhattan distance from the initial robot positions. The robots operate under a fair asynchronous (ASYNC ) scheduler, which activates every non-faulty robot infinitely often. They have no common notion of time, and their Look , Compute , and Move phases may take unpredictable amounts of time. Up to n − 2 robots may crash permanently. A crashed robot stops ex-ecuting its LCM cycles but remains visible at its last position. Since a crash is indistinguishable from an arbitrary delay, the non-faulty robots cannot rely on the future movement of any particular robot. We characterize the initial con-figurations from which crash-tolerant optimal gathering is possible and give a deterministic algorithm that gathers all non-faulty robots at the same Weber Meeting Node without waiting for any other robot. 

Related work. Gathering has been studied in the continuous plane under differ-ent assumptions on visibility, multiplicity detection, orientation, and activation schedules [26,19], and in discrete environments such as rings, trees, grids, and hypercubes [24,13,22,12,6]. For rings, impossibility and algorithmic results un-der different forms of multiplicity detection were given in [24,14,23]. Gatherable configurations on finite grids and trees were characterized in [12]. Problems with prescribed destinations include landmark covering [20], em-bedded pattern formation [11], and gathering over meeting points [10]. Bhagat et al. [2] studied gathering over meeting nodes on an infinite grid in the fault-free setting. Fault-tolerant gathering has been studied in the plane [1,16,8,21,4] and on graphs under different faults [7,9,18,15]. Pelc [25] considered labeled agents with memory and communication, while Bose et al. [5] considered anonymous Crash-Tolerant Gathering at Weber Meeting Nodes 3

robots on finite grids with at most one crash. In contrast, we study crash-tolerant 

optimal gathering over a finite set of designated meeting nodes on an infinite grid, where the gathering destination must be a Weber Meeting Node . Our model allows up to n − 2 permanent crash faults, which are indistinguishable from ar-bitrary activation delays, and therefore does not permit the robots to rely on the future movement of any particular robot. 

Our contributions. Our main contributions to crash-tolerant optimal gather-ing at Weber Meeting Nodes are as follows: 

– We prove that weak multiplicity detection is insufficient for optimal Weber-meeting-node gathering in the presence of up to n − 2 crash faults, even under a fully synchronous ( FSYNC ) scheduler, where all non-faulty robots are activated simultaneously in each round and execute their Look–Compute– Move cycles synchronously. Thus, strong multiplicity detection is necessary. 

– We characterize reflectionally and rotationally symmetric configurations for which optimal gathering becomes impossible when robots occupying symmetry-breaking positions crash. 

– For every remaining configuration, we give a deterministic algorithm that selects a unique Weber Meeting Node without relying on the movement of any distinguished robot. 

– We introduce target-dependent restricted shortest paths and prove that they preserve the selected region and the target Weber Meeting Node through-out the execution. This guarantees that all non-faulty robots gather at the selected target in finite time despite up to n − 2 crash faults.

## 2 Impossibility Results 

The impossibility results are based on a common geometric obstruction: robots occupying symmetric positions cannot deterministically break the symmetry when no admissible gathering node is fixed by that symmetry [3]. 

Impossibility inherited from the crash-free setting. The partitive-symmetry results of [3] imply that gathering is impossible from any symmetric initial con-figuration in which the fixed part of the symmetry contains no admissible target. In particular, this includes reflectionally symmetric configurations whose sym-metry line contains no meeting node, and rotationally symmetric configurations whose center is not a meeting node. For optimal gathering, the same conclusion holds when the fixed part contains no Weber Meeting Node . These results remain valid in our model because every crash-free execution is an admissible execution when up to n − 2 robots may crash. 

Theorem 1 (Crash-induced symmetry impossibility). No deterministic algorithm can guarantee gathering at a meeting node in any of the following cases: (i) I(t0) is reflectionally symmetric about a line L, M ∩ L = ∅, and all robots initially on L crash before moving; (ii) I(t0) is rotationally symmetric about a grid node c / ∈ M , and the robot initially at c crashes before moving; or (iii) I(t0) is rotationally symmetric about c / ∈ M , and all robots initially on two invariant perpendicular grid lines through c crash before moving. In each case, at least one complete nontrivial symmetry orbit outside the crashed set remains non-faulty. Consequently, gathering at a Weber Meeting Node is also impossible. 

Theorem 2 (Impossibility under weak multiplicity detection). No de-terministic distributed algorithm equipped only with global weak multiplicity de-tection can solve optimal Weber-meeting-node gathering in the presence of up to 

n − 2 crash faults, even under FSYNC activation and rigid movement. 

In the FSYNC model, all non-crashed agents are activated synchronously in each round. Thus, in every round, each non-crashed agent performs a complete Look–Compute–Move cycle, and all agents observe the same global configuration 6 A. Maiti et al. 

before making their moves. The complete proofs of these two theorems are given in the APPENDIX. Therefore, global strong multiplicity detection is necessary, even under FSYNC activation and rigid movement. Let I be the set of all initial configurations and let U ⊆ I contain the configurations excluded by the preceding impossibility results. The proposed algorithm is therefore defined for every initial configuration I(t0) ∈ I \ U.

## 3 Algorithm CrashTolerantWeberGathering() 

High-Level Idea of the Algorithm. The algorithm consists of two phases: 

Target Selection and Crash-Tolerant Directed Gathering . Upon activation, a robot observes the current configuration and identifies the applicable config-uration class from the symmetry of M and the robot positions. In the first phase, the robot either selects a common target Weber Meeting Node m†

directly or first determines a target region. Direct selection applies when M is asymmetric, when c ∈ M is the unique Weber Meeting Node , or when a suitable candidate Weber Meeting Node lies on the symmetry line L. In the remaining symmetric cases, the prescribed occupancy and tie-breaking rules de-termine either a target half-plane B† or a target quadrant Q†. In the rotational case, the selected region is Q† ∪ L ++ 1 ∪ L ++ 2 , where L++ 1 and L++ 2 bound Q†. A unique node m† associated with the selected region is then chosen. In the second phase, every non-faulty robot executes either Direct Gathering or Trans-fer Plane . Under Direct Gathering , the robot invokes MoveToDestina-tion() to move toward m†. This applies when m† is selected directly and also when m† ∈ L ++ 1 ∪ L ++ 2 . Under Transfer Plane , a robot first moves into B†

in the unique-reflection case or into Q† in the rotational case, and then invokes 

MoveToDestination() to proceed toward m†. The overall structure of the algorithm is illustrated in Figure 1.

For a snapshot I(t) = ( R(t), M), let m†(t) denote the target Weber Meeting Node computed by an activated robot and, when applicable, let B†(t) or Q†(t)

denote the computed target region. Let F be the unknown, possibly empty, set of crash-faulty robots. Due to page constraints, the proofs of the following results are provided in the APPENDIX. 

Lemma 1 (Uniqueness of the target region). For every configuration in 

I321 , I322 , or I323 , Phase I-A uniquely selects one of B1 and B2. For every configuration in I4211 , I42121 , I(1) 42122 , I(2) 42122 , I(3) 42122 , I4221 , or I42221 , it uniquely selects one of Q1, . . . , Q4.

Lemma 2 (Uniqueness of the target node). For every solvable configura-tion, Phase I determines exactly one target Weber Meeting Node m† ∈ W (t)

from the current snapshot. 

Lemma 3 (Invariance of the selected half-plane). Let B† be the target half-plane selected from a configuration in I321 , I322 , or I323 . If every subse-quent move follows MoveToDestination() , every configuration reached during Phase II selects the same half-plane B†.

Lemma 4 (Invariance of the selected quadrant). Let Q† be the target quadrant selected at the beginning of Phase II. Every subsequent configuration reached during Phase II selects the same quadrant Q†.

Lemma 5 (Invariance of the selected target). Let m† be selected from I(t).If every move completed after time t follows MoveToDestination() toward 

m†, then every subsequent snapshot selects the same target m†.

Lemma 6 (Independent progress under crash faults). Let r /∈ F be a non-faulty robot activated at time t, with r(t)̸ = m†, and let t′ > t be the time at which it completes the move prescribed by MoveToDestination() . Then 

d(r(t′), m †) ≤ d(r(t), m †) − 1. The completion of this move does not require the activation or movement of any other robot. 

Lemma 7 (Finite-time gathering). Under every fair asynchronous schedule, all non-faulty robots reach m† after finitely many completed moves and remain there. 

Theorem 3 (Correctness). Let I(t0) /∈ U, and suppose that at most n − 2

robots crash. Under every fair asynchronous schedule, CrashTolerantWe-berGathering() gathers all non-faulty robots in finite time at a common node 

m† ∈ W (t0). Moreover, no non-faulty robot must wait for another robot to move or become activated. Crash-Tolerant Gathering at Weber Meeting Nodes 17 

Proof. Because I(t0) /∈ U, the applicable rules of Phase I are defined. Lemmas 1 and 2 provide a unique m† ∈ W (t0) and, when necessary, a unique target re-gion. Lemma 5 shows that every subsequent snapshot yields the same choices. Lemma 8 (see APPENDIX) additionally ensures that m† remains a Weber Meeting Node throughout Phase II. A crash only prevents the faulty robot from moving further. By Lemma 6, it neither blocks another robot nor forces a non-faulty robot to wait. Finally, Lemma 7 implies that fairness brings every non-faulty robot to m† in finite time and that all such robots remain there. 

## 5 Conclusion and Future Work 

We studied crash-tolerant optimal gathering at Weber Meeting Nodes for asynchronous, anonymous, oblivious, and disoriented robots on an infinite grid, assuming global visibility, global strong multiplicity detection, and up to n − 2

crashes. We established both inherited and crash-specific impossibility results and showed that global weak multiplicity detection cannot preserve min-sum optimality, even under FSYNC activation and rigid movements. For every re-maining configuration, CrashTolerantWeberGathering() selects a unique target m† ∈ W (t0) and, when required, a unique target region.Restricted short-est paths preserve these choices and ensure that all non-faulty robots gather at the common target in finite time under any fair ASYNC scheduler. Future work includes characterizing solvable configurations under weaker multiplicity detection, possibly using persistent memory or visible lights; re-moving the grid-node restriction on symmetry lines and rotation centers; and considering limited or obstructed visibility, Byzantine faults, and alternative op-timization criteria such as maximum travel distance or completion time.
