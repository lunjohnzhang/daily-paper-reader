## Abstract 

We study a problem of searching for a mobile target in an 𝑚 -ray star graph, a natural generalization of linear search to multiple directions. The target is placed adversarially on one of the rays and may move with constant speed. We investigate a two-robot setting, where cooperation and communication play a central role. We study two communication models: the Face-to-Face (F2F) model, where robots communicate only upon meeting, and the Sender-Receiver (S/R) model, where communication is asymmetric. We focus on the 

away model , in which the target moves away from the origin with speed 𝑣 < 1. We design search strategies that minimize the competitive ra-tio and analyze the problem under three knowledge assumptions: 

NoDistance , NoSpeed , and NoKnowledge . For each setting, we de-rive upper bounds on the competitive ratio and for some cases, we derive the lower bound. Our results highlight how the number of rays and the communication model influence the competitive ratio. 

Key words and phrases. Capturing, Knowledge, Mobile Agents, Moving Target, Search, Sender/Receiver, Star Graph. 

## 1 Introduction 

Search and evacuation problems are fundamental topics in algo-rithm design, with applications in areas such as robotics, surveil-lance, data mining, and network exploration. In the classical linear search problem, one or more agents must locate an unknown target on an infinite line, while in evacuation problems multiple agents cooperate so that they all find an unknown exit. These problems have been extensively studied under various assumptions on the number of agents, their speeds, and the amount of information available. A central measure of performance is the competitive ratio , which compares the time required by an online search strategy to find the target without knowledge of its location to the time required by an optimal offline strategy that knows the target’s location in advance. An algorithm is optimal if it minimizes this ratio. While most existing work focuses on static targets and simple domains such as the line, less attention has been given to more complex environments and dynamic targets. In this paper, we study the problem of searching for a mobile target in an 𝑚 -ray star graph, a natural generalization of the linear domain to multiple directions. The presence of multiple rays introduces a fundamental trade-off between exploring new directions and revisiting previously ex-plored regions, while the motion of the target further increases the difficulty.

We investigate two-robot settings under two communication models: the Face-to-Face (F2F) model, where robots communicate only upon meeting, and the Sender-Receiver (S/R) model, which al-lows asymmetric wireless communication. We analyze the problem under different knowledge assumptions about the target, namely whether its initial distance, speed, or neither is known. The three knowledge assumptions are chosen to capture different levels of information available to the searching robots and to investigate how such information affects the difficulty of the capture problem. In particular, they provide a natural progression from settings in which the robots possess useful information about the target to the completely uninformed setting. Studying these variants allows us to quantify the benefit of prior knowledge through the resulting com-petitive ratios and to identify how the availability of information interacts with the topology of the underlying graph. These assump-tions are also motivated by the corresponding settings considered for the infinite line in previous work [10, 11], allowing us to exam-ine how the transition from a line to an 𝑚 -ray star graph affects the role of information in online target capture. Our goal is to design search strategies that minimize the competitive ratio of capture time (also referred to as evacuation time) and to understand how the interplay between domain structure, target dynamics, commu-nication constraints, and available knowledge affects performance. We provide upper bounds on the competitive ratio for each setting and establish lower bounds for other cases. 

## 1.1 Problem Formulation and Objectives 

The primary objective of this work is to design online search strate-gies for two mobile agents that minimize the competitive ratio when searching for an oblivious moving target on an 𝑚 -ray star graph. Specifically, we aim to quantify the precise trade-offs between the agents’ initial knowledge, communication constraints, and the par-ity of the graph’s rays. To evaluate how partial information influences performance, we consider three knowledge settings: 

• NoDistance (Known 𝑣 , Unknown 𝑑 ): The robots know the target’s constant speed 𝑣 < 1, but have no prior infor-mation regarding the target’s initial distance 𝑑 from the origin. 

• NoSpeed (Known 𝑑 , Unknown 𝑣 ): The robots know the target’s initial distance 𝑑 from the origin, but do not know its speed 𝑣 .

• NoKnowledge (Unknown 𝑑 and 𝑣 ): The robots possess no prior knowledge about either the target’s initial distance

## 1.2 Context and Motivation 

The main motivation of this study is to understand how the lack of knowledge of the target’s speed and/or initial distance—affect the competitive ratio of search algorithms. While search problems have been extensively studied in the literature, most existing work focuses on static targets or simplified settings. The problem of capturing a moving target has received less at-tention, especially in non-linear domains and under asymmetric communication models. Previous studies have mainly considered moving target capture on the infinite line, often under simpler communication assumptions such as symmetric or F2F interaction. Although the 𝑚 -ray star graph has been studied as a search domain, the case of a moving target in this setting remains relatively unex-plored. In this work, we aim to address this gap. We also investigate how different communication models affect performance, showing that the S/R model can improve over the F2F model in certain set-tings. In addition, we highlight how the parity of the number of rays influences the competitive ratio, illustrating how symmetry breaking in the search domain can lead to improved performance under asymmetric communication. 

Motivation for the Sender-Receiver (S/R) Model. The Sender-Receiver model models asymmetric communication scenarios commonly en-countered in heterogeneous robotic systems. For instance, energy or payload constraints may allow only one agent (the Sender 𝑆 )to be equipped with a long-range wireless transmitter or optical beacon, while the other agent (the Receiver 𝑅 ) carries only a passive receiver or sensor. Studying this model allows us to establish the minimal communication infrastructure required to improve perfor-mance over pure face-to-face (F2F) interaction, highlighting how asymmetric signalling interacts with the graph topology. 

## 1.3 Model, Preliminaries, and Notation 

We consider a star graph consisting of 𝑚 infinite rays (branches), where 𝑚 ≥ 2. The robots are initially located at the origin and can move with unit speed. An oblivious mobile target is placed adversarially on one of the rays at an initial distance 𝑑 from the origin and moves along that ray with constant speed 𝑣 , either toward or away from the origin. If the target moves away from the origin, we assume 𝑣 < 1, while if it moves toward the origin, we allow 

𝑣 ≥ 0. When 𝑣 = 0, the target is stationary. Both robots are equipped with pedometers and computational capabilities, allowing them to infer each other’s positions based on exchanged information. The robots and the target start moving simultaneously. Throughout the paper, we use the notation 𝑢 = 11−𝑣 and 𝑀 = max {𝑑, 𝑢 }.

In the two-robot setting, we consider the following communi-cation models. Face-to-Face (F2F): Robots can communicate only when they are co-located. Sender-Receiver (S/R): One robot, called the sender ( 𝑆 ), can transmit information wirelessly but receives information only via face-to-face communication, while the other robot, called the receiver ( 𝑅 ), can receive information wirelessly but transmits only via face-to-face communication. The analysis takes into account the following knowledge models: NoDistance: the speed 𝑣 is known, but the initial distance 𝑑 is unknown; NoSpeed: the initial distance 𝑑 is known, but the speed 𝑣 is unknown; No-Knowledge: neither 𝑑 nor 𝑣 is known.

## 1.4 Related Work 

Search and evacuation problems have been studied in the literature under a wide range of models and assumptions. Work by Bell-man [5] introduced stochastic search models, laying the foundation for subsequent research. Numerous variants have been explored, in-cluding settings with static or mobile targets, multiple agents with heterogeneous speeds, and communication capabilities [4, 7, 8]. In the linear domain, evacuation and search problems with mul-tiple robots have received significant attention. For example, evacu-ation on a line with two robots of distinct speeds was studied in [3], where coordinated zigzag strategies achieve a competitive ratio of 9 for a wide range of speeds. These results highlight the importance of coordination and synchronization between agents in minimizing search time. Beyond the line, search and evacuation have also been investi-gated in more complex environments. Multi-agent search on tree graphs was considered in [13] under both wireless and face-to-face communication models, achieving improved competitive ratios through bidirectional exploration strategies. The 𝑚 -ray star graph is a natural extension of the linear domain and has been studied in several contexts. By 𝑚 -ray star we mean a star graph consisting of 𝑚 rays. For 𝑚 = 1, this is an infinite half-line with the robot starting at the unique endpoint and the search problem is trivial. For 𝑚 = 2, it is an infinite line with the robot starting at the origin in which case the search problem has been analyzed in the papers such as [10, 11]. In conclusion, the interesting unknown case is 

𝑚 ≥ 3. Multi-target exploration on 𝑚 -ray graphs was investigated in [2], where performance is evaluated under different levels of prior knowledge. Wireless evacuation on 𝑚 -ray graphs with mul-tiple robots was studied in [9], establishing both upper and lower bounds through coordinated exploration strategies. Search for moving targets has been studied in both stochastic and deterministic settings. McCabe [14] considered stochastic motion, while Alpern and Gal [1] developed a deterministic framework for searching oblivious targets moving at constant speed. Distributed pursuit and search problems on graph topologies have also been studied in the context of network security and intruder contain-ment; see, e.g., Blin et al. [6] for distributed chasing of network intruders. Our work is closely related to these lines of research, but differs in several key aspects. We study the problem of capturing a mobile target in an 𝑚 -ray star graph, considering two-robot settings. In the latter, we analyze two communication models—Face-to-Face (F2F) and Sender-Receiver (S/R)—and investigate how communica-tion constraints, target dynamics, and partial knowledge affect the design and performance of search strategies. ## 2 Searching for a Moving Target Using the F2F model 2.1 Overview of the Search Strategy 

All proposed search algorithms share a common geometric struc-ture: the two robots operate in synchronized rounds. In round 𝑘 , the robots explore two consecutive, previously unexamined rays up to a specific search depth 𝑥 𝑘 (or 𝑎 𝑘 ), before returning to the origin to exchange information or synchronize. If neither robot encounters the target, they advance to the next pair of rays and scale up their search radius according to an exponentially increasing parameter sequence.

## 3 Searching for a Moving Target Using the S/R model 

In this section, we propose an algorithm for the S/R communica-tion model that achieves a better upper bound than the F2F model when the number of rays is odd. This follows from the fact that the symmetry of the search configuration cannot be broken in the even-ray case. For an odd number of rays, the search process re-quires (𝑚 + 1)/ 2 iterations before the target can be captured. This additional iteration gives the sender another opportunity to en-counter the target and communicate its location to the receiver before the exploration pattern is completed, resulting in an im-proved competitive ratio. In contrast, when the number of rays is even, every ray has a corresponding opposite ray, and the initial configuration remains symmetric. Since deterministic algorithms cannot break this symmetry, the sender cannot obtain an advan-tage by communicating the target location earlier than in the F2F model. Therefore, the S/R communication model does not provide an improvement over F2F when the number of rays is even. We formalize this observation in the following lemma. Lemma 3.1 (Symmetry Invariance for Even 𝑚 ). For any even integer 𝑚 , any deterministic algorithm A𝑆𝑅 under the Sender-Receiver Khaled Jawhar and Evangelos Kranakis 

model can be simulated by a deterministic algorithm A𝐹 2𝐹 under the Face-to-Face model with identical competitive ratio. 

Proof. When 𝑚 is even, the star graph exhibits central sym-metry around the origin (every ray has a diametrically opposed counterpart). Since both robots start at the origin and execution is deterministic, an adversary placing the target symmetrically en-sures that the Sender cannot transmit information to the Receiver any earlier than when their paths intersect at the origin. Thus, wireless transmissions provide no additional information prior to co-location, reducing the execution to the F2F model.

## 5 Conclusion and future work 

We studied the problem of capturing an oblivious moving target on an 𝑚 -ray star graph using two robots under both F2F and S/R com-munication models. The analysis considers different levels of knowl-edge about the target. We designed new algorithms and proved upper and lower bounds. Future work includes improving the lower bounds and extending the work to multiple searchers capturing an oblivious moving target in star graphs.
