Abstract 

This paper considers the problem of coordinating a heterogeneous team of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs), where the ground vehicles must traverse a domain containing an unknown number of potentially hazardous targets. The UAVs provide aerial sensing using onboard optical cameras, and their trajectories must adapt to both the ground routes and the information collected about the targets. We for-mulate the aerial search problem using ergodic control, which distributes sensing effort ac-cording to a spatial information density to balance exploration and target localization. The formulation incorporates dynamic camera field-of-view sensor footprints and probabilistic detection models that depend on vehicle position and attitude. Angles-only measurements are processed through Bayesian inference and marginalization over data-association hy-potheses to obtain a Poisson multi-Bernoulli (PMB) approximation of the target posterior, representing both detected and undetected targets. This belief directs aerial sensing toward unexplored regions along the ground routes and viewpoints that improve target localiza-tion. Ground planning combines chance-constrained target avoidance, enforced through confidence sets, with a void-probability objective that favors routes unlikely to contain threats. We solve each team’s optimization problem in a fully decentralized manner using a nested SCvx*–C-ADMM scheme, with outer sequential convex programming iterations and inner distributed consensus optimization, accounting for nonlinearities, nonseparable objectives, and continuous-time inter-agent separation constraints. A closed-loop scheme updates both fleets’ trajectories as information becomes available and permits ground mo-tion when the remaining route risk falls below a prescribed threshold. Numerical case studies examine the effects of sensing geometry and target uncertainty, compare decen-tralized and centralized solvers, and demonstrate full closed-loop collaboration between multiple UAVs and UGVs. 

Keywords: Ergodic control, trajectory optimization, sequential convex programming, distributed optimization, optical sensor modeling, risk-sensitive control, random finite set theory, multi-target tracking 

1 Introduction 

Collaborative autonomy deals with the problem of coordinating multiple autonomous vehi-cles to accomplish a common objective. In a homogeneous team, the vehicles typically share similar motion and sensing capabilities, allowing coordination to be formulated around a common vehicle and sensor model. Heterogeneous teams introduce an additional level of arXiv:2610.39379v1 [cs.RO] 29 Oct 2026 Joshua Pilipovsky 

coupling, since the capabilities of one vehicle class may be needed for another to complete its task. For example, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) can observe regions that are inaccessi-ble to unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs), while the ground vehicles can carry the payloads needed to execute a mission. This complementary use of aerial and ground platforms has been studied in coordinated exploration and mapping (Dewan et al., 2013), collaborative localization (Butzke et al., 2016), and persistent sensing with mobile recharging (Naveed et al., 2025). In this work, we consider a UAV/UGV team in which the ground vehicles must traverse a bounded domain containing an unknown number of potentially hazardous targets. The UAVs provide aerial overwatch, using onboard optical cameras to discover, localize, and track these targets before the UGVs encounter them. The difficulty is that neither fleet can plan effectively in isolation. The value of a UAV observation depends on its relevance to the ground routes, while the admissibility of those routes depends on what the UAVs have observed. Moreover, failing to detect a target does not imply that a region is empty, since the camera has a limited field of view and a non-unit probability of detection. The planning problem must therefore account for both uncertainty in detected targets and the possibility of targets that have not yet been observed. A natural approach to the aerial search problem is ergodic control, which seeks tra-jectories whose time-averaged spatial statistics match a prescribed distribution over the search domain. Regions assigned greater probability mass receive proportionally more sens-ing effort, allowing a single objective to express both broad exploration and concentrated information gathering. The work of Mathew and Mezi´ c (2011) introduced multiscale met-rics for measuring deviations from ergodicity and designing multi-agent coverage dynam-ics. Subsequent works formulated continuous-time ergodic trajectory optimization (Miller and Murphey, 2013) and used expected information densities to guide active sensing with nonlinear measurement models (Miller et al., 2016). The commonly used spectral metric compares weighted Fourier coefficients of the trajectory occupation measure with those of the desired spatial distribution. Alternatively, Hughes et al. (2025) formulate ergodic tra-jectory optimization using maximum mean discrepancy between kernel mean embeddings in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, allowing coverage objectives to be specified from samples on more general domains. For a fleet of UAVs, the ergodic objective depends on the aggregate coverage of all agents and thus couples their trajectories through the team Fourier coefficients. Abraham and Murphey (2018) address this coupling through consensus on the coverage statistics, obtaining decentralized ergodic control for agents with nonlinear dynamics. The sensing model introduces a second difficulty: the point occupation measure of a vehicle does not describe the ground area observed by a camera whose footprint changes with altitude and attitude. Finite sensor footprints and obstacle constraints have been incorporated into ergodic coverage by Ayvali et al. (2017). Here, we derive the elliptical ground footprint from the position and attitude of the camera and construct a smooth, normalized sensing kernel from this geometry. Integrating this kernel along a trajectory gives an occupation measure that describes the spatial distribution of camera coverage and depends on both the translational and rotational motion of the UAV. Its spectral coefficients enter the fleet ergodic objective, so that the trajectories are optimized with respect to the area observed by the cameras. The same camera geometry also enters a spatially varying detection probability

and an angles-only measurement model with pose-dependent covariance. This connects the coverage model used in trajectory planning with the missed detections and measurement uncertainty accounted for in Bayesian inference. The search distribution must, in turn, reflect the information collected by the UAVs. Random finite set (RFS) theory provides a natural Bayesian description of this informa-tion, since both the number of targets and their states are uncertain (Mahler, 2007). The measurements are also a finite set, containing an unknown combination of target detec-tions and clutter, with some targets producing no measurement. Poisson multi-Bernoulli mixture (PMBM) filtering represents undetected targets by a Poisson point process and detected targets by Bernoulli components conditioned on global data-association hypothe-ses (Garc´ ıa-Fern´ andez et al., 2018). Marginalizing the association uncertainty provides tractable multi-Bernoulli approximations (Williams, 2015). These ideas have also been used for multi-agent planning that balances the discovery of new objects with the track-ing of existing ones (Nguyen et al., 2020). Here, we specialize the measurement update to camera-based, angles-only observations with pose-dependent detection and measurement quality, and maintain a Poisson multi-Bernoulli (PMB) approximation of the target pos-terior. This distinction between detected and undetected targets is particularly useful for ground planning, since avoiding known tracks alone does not account for threats in regions that have not been sufficiently explored. Planning around the estimated target positions alone neglects this uncertainty. Chance-constrained trajectory optimization instead limits the probability of violating a safety re-quirement (Blackmore et al., 2011), and confidence sets provide a geometric means of ac-counting for uncertain target locations. For a multi-object belief, the corresponding question is whether an entire region contains any targets. The RFS void probability, namely, the probability that a set contains no objects, has been used to impose safety constraints in sensor control (Beard et al., 2017); more recently, Han et al. (2026) have developed belief control barrier functions from a Poisson representation of multi-object uncertainty. We use the PMB void probability over the corridor swept by a candidate UGV route, which accounts for both the residual undetected-target intensity and the existence and location uncertainty of detected tracks. Its negative logarithm supplies a route-risk cost, while a separate threshold on the remaining fleet corridor determines when the UGVs may proceed under the current belief. As sensing reduces uncertainty or reveals a threat, the ground routes and the aerial search distribution are updated accordingly. Solving the resulting fleet trajectory problems at a centralized ground station requires aggregating all vehicle decision variables and constraints. A decentralized formulation in-stead allows each vehicle to solve a local problem and exchange the quantities needed to coordinate with its neighbors. Consensus alternating direction method of multipliers (C-ADMM) provides an established framework for this purpose when the local problems are convex (Boyd et al., 2011). For trajectory planning, Rey et al. (2018) combine decentralized ADMM with adaptive linearization of collision constraints for agents with linear dynamics. For nonlinear model predictive control, Stomberg et al. (2026) develop a closely related nested scheme in which decentralized sequential quadratic programming uses ADMM to solve the coupled quadratic subproblems. In our setting, nonlinear vehicle dynamics, cam-era footprints, and collision constraints likewise require an outer approximation method before the fleet problem can be distributed into convex local solves.

To this end, we use successive convexification (SCvx), which replaces the nonlinear op-timal control problem by a sequence of convex subproblems, with virtual controls and trust regions to handle infeasible linearizations and control the approximation error (Mao et al., 2016). The SCvx* extension embeds these iterations in an augmented Lagrangian frame-work to address feasibility of the original nonlinear problem (Oguri, 2023). We also adopt the continuous-time constraint formulation of Elango et al. (2024), in which integrated constraint violations are represented by auxiliary dynamics, to account for inter-agent sep-aration between discretization nodes. Within each SCvx* iteration, local copies and graph flows separate the shared collision constraints and the global ergodic coefficient sum, allow-ing the convex subproblem to be solved using neighbor-to-neighbor C-ADMM updates. The main contributions of this work are as follows: 1. We formulate multi-UAV ergodic trajectory optimization with optical sensor foot-prints that depend on the full camera pose. Starting from a conic field of view, we derive the position, orientation, and extent of the ground footprint and construct a smooth footprint kernel that defines the team occupation measure and its Fourier coefficients. The resulting ergodic objective measures coverage of the ground area observed by the cameras throughout their motion. 2. We connect Bayesian inference from UAV angles-only measurements to both aerial and ground planning through a shared PMB target belief. The resulting formulations direct aerial sensing toward unexplored regions along the ground routes and informa-tive views of detected targets. The same belief accounts for detected and undetected threats in ground planning through confidence-set avoidance and a route-risk cost based on the PMB void probability. 3. We develop a novel decentralized method for non-convex fleet trajectory optimiza-tion problems by combining outer SCvx iterations with inner C-ADMM solves. The method accommodates nonlinear dynamics, continuous-time inter-agent collision avoidance, and nonseparable team objectives. For the UAV problem, we addition-ally introduce a new graph-flow formulation of C-ADMM that replaces the global coefficient-sum constraint with local balance equations and neighbor agreement. We establish its equivalence at consensus to the centralized convex subproblem, en-abling decentralized optimization of the coupled ergodic objective without a central coefficient aggregator. 4. We construct a closed-loop coordination scheme that updates aerial sensing plans as the target belief evolves, replans ground routes when newly confirmed threats affect them, and authorizes ground motion according to the remaining route risk. Numerical case studies assess UAV search through spatial coverage and ergodic mismatch, and UGV planning through route risk, control effort, and progress toward the goals. We also evaluate solver convergence, computational scaling, and solution quality relative to centralized optimization. The paper is organized as follows. Sections 2 and 3 introduce the notation and math-ematical preliminaries. Section 4 formulates the UAV sensing and trajectory optimization problem and its decentralized solution. 3 Preliminaries 

This section introduces random finite set models for target inference in Section 5 and the spectral ergodic metric for UAV coverage in Section 4. Sequential convex programming addresses the nonlinear UAV and UGV trajectory problems, an isoperimetric reformula-tion enforces continuous-time pairwise separation, and consensus ADMM distributes the resulting convex subproblems across each fleet. 

3.1 Random Finite Set Theory 

When detecting objects 1 in an unknown environment, there are often two sources of am-biguity regarding the objects, namely, both the estimated states of detected targets and the number (or cardinality) of the targets themselves. Indeed, it is rare that one would ever know the true number of targets in a domain, aside from some exceptional prior re-connaissance or intelligence. In this context, a Random Finite Set (RFS) is precisely the mathematical tool that can model uncertainty in both the cardinality of a set and the el-ements making up that set (Mahler, 2007). Below we give an overview of two classes of RFS which will be used in the sequel, namely the i) Multi-Bernoulli (MB) RFS, and the ii) Poisson Point Process (PPP).

3.2 Spectral Ergodic Control 

Ergodic control refers to the formulation and solution of optimization problems that drive a dynamical system towards ergodicity. In the context of multi-agent collaborative search, there will be one shared base distribution over the domain which the UAVs will jointly cover. Asymptotically, the fleet should spend more time in regions of higher (prescribed) probability density than in regions of lower density. Having a perfect balance between the time-varying statistics of the trajectory distribution, and the underlying spatial distribution of which to search, is in essence an ergodic system. 3.3 Sequential Convex Programming 

Successive convexification, or sequential convex programming (SCP), refers to an optimiza-tion method to find local minima of nonlinear and non-convex programs, by iteratively lin-earizing the problem around a given reference, solving the linearized (convex) sub-problem, using that solution as the next reference, and iterating until convergence. SCP is a rich and well-studied topic and has been successfully applied to numerous problems in guid-ance and control. For a good overview, we refer the reader to (Mao et al., 2016; Oguri, 2023). In this particular work, we use a variant of SCP, called SCvx*, to solve the non-linear UAV and UGV optimization problems. Under the regularity, penalty-update, and subproblem-accuracy assumptions stated in (Oguri, 2023), SCvx* provides feasibility and local convergence guarantees; whether those assumptions hold for each specialized team problem must be verified separately, and a finite numerical run yields only a candidate so-lution subject to the chosen stopping tolerances. Remark 1 (Computational complexity) . For fixed state and control dimensions and a fixed number of spectral modes, agent i’s primal vector has dν,i = O(N (1 + |N i|)) entries. Runtime can be improved by exploiting temporal sparsity and warm-starting successive solves (Wang and Boyd, 2010), while graph-based decomposition enables parallel local computations (Boyd et al., 2011).

9 Conclusion 

In this work, we have developed a framework for information-driven collaboration between UAV and UGV fleets in a domain containing an unknown number of targets. We formulated aerial search using an ergodic objective whose occupation measure accounts for the ground area observed by pose-dependent camera footprints. Bayesian inference from angles-only measurements provides a PMB belief over detected and undetected targets, linking aerial search priorities to ground-route risk. The resulting coordination scheme updates both fleets’ plans as information becomes available and permits ground motion once the remaining route risk satisfies a prescribed threshold. We developed a nested SCvx*–C-ADMM method for the nonconvex fleet trajectory problems, accounting for nonlinear dynamics, nonseparable objectives, and continuous-time inter-agent separation. For the UAV problem, the graph-flow formulation replaces the global coefficient-sum constraint with local balance equations and neighbor agreement. Its equivalence at consensus to the centralized convex subproblem allows the coupled ergodic objective to be optimized without a central coefficient aggregator. The numerical studies demonstrate how the UAVs distribute camera coverage along the ground routes and how accounting for undetected threats changes the UGV risk–effort tradeoff. The solver comparisons assess convergence, solution quality, and computational scaling relative to centralized optimization. Closed-loop missions with one and two UGVs further demonstrate the interaction between aerial sensing, target confirmation, ground replanning, and motion authorization as the ground vehicles progress to their goals. Future work will consider UGVs with onboard sensing and decentralized RFS fusion, allowing both fleets to contribute observations through local belief updates and inter-agent communication. Further investigation of the nested optimization structure may improve convergence and reduce the computation and communication required by the consensus iterations. Lastly, control-barrier-function safety filters adapted to the relative degree of the vehicle dynamics could address execution errors and disturbances (Ames et al., 2017; Xiao and Belta, 2022).
