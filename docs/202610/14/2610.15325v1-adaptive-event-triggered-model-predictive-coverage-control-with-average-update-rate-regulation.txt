# Adaptive Event-Triggered Model Predictive Coverage Control with Average Update-Rate Regulation 

Pengbo Zhu 1, Jicheng Shi 2, Colin N. Jones 2, and Andreas A. Malikopoulos 3

Abstract — Model predictive coverage control for multi-agent systems operates under tight communication and computation budgets, which requires it to balance the load of optimization against responsiveness to varying spatial priorities. With a fixed event threshold, the update rate varies with operating condi-tions, making a prescribed average update budget difficult to maintain. We propose threshold-adaptive event-triggered model predictive control (MPC), which adjusts the threshold online in proportion to the difference between the target average period and the latest inter-event interval. We prove convergence of the empirical average update period to any feasible prescribed target and establish initialization-dependent one-sided bounds on the cumulative average. A nonlinear benchmark illustrates these budget guarantees. We then apply the mechanism to coverage MPC using a task-aware statistic that captures robot motion and changes in spatial importance. In a five-agent moving-density study, the adaptive policy uses 204.7 global MPC updates on average against a target of 200, compared with 266.7 for a fixed-threshold policy using the same statistic and initial threshold. It also reduces coverage cost relative to periodic schedules with exactly matched update counts. These results demonstrate direct average-budget regulation and effective task-dependent scheduling of MPC updates. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

A. Background 

Coverage control coordinates mobile robots to distribute sensing or service over an area according to spatial de-mand. A spatial importance density assigns greater weight to regions requiring closer coverage; Voronoi partitions and density-weighted centroids provide a classical means of or-ganizing the robots [1]. When the density varies, the desired configuration changes with the task [2]. Maintaining cover-age therefore requires refreshed position information, spatial references, and control decisions. Frequent coordination can burden communication, while repeated optimization adds computational cost. Event-triggered control (ETC) schedules updates according to observed need rather than a fixed clock [3]. B. Related Work 1) Model predictive coverage control: Model predictive control (MPC) incorporates robot dynamics and constraints into the coverage problem, but each update entails solving an optimization problem for each participating robot. Carron and Zeilinger [6] combine centroid-based coverage with tracking MPC. Subsequent architectures address learning in unknown environments [7] and multirate planning and tracking for time-varying coverage [8]. These developments make the choice of when to refresh references and recompute control plans an important part of resource-aware coverage. 

2) Resource budgets and event-triggered MPC: This pa-per considers a prescribed average update period as a simple resource specification: recompute the MPC plan once every 

α sampling steps on average, where α ≥ 1 is a user-specified target. This specifies a long-run update rate rather than a hard limit on concurrent computations, as considered in [9]. For a synchronously updated team of M agents, each global MPC update entails M local solves. The update count therefore provides a computational resource measure, and total communication also depends on the implementation of trigger monitoring. Event-triggered MPC recomputes a control plan when a triggering condition is met, for example when a state-prediction error exceeds a threshold [10]. Adaptive triggering has also been studied for perturbed nonlinear systems [11]. However, selecting a threshold does not directly specify the resulting average update period. A fixed threshold may require offline calibration, and the resulting rate can change with the disturbance regime or task evolution. Average-rate design has precedents: Brunner et al. [12] develop robust event-triggered MPC with a guaranteed average sampling rate, while Deng et al. [13] use the disturbance distribution to construct triggering sets for a prescribed expected inter-execution time. Our objective is instead to regulate the realized empirical average period through online threshold feedback, without requiring a disturbance distribution. The coverage control problem highlights why both task sensitivity and update rate matter. A moving density can change the desired configuration even when robots follow their predicted dynamics exactly; prediction error alone may therefore miss a relevant task change. A task-aware statistic can detect that change, but its growth rate also depends on robot motion and density evolution. A threshold calibrated for one density speed may consequently produce a different arXiv:2610.15325v1 [eess.SY] 11 Oct 2026 update rate at another speed. Adapting the threshold from realized inter-event intervals addresses this budget mismatch. Periodic scheduling can also meet an average target, but event-based scheduling retains the freedom to place updates when they are useful to the coverage task. 

C. Contributions and Organization 

We propose threshold-adaptive event-triggered MPC that adjusts its threshold using the difference between the de-sired average period and the latest inter-event interval. The feedback principle is related to our disturbance-adaptive predictive control work [14], [15], which regulates average constraint violations; here, the regulated quantity is the average inter-event period. Our contributions are: 1) A threshold-adaptation mechanism that regulates the average MPC update period to a prescribed tar-get without requiring a disturbance distribution. Un-der bounded-trigger and executable-plan assumptions, we establish asymptotic average-period tracking and initialization-dependent one-sided bounds. 2) A task-aware application to multi-agent coverage MPC, with a sufficient trigger bound and a global MPC update-budget interpretation. 3) Complementary numerical evidence: a nonlinear benchmark illustrates the timing guarantees, while moving-density coverage demonstrates budget regula-tion under a task-speed change and improved coverage relative to periodic schedules with matched update counts. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II presents the problem formulation, and Section III presents the method and its theoretical guarantees. Section IV evaluates the nonlinear benchmark. Section V presents the coverage formulation, the triggering mechanism, and the associated case study. This supports operation without scenario-specific threshold initialization in this case. VI. C ONCLUSION 

Coverage control must respond to changing spatial de-mand while managing the cost of repeated coordination and optimization. Event triggering allows update times to follow task changes, while threshold adaptation regulates their average frequency as operating conditions vary. The proposed threshold-adaptive event-triggered MPC makes the desired long-run average update period a direct design input, with asymptotic tracking and initialization-dependent one-sided guarantees under the stated assumptions, without re-quiring a disturbance distribution. The nonlinear benchmark illustrates these timing properties. In the moving-density coverage study, adaptation maintains the update count near the requested budget when the density moves faster than in calibration, while event-dependent timing improves coverage relative to periodic schedules with matched update counts. The principal benefit is a direct update-budget interface that retains the responsiveness of event-triggered MPC.
