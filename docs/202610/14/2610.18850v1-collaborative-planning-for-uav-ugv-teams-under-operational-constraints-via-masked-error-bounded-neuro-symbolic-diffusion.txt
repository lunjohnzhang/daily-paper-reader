# Collaborative Planning for UAV-UGV Teams under Operational Constraints via Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion 

Zihao Deng 1, Qianhuang Li 1, Maggie Wigness 2,John Rogers 2, Donghyun Kim 1, and Hao Zhang 11 University of Massachusetts Amherst

Abstract: Collaborative planning under operational constraints is essential for heterogeneous robot teams in large-scale missions. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) provide rapid area coverage but are limited by onboard energy, while Un-manned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) can serve as mobile recharging stations yet are constrained by road-network traversability. Effective collaboration must enforce op-erational constraints during execution while enabling parallel, coordinated actions. Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) have shown promise through token unmasking but have two key limitations: entropy-guided scheduling assumes distributional alignment and reliance on differentiable constraints for diffusion-time optimization. We propose Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion (MEND), which formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process to generate context-aware action sequences for UAV-UGV teams. MEND introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies at execution by reweighting the learned action distribution with a logic-induced admissibility indicator, thereby enforcing compliance with operational constraints. MEND introduces an error-bounded inference scheduling at execution that constructs the unmasking schedule by selecting positions with minimal predic-tive entropy over the constraint-aware action distribution, enhancing collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. Extensive experimental results in simulation, on real-world maps, and with physical robot teams demonstrate that MEND advances UAV-UGV collaborative planning under operational constraints. More details are available on the website: https://hcrlab.gitlab.io/project/mend. 

Keywords: Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Planning, Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion.

Collaborative planning enables multi-robot sys-tems to coordinate actions and share resources for interdependent tasks, supporting parallel op-eration [1], scalability [2], and robustness [3]. In applications such as search and rescue [4–7] and environmental monitoring [8–11], robot teams are often heterogeneous, comprising platforms with complementary capabilities and constraints. For example, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) pro-vide rapid area coverage but are limited by on-board energy, whereas Unmanned Ground Vehi-cles (UGVs) offer substantial power reserves and 

10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026), Austin TX, USA. arXiv:2610.18850v1 [cs.RO] 13 Oct 2026 enable in-mission UAV recharging, yet are constrained by road-network traversability. Consequently, collaborative planning must explicitly enforce operational constraints at execution time. To ensure efficiency, multi-robot systems must execute plans in parallel with synchronized actions (Figure 1). Optimization-based methods, e.g., mixed-integer programming [12–14], and symbolic planners, e.g., based on First-Order Logic (FOL) [15–17], provide optimal solutions and constraint satisfaction but scale poorly due to combinatorial search. Reinforcement learning methods [18] often assume greedy recharging, simplify robot motion, or encode constraints only during training. Diffusion models excel in trajectory planning [19–22], while Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) achieve stronger results via token unmasking [23, 24]. MDMs typically employ entropy-guided inference scheduling [25, 26] to improve sampling quality. However, they assume alignment between the learned and true distributions; when generated actions violate constraints, entropy estimates are distorted, leading to suboptimal solutions that breach operational constraints in collaborative planning. Recent work extends MDMs to constrained generation via classifier-guidance [27] and neuro-symbolic constraint enforcement during sampling [28, 29]. However, a theoretical limitation is that they rely on gradient-based optimization over differentiable constraints in sampling, whereas operational constraints are often discrete, non-differentiable, and not preserved by standard diffusion dynamics. We propose Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion (MEND ), a theoretical approach for collaborative planning in heterogeneous teams under operational constraints. MEND represents interdependent operational contexts as a graph, and formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process to generate context-aware action sequences for UAVs and UGVs. MEND introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies at execution time by reweighting the learned action distribution with a FOL-induced admissibility indicator, thereby enforcing compliance with operational constraints at execution. MEND also introduces an error-bounded inference scheduling strategy at execution that constructs the unmasking schedule by selecting positions with minimal predictive entropy over the constraint-aware action distribution, enhancing collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. The joint action sequences generated by MEND are executed by UAVs and UGVs simultaneously, which enables parallel operation of heterogeneous robots while satisfying operational constraints. Our contribution is MEND, a novel and principled learning approach for masked error-bounded neuro-symbolic diffusion, to enable collaborative planning for UAV-UGV teams under operational constraints. MEND introduces two key novelties: MEND introduces two key novelties: • Execution-time masked neuro-symbolic diffusion that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies via neuro-symbolic reasoning , reshaping the learned action distribution into a constraint-aware distribution using a logic-induced admissibility indicator. This enables the satisfaction of non-differentiable constraints that cannot be optimized by gradient-based diffusion updates, thereby enforcing operational constraints for UAV-UGV teams. • Execution-time error-bounded inference scheduling , which guarantees that predictive entropy computed under the constraint-aware action distribution incurs a provably smaller sampling error relative to the true distribution. This reduces misalignment between the learned and true action distributions, thereby improving collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. 

2 Related Work 

Collaborative Planning for Multi-Robot Systems Optimization-based methods formulate UAV-UGV planning as NP-hard routing problems. Mixed Integer Programming (MIP) [12–14, 30], dynamic programming [31–33], and heuristic search methods [34–39] can compute optimal or near-optimal solutions, but their computational cost limits scalability and real-time deployment. Symbolic planning employs predicate-based representations for constraint-guaranteed reasoning. Prior work uses action language BC [40, 41], Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) [42, 43], Temporal Logic (TL) [44, 45], and Backus-Naur Form (BNF) [46] to model mission structures and 2operational constraints in multi-robot systems. These formalisms are largely grounded in First-Order Logic (FOL) [15–17], enabling quantified reasoning for constraint-aware multi-robot planning. Reinforcement Learning has been applied to routing tasks [47–49] with energy-constraints [50– 52], combined with Graph Learning to encode context [53–55]. Neuro-symbolic approaches have emerged for both single-robot [56] and multi-robot systems [57–60] for constraint satisfaction. Energy-constrained UAVs with UGV support have been explored [18]. However, these methods are often limited by greedy recharging strategies, simplify UGV traversal as straight-line trajectories, and enforce constraints during training rather than providing execution-time guarantees. 

Masked Diffusion Models. Diffusion models have shown promise in trajectory planning [19–22]. In discrete domains, masked diffusion models (MDMs) achieve competitive results in task and maze planning [23, 24, 61–63]. Recent work extends MDMs to constrained generation via classifier-guidance and neuro-symbolic reasoning [27–29]. However, these approaches rely on differentiable constraints optimized during diffusion via gradient-based updates, whereas operational constraints are often discrete, non-differentiable, and not preserved by standard diffusion dynamics. MDMs support flexible inference schedules, whose choice strongly affects sampling quality, as random unmasking can lead to error propagation [64]. Prior work improves sampling via variable-length generation [24], caching [65], and entropy-guided scheduling [25, 66], with recent theory relating optimal scheduling to predictive entropy [26]. However, existing entropy-guided scheduling assumes alignment between the learned and true data distributions; when constraints are violated, entropy estimates become distorted, resulting in biased unmasking schedules in practice.

Symbolic Logic in Robotic Planning can be formalized by a tuple (O, A, Φ, R) [69], where O

5 Experiments 

Experimental Setups We evaluate MEND in a high-fidelity Unity-based 3D multi-robot simulator in ROS, using real-world maps reconstructed from 3D LiDAR scans, and on physical robot teams. Mission complexity ranges from 15 tasks with 5 paths to 45 tasks with 10 paths, and team size from 1 UAV-1 UGV to 4 UAV-2 UGV. Experiments are conducted in a 10 , km × 10 , km region, where UAVs and UGVs operate at fixed speeds of qa = 10 m/s and qg = 4 .5 m/s, with energy capacities of 287 .7 kJ and 36 ,810 kJ, respectively. UAV and UGV energy consumption are modeled as 0.0461( qa)3 − 0.5834( qa)2 − 1.8761 qa + 229 .6 and 464 .8qg + 356 .3, respectively [72]. In all scenarios, UAVs visit tasks while UGVs provide recharging support along road networks under operational constraints. To obtain expert data, we adopt MIP for constrained UAV task assignment and UGV path planning. While effective offline, MIP is unsuitable for real-time execution. See Appendices D and E for implementation and training details. We compare MEND against three metaheuristics planners and two baselines: (1) Guided Local Search 

(GLS ) [34], which augments the routing with penalties to bias the search away from repeatedly selected task assignments; (2) Tabu Search (TS ) [35], which performs an iterative neighborhood search with a tabu list; (3) Simulated Annealing (SA ) [36], which allows early exploration via probabilistic acceptance of worse routes and tightens acceptance over iterations; (4) Vanilla MDM 

(VMDM ): a baseline method performs diffusion sampling from the learned distribution using a uniform scheduling; and (5) Entropy-guided MDM (EMDM ), a baseline method (presented in the preliminary based on [25]), which replaces uniform scheduling with entropy-guided scheduling while sampling from the unconstrained learned distribution. We quantitatively evaluate and compare MEND with other methods using three metrics: (1) Task Completion Rate (TCR ) [73] is defined as the ratio of completed tasks to all required tasks, measured as a percentage; (2) Makespan [74] is defined as the total time required to complete all task visits, and is reported only for scenarios in which all methods achieve full task completion, measured in seconds; (3) Energy [75] is defined as the cumulative energy consumption of all UAVs and UGVs over the mission horizon, measured in kilojoules. 6 Limitation 

MEND has two main limitations. First, MEND plans over a fixed-length masked sequence with a static task set within each planning episode; although replanning can accommodate new tasks, dynamic task arrivals are not explicitly modeled. Second, while MEND supports decentralized execution via local graph communication, planning relies on joint inference, which potentially limits robustness under communication constraints. Future work will investigate online task insertion with variable-length masked diffusion models for horizon-agnostic planning and graph-factorized masked diffusion for fully decentralized planning under limited communication. 

7 Conclusion 

We propose MEND, a theoretical approach for collaborative planning in heterogeneous robot teams under operational constraints. MEND formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process for context-aware planning of both UAVs and UGVs. It introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains masked diffusion policies to enforce compliance with operational constraints during execution, together with an error-bounded inference scheduling strategy to improve collaborative planning optimality under these constraints. Extensive experiments in simulation, on real-world maps, and with physical robots demonstrate that MEND advances UAV-UGV collaborative planning under operational constraints. 9Acknowledgments
