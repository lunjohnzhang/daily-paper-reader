# Multi-Agent Path Planning via Compatible Encounter-Space Allocation in Crowded Spaces Dynamically Occupied by Work Activities     

> Boda Liu Institute of Intelligent Manufacturing Technology, Hefei University of Technology, Hefei, China wlbdwww@163.com Xuezhi Yang* School of Computer Science and Information Engineering, Hefei University of Technology, Hefei, China xzyang@hfut.edu.cn Bin Yang* College of Civil Engineering, Tongji University, Shanghai, China yangbi@tongji.edu.cn Shu Li Hefei Institute of Public Safety Research, Tsinghua University, Hefei, China lishu@tsinghua-hf.edu.cn * Corresponding authors

# Abstract 

Lifelong multi-agent path planning on construction floors must accommodate continually changing goals and work occupancy. Existing planners can update paths and timing online, but work–motion space coupling changes the spatial basis of unfinished encounters, while rerouting affects later encounters. Planning must therefore construct and maintain local encounter passability. We propose Width-based Planning with Compatible Encounter-Space Allocation (WP), which constructs shared passage relationships from actual encounter demands and real-time widths. Shared channels, ordered anchors, and urgency-based updates coordinate successive encounters while retaining individual greedy motion between them. A necessary and sufficient width condition for fixed-point position exchange and a necessary directional condition for composite exchange provide geometric grounds for channel construction and encounter relocation. In crowded-floor computational experiments, WP maintained route continuation after occupancy changes and enabled multi-agent clearing, with infrequent, brief near-stops. Complete-run comparisons with four methods showed 25.2%–52.2% more mean goal arrivals than the best baseline at each configuration of 5–10 agents, with lower mean computation time than the rolling-horizon K-CBS adaptation. The contribution is a planning principle that constructs and continuously maintains local passage relationships from real-time space, unifying shared spatial allocation and conditional local clearing to extend lifelong planning to work–motion space coupling. 

Keywords: lifelong multi-agent path planning; coupling between work and motion spaces; compatible encounter-space allocation; local passability; real-time width measurement 

# 1. Introduction 

Confined activity areas on building floors are linked by narrow entrances. Workers and mobile equipment repeatedly travel between work locations and material storage areas, receiving a new motion goal after reaching the current one. This gives rise to lifelong multi-agent path planning: agents must select routes as goal demands change and repeatedly coordinate passage through shared space. Multi-agent path planning coordinates individual paths and passage times to resolve motion conflicts and has been applied to warehouse transport and ongoing pickup-and-delivery tasks [1,2]. Continually arriving goals, neighboring motion, and local blockages require online adjustment. Existing approaches use reciprocal collision avoidance to respond 2to neighbors [3,4], rolling-horizon planning and continuous conflict search to update paths and timing [5,6], and topological guidance, corridor reasoning, safe regions, and behavioral rules to organize passage and recovery from blockage in crowded spaces [7–14]. Construction tasks both generate motion demands and occupy or release surrounding motion space as work starts and ends. This coupling between work and motion spaces changes not only motion goals but also the spatial conditions supporting unfinished encounters. New occupancy may reduce the space available for velocity-based avoidance or invalidate paths, passage positions, and ordering arrangements. Detours then alter encounters with other agents, so a local repair must also connect to subsequent passage arrangements. Previous studies have addressed ongoing tasks, environment configuration, and dynamic navigation [2,5,15–17], but have given less focused attention to how work activation and completion interact with lifelong motion to change encounter conditions. Persistent blockage interrupts movement during construction activities. The resulting planning problem is the construction and maintenance of local encounter passability in changing confined spaces : establishing compatible passage arrangements from the available space, retaining valid relationships as conditions change, and reconstructing the affected parts. We address this problem through Width-based Planning with Compatible Encounter-Space Allocation (WP) . Joint decisions focus on predicted body conflicts along current routes. Real-time width, body size, and relative motion determine passage arrangements, which shared channels and ordered anchors connect across multiple agents. New urgent encounters trigger updates to affected arrangements while valid relationships are retained; continuous, greedily guided routes remain available between encounters. A necessary and sufficient width condition for fixed-point exchange and a necessary directional condition for composite exchange establish the geometry underlying channel construction and encounter relocation. Compatible channels organize normal passage and provide space for temporal yielding and unidirectional clearing when entry is blocked. Constructing and maintaining passability thus become parts of one planning process. Computational experiments on a crowded building floor with complex corridors and narrow entrances showed that WP continued trips toward their original goals after work occupancy changed and enabled passage and clearing in encounters involving two to five agents. Across multiple complete WP runs, near-stops accounted for 0.393% of navigating agent-steps; the longest continuous near-stop within a trip toward the same goal lasted 1.0 s. The encounter sequences and motion records demonstrate local blockage clearing in the crowded scenarios examined. In complete-run comparisons with four representative adapted methods, WP achieved a higher mean number of goal arrivals at every configuration of 5–10 agents. The increase over the best baseline at each configuration was 25.2%–52.2%, with larger relative gains at higher densities. Mean per-step computation time was lower than that of the rolling-horizon K-CBS adaptation. Together, these results show that WP combined sustained passage, efficient goal arrival, and computational economy under the tested occupancy changes and continuous motion demands. The method combines group coordination with individual freedom to follow greedy routes, complementing representations based on grid positions, spatial partitions, and sampled connections [6,18,19] with joint structures generated from actual encounter demands. Geometric derivations establish the conditions for local construction, encounter records demonstrate continued motion, and complete-run comparisons provide system-level evidence. This architecture extends lifelong planning to work–motion space coupling and offers a methodological basis for engineering settings with both task occupancy and continual travel demands. It may also inform local collision-avoidance dependencies in dynamic configuration spaces, including those of robotic manipulators; the corresponding separation, reachability, and clearing conditions must be established for their kinematics. 3

# 2. Related work 

Multi-agent pathfinding (MAPF) coordinates occupancy of shared positions and passage timing as agents travel between their goals. Spatial representations, conflict definitions, and optimization objectives jointly specify solution requirements [20]. For work–motion space coupling, we review three successive questions: how joint decisions can focus on necessary interactions, how local space supports mutual passage, and how passage relationships persist as motion and occupancy change. 

2.1 Localizing joint decisions and representing space 

Reactive collision avoidance adjusts velocities directly in response to neighboring motion. Velocity obstacles exclude relative velocities that can cause collisions [21]; Reciprocal Velocity Obstacles (RVO) distribute avoidance responsibility between agents, and Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA) formulates velocity selection with linear constraints [3,4]. Combined with obstacle-avoiding paths, these methods provide hierarchical navigation, although narrow spaces still require coordination of several bodies passing together. Search methods coordinate paths and occupancy order in advance. Conflict-Based Search (CBS) adds constraints incrementally to eliminate path conflicts [22], while Priority-Based Search (PBS) compares precedence relationships between agents [23]. Graph resolution affects both geometric freedom and computational scale: coarse graphs may omit lateral room, whereas fine graphs increase search and update costs. Safe Interval Path Planning (SIPP) represents passage opportunities as collision-free time intervals [24], and Kinodynamic Conflict-Based Search (K-CBS) directly coordinates continuous trajectories [6]. These representations expand space–time choices but increase the burden of joint planning. Local coupling jointly plans only agents or paths that affect one another. M* expands joint search as new interactions arise [25]; Experience-based Multi-Agent Path Finding (EMP) retrieves offline solutions for narrow-corridor conflicts, with reuse restricted by stored coordination patterns [9]; CDR-RRT guides continuous search using a passage skeleton and its capacity [7]. Interaction scope, existing solutions, and spatial structure each provide a way to reduce search. New construction occupancy can restrict avoidance directions, invalidate connections, and require local coordination to be rebuilt. Online adjustment must therefore determine lateral positions and passage order from current clearance. Existing routes already encode connectivity, obstacle avoidance, and forward direction. Predicting simultaneous body conflicts along them identifies where joint decisions are needed and provides a basis for constructing body-placement choices from real-time geometry. 

2.2 Conditions for spatial separation and encounter completion 

Spatial-separation methods first assign admissible regions and then generate body motion. Buffered Voronoi Cells (BVC) subtract body margins from neighboring agents’ partitions and use a right-hand rule to restore blocked motion [18]. This simplifies collision constraints, but local configurations still need adjustment when the partitions restrict progress toward a goal. Safety constraints can combine with goal-directed motion to produce blocked equilibria [26]. IMPC-DR adjusts spatial separation over a receding horizon, excludes stable deadlocks under specified goal conditions, and analyzes recursive feasibility [11]. DLSC-CDPOP combines time-varying safe domains, neighboring-trajectory exchange, and distributed optimization, then applies rightward yielding and priority-based retreat to address blockage [13]. These studies connect body separation, sustained progress, and recovery; their respective guarantee conditions are distinct. 4Passage rules must also fit the available geometry. Social-rule coordination uses door location, width, and entry or exit direction to prioritize departing agents [14]. The narrow-road passing method SM-NR searches for gaps based on width occupancy and selects passing positions and motion plans [27]. Roads have a defined longitudinal direction, whereas general planar encounters require current routes to determine the measurement direction. When construction clearance changes, existing partitions, preferred directions, and capacities must be rechecked. Independent goals produce different motion directions, so a single capacity value cannot determine passage: opposing bodies require separated positions, same-direction bodies may follow one another, and side changes involve approach and departure. Slowing, turning, and waiting must leave others room to advance and clear; waiting longer cannot clear a channel blocked by stationary bodies. Local passability must therefore relate clearance, body size, relative motion, and temporal yielding to placement spacing, route continuation, and actual clearing. 

2.3 Maintaining and reconstructing relationships under dynamic changes 

Velocities, goals, and occupancy continue to change after passage arrangements have been established. Planning must respond while preserving unfinished coordination. Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR) decomposes continuing goal demands into finite-horizon conflict problems [5]. Each window’s final state becomes the next window’s initial state, so current arrangements must also account for subsequent release of bottleneck space. Execution deviations need not require every relationship to be rebuilt. Homotopy-based coordination on fixed paths permits pauses or delays to adjust timing [28]. Switchable Action Dependency Graphs (SADG) reorder actions over a receding horizon and maintain execution under conditions that prevent circular waiting [29]. Distributed coordination can also propagate motion constraints across multi-task journeys [30]. These methods support retention of valid relationships while adjusting motion; for physical encounters, the dependencies must still be realized in shared passage space. Environment updates concern different objects. Configurable-environment MAPF (C-MAPF) jointly selects layouts and routes [16]; path and action planning (PAPO) coordinates width, load, and directional constraints in construction transport [2]; dynamic construction navigation combines maps, real-time perception, and constrained planning to update motion [17]. Active configuration changes layouts, perception revises passage judgments, and work activation or completion directly changes occupancy. Construction occupancy may invalidate avoidance positions or reduce parallel passage to sequential passage; new members, delays, and rerouting also change encounter urgency. Retaining dependencies requires their geometry to remain valid, and motion updates must respect other unfinished encounters. Replacing everything can disrupt valid arrangements, whereas unconditional reuse can retain invalid constraints. Planning therefore needs conditions for retaining, rebuilding, and releasing relationships, admitting new conflicts promptly and releasing space after bodies clear it. 

2.4 Synthesis and research question 

Existing research uses dynamic avoidance, local search, spatial separation, and dependency maintenance to support safe motion, blockage recovery, and continuous execution. Work–motion space coupling makes these requirements act simultaneously on unfinished encounters: new occupancy changes geometry, rerouting changes participants, and goals and actual progress change subsequent order. Restoring a single route must therefore be accompanied by connecting the agents’ passage relationships; local arrangements also need executable entry and clearing conditions. 5The resulting scientific problem is the construction and maintenance of local encounter passability in changing confined spaces . First, how do clearance, body size, and relative motion determine compatible arrangements and provide space for clearing when entry is blocked? Second, when space, membership, or motion changes, which relationships should be retained, rebuilt, or released, and how should subsequent encounters be connected? WP constructs compatible spatial allocations from real-time widths and maintains continuation through anchored routes and urgency-based updates. Experiments evaluate passage, sustained arrivals, and computational cost. We first establish spatial compatibility conditions, then use them to organize planning and multi-agent coordination.

# 5. Experiments and results 

WP is evaluated through encounter progression, sustained goal arrivals, and computational cost. Encounter replays and motion-state statistics examine how agents continue moving after goal updates, the arrival of additional agents, and changes in work occupancy. 5.1 Case study and experimental setting 

A computational lifelong multi-agent planning scenario was constructed from the typical-floor layout and construction demands of a building. Work on vertical components in the mixed precast and cast-in-place system generates both motion goals and occupied spaces. Workers from different trades travel between material storage and work locations and move between neighboring components. Their task-driven journeys share portals and connecting passages, while operating spaces restrict neighboring movement. Encounter positions and available widths therefore change with construction state. Figure 24 shows the floor layout and worker model. Walls (W), columns (C), and precast components (PC) are the main construction objects; openings between barriers and components connect the activity regions. Workers belong to five trades: reinforcement (R), formwork (F), precast joint treatment (JC), precast grouting (G), and hoisting (HI). Each worker selects tasks compatible with their trade and is represented by a disk of the same radius. Ongoing work occupies its required operating space, while work locations and storage areas alternately serve as movement goals.

The narrow ring around the disk illustrates the safety margin 𝑏 defined in Section 3.1; its thickness is schematic.

Work progression and material replenishment were accelerated, and the quantity carried per trip was reduced, to generate repeated encounters within a finite observation window. These settings increased travel between work locations and storage areas. Subsequent goals were selected from reachable locations compatible with each worker’s trade. When task-generated transfers were insufficient, reachable locations of the same type supplied additional movement demands. The scenario retained the effects of work occupancy on passage geometry while providing more observations of sustained movement and repeated encounters. The experiments use a common observation window of 1,000 simulation steps (200 s), combining encounter replays with statistics from multiple complete runs. Comparative 29 experiments cover six configurations of 5–10 agents, with repeated runs for every method at each configuration.

5.2.4 Near-stop frequency and duration 

Local yielding and execution constraints can temporarily slow an agent; their effects depend on how often and for how long this occurs. One agent’s state during one motion step constitutes an agent-step . Navigation states are classified by the ratio of actual distance traveled to the distance allowed by the nominal speed during that step. A near-stop has a ratio no greater than 1%; low-speed motion has a ratio greater than 1% but below 20%; all remaining navigation states have ratios of at least 20%. Work activity is counted separately. Under accelerated work and material replenishment, navigation accounted for 97.15% of all agent-steps across multiple complete runs. The 126 near-stop agent-steps represented 0.393% of navigation agent-steps, while low-speed motion accounted for 0.128% (Figure 30). Within the same agent’s trip to the same goal, the longest consecutive near-stop lasted five steps, or 1.0 s. Most near-stops were attributed to execution constraints or collision-avoidance adjustments; only a small fraction were explicitly recorded as channel yielding. Near-stops were therefore infrequent and brief during the repeated encounters and goal updates examined, and the complete method generally maintained ongoing movement.

# 6. Discussion 

Work activation and completion change local clearance, while rerouting affects later encounters, coupling current passage to subsequent continuation. Local conflict resolution, width guidance, spatial separation, and dependency maintenance have established foundations [7–14,18,25,28,29]. WP connects these functions through actual encounter demands: it constructs shared passage relationships from real-time space, retains continuous greedy connections between encounters, and updates affected arrangements as space and membership change. Relative to representations organized by grid positions, partition boundaries, or sampled connections [6,18,19], this architecture specifies where shared constraints arise, how they connect, and when they change, while retaining regional topology and geometric path solving. Spatial compatibility criteria provide the geometric basis. Under the stated local-space conditions, Theorem 1 gives the necessary and sufficient width condition for fixed-point exchange, and Theorem 2 identifies the separation directions that composite exchange must cover. They guide channel construction and encounter relocation, respectively. Agents coordinate positions on shared grids using Eq. (34), insert them into individual anchored routes through Eqs. (35)–(36), and repeat prediction after rerouting. All executed motion satisfies Eq. (1). Local spatial conditions therefore govern both initial arrangements and their subsequent retention or reconstruction. Shared channels provide a common spatial basis for yielding and clearing. Separated positions keep passage space available, yielding separates arrival times, and unidirectional channel clearing recursively brings forward blockers into the same direction of motion. Lateral separation along channel axes follows from Eq. (26); finite-step clearing also requires exit space to receive agents and actual progress, as specified in Eq. (38). Records of encounters involving two to five agents and of occupancy changes show how passage and clearing continue through these events. Across complete WP runs, near-stops accounted for 0.393% of navigating agent-steps, and the longest continuous near-stop for the same agent within a trip toward the same goal lasted 1.0 s. These results support the complete method’s ability to maintain motion, although the benefits of individual mechanisms remain to be isolated. In complete-run comparisons with 5–10 agents, WP achieved 25.2%–52.2% more mean goal arrivals than the best baseline. More arrivals were accompanied by shorter mean durations of completed trips, providing consistent evidence of arrival performance. Mean computation time was lower than that of the rolling-horizon K-CBS adaptation, supporting sustained arrivals with manageable computation in the tested setting. These system-level results provide computational evidence for combining shared coordination at encounters with route freedom between them. 38 The contribution is a planning principle that constructs local passage relationships from real-time space and maintains them during continuous motion. Geometric criteria support construction; shared channels and anchor connections embed local arrangements in multi-agent coordination; urgency-based updates and actual clearing determine when relationships are retained or released. Together, these mechanisms address work–motion space coupling and provide a methodological basis for engineering settings with both task occupancy and continual travel demands. 

# 7. Conclusions 

We proposed WP to construct and maintain local encounter passability under work– motion space coupling. It organizes joint decisions around predicted body conflicts and integrates real-time compatible spatial allocation, continuous route connections, and blockage clearing in an architecture generated on demand and updated during execution. The necessary and sufficient width condition for fixed-point exchange and the necessary directional condition for composite exchange provide geometric grounds for channel construction and relocation. Shared channels and ordered anchors connect multi-agent encounters; urgency-based updates retain valid relationships and rebuild affected parts. This yields a planning principle for continuous collective motion organized by local passage relationships, combining shared coordination with greedy route freedom and extending lifelong planning to changing work occupancy and continuing movement demands. Computational experiments on a building floor recorded route continuation after occupancy changes and clearing in multi-agent encounters. Complete WP runs exhibited infrequent, brief near-stops. Across complete 200 s runs with 5–10 agents, WP achieved 25.2%–52.2% more mean goal arrivals than the best baseline at each configuration, with lower mean computation time than the rolling-horizon K-CBS adaptation. These findings provide computational evidence for the architecture in engineering production settings. Local clearing remains subject to the stated geometric and execution conditions; the independent effects of its mechanisms and applicability across settings require controlled evaluation.

K-CBS 滚动适配。本文的贡献在于建立了依据实时空间构造并持续维护局部通过关系 的规划原则，将共同空间分配与有条件的局部疏通统一于同一架构，扩展了终身路径 规划对作业与运动空间耦合情景的处理。

# 1. 引言 

楼层中的受限活动区域通过狭窄入口相连。工人或移动设备持续往返于作业位置 和材料堆场，到达当前目标后继续接受新的运动目标。这构成多代理终身路径规划问 题：代理需要在不断更新的目标需求下选择路线，并在共享空间中反复协调相互通 过。 

多代理路径规划通过协调个体路径与通行时序处理运动冲突，已用于仓储搬运和 持续取送任务 [1,2] 。持续到来的目标、邻居运动及局部受阻要求规划能够在线调整。 已有研究利用互惠避碰响应邻居运动 [3,4] ，利用滚动规划和连续冲突搜索更新路径与 时序 [5,6] ，并通过拓扑引导、走廊推理、安全域及行为规则组织拥挤空间中的通行与 受阻恢复 [7 –14] 。

然而，施工任务在产生新运动需求的同时，还通过作业启停占用或释放周边运动 空间，形成作业与运动空间耦合。运动目标持续更新，尚未完成交会所依赖的空间条 件也可能改变。具体而言，新增占用会压缩速度避让的可用空间，或使原路径、通过 位置及次序安排失效；绕行又改变与其他代理的交会，局部修复因而还需接续后续通 过关系。本文回顾的研究已分别处理持续任务、环境配置和动态导航 [2,5,15 –17] ，但 对作业启停与终身运动共同改变交会条件的过程，针对性讨论仍较少。持续受阻会妨 碍施工活动中的运动接续，因此规划需要解决变化的受限空间中，局部交会可通过性 的构造与持续保持：依据当前空间建立相容的通过安排，在条件变化时保留有效关 系、重建受影响部分。 41 

本文据此提出了基于交会空间相容分配的路线规划方法（ Width-based Planning with Compatible Encounter-Space Allocation ，简称 WP ）。其创新在于将共同决策集中 于当前路线上的预测身体冲突，依据实时宽度、身体尺度与相对运动构造通过关系， 再以共享通道和有序锚点接续多方运动。出现新的紧迫交会时，更新受影响安排并保 持有效关系，交会之间保留贪婪引导下的连续路线。为建立这种空间分配的几何依 据，本文给出了定点换位的充要宽度条件与组合换位的必要方向条件，分别用于通道 构造和交会位置迁移。相容通道既组织正常通过，也在接入受阻时为时间让行和单向 疏通提供空间基础，使可通过性的构造与持续保持纳入同一规划过程。 

在含复杂走廊与狭窄入口的拥挤楼层计算实验中， WP 完成了作业占用变化后的 原目标行程接续及两至五人交会的通过与清出。多次完整 WP 运行中，近停占导航代 理步 0.393% ，同一目标行程内最长连续近停为 1.0 s ；交会过程与运动记录表明， WP 

在所考察的拥挤场景中实现了局部阻塞疏通。与四种代表性适配方法的完整运行比较 显示， WP 在 5—10 代理各配置下的平均目标到达量均高于对照，较各配置最佳对照 提高 25.2% —52.2% ，且较高密度配置的相对优势更大；平均计算耗时低于 K-CBS 滚动适配。这些结果表明， WP 在所考察的占用变化与持续运动条件下，兼顾了持续通 行、到达效率和计算代价。 

本文贡献在于将群体协调与个体贪婪运动自由相结合，为网格位置、空间分区和 采样连接等基本表达 [6,18,19] 补充了按实际交会需求生成共同决策结构的方式。其 中，几何推导给出了局部构造的空间条件，动态交会记录展示了运动接续，完整运行 比较提供了整体性能证据。这一架构扩展了终身路径规划对作业与运动空间耦合过程 的处理，并为其他兼有任务占用与持续转移需求的工程场景提供了方法依据。该方法 也可帮助探索机械臂等系统在动态构型空间中的局部避碰依赖；相应的身体分离、运 动可达与疏通条件需结合其运动学重新建立。 

# 2. 相关研究现状 

多代理路径规划（ MAPF ）通过协调共享位置的占用与通行时序，支持代理在各 自目标之间运动；空间表达、冲突定义与优化目标共同确定求解要求 [20] 。针对作业 与运动空间耦合，本文从三个递进问题回顾相关研究：共同决策如何集中于必要交 互，局部空间如何支持相互通过，以及通过关系如何随运动和占用变化延续。 

2.1 共同决策的局部化与空间表达 

反应式避碰直接调整速度以响应邻居运动。速度障碍方法排除可能导致碰撞的相 对速度 [21] ；互惠速度障碍（ RVO ）由双方分担避让，最优互惠避碰（ ORCA ）将速度 选择转化为线性约束问题 [3,4] 。这类方法可与绕障路径结合形成分层导航，但狭窄处 仍需安排多个身体共同通过。 

搜索方法提前协调路径与占用次序。冲突搜索（ CBS ）逐次添加约束以消除路径 冲突 [22] ，优先级搜索（ PBS ）比较代理间的先后关系 [23] 。图上粒度同时影响几何余 量与求解规模：粗图可能舍弃侧移空间，细图增加搜索和更新成本。安全区间路径规 划（ SIPP ）以无碰撞时间区间表示通行时机 [24] ，运动学与动力学冲突搜索 K-CBS 直接协调连续轨迹 [6] ，扩展了时空选择范围，也增加了共同求解的负担。

施工新增占用会压缩避让方向、改变连接的可行性，并使局部协同范围需要重 建。因此，在线调整还需依据当前净空确定侧向位置与通过次序。当前路线已包含连 通、绕障和前进方向信息；沿路线预测同步身体冲突，可限定共同决策的位置，为按 实时几何构造身体占位选择提供依据。

通行规则还需适应可用几何。社会规则协调利用门的位置、宽度和出入方向安排 离开者优先 [14] ；窄路会车方法 SM-NR 按宽度占用寻找间隙，并选择会车位置与运动 方案 [27] 。道路具有明确纵向，一般二维交会则需结合当前路线确定测宽方向。 

施工净空变化后，原有分区、优先方向和容量均需复核。独立目标带来不同运动 方向，单一容量值不足以确定通过方式：对向需要分离位置，同向可跟随，换侧涉及 接入与离开。减速、改向和等待还须为其他代理保留推进及清出空间；若停留身体封 堵通道，仅延长等待不能疏通。因此，局部可通过性需共同联系净空、身体尺度、相 对运动与时间让行，明确占位间隔、路线接续和身体清出的条件。 

2.3 动态变化中的关系保持与重建 

通过安排形成后，速度、目标和占用仍会改变，规划须兼顾响应新条件与延续未 完成协同。滚动时窗协调方法 RHCR 将持续目标需求分解为有限时域内的冲突求解 

[5] ；窗口末端状态成为下一轮起点，因而当前安排还需考虑后续释放瓶颈空间。 

执行偏差未必要求重建全部关系。固定路径上的同伦协调允许通过暂停或延迟调 整时序 [28] ；可切换动作依赖图（ SADG ）滚动调整动作次序，并以避免循环等待等条 件维持执行 [29] 。分布式协调还可传播运动约束以衔接多任务行程 [30] 。这些方法为保 持有效关系、调整具体运动提供了依据；用于身体交会时，相应依赖仍须落实为共同 可用的通过空间。 

环境更新的对象也有所不同。可配置环境路径规划 C-MAPF 联合选择布局与路线 

[16] ；路径与动作规划 PAPO 协调施工搬运中的宽度、载荷及方向约束 [2] ；施工动态 导航则结合地图、实时感知与多约束规划更新运动 [17] 。主动配置改变布局，感知修 正通行判断，作业启停直接改变占用。 

在施工中，旧避让位置可能失效，并行区可能缩为顺序通行区；新成员、延迟与 改线又改变交会紧迫性。因此，保持依赖须复核其几何依据，更新运动须兼容其他未 完成交会。整体替换可能打断有效安排，无条件沿用则可能保留失效约束。规划需要 明确保持、重建和解除的条件，使新冲突及时进入规划，并在身体清出后释放空间。 

2.4 研究总结与科学问题 

已有研究通过动态避碰、局部求解、空间分离和依赖维护，分别支持安全运动、 受阻恢复与连续执行。作业与运动空间耦合使这些要求同时作用于未完成的交会：新 43 

增占用改变几何依据，改线重新关联参与者，目标与实际进度改变后续次序。因此， 单条路线恢复可行后仍需接续多方通过关系，局部安排还须具有可执行的接入与清出 条件。 

本文据此提出科学问题：变化的受限空间中，局部交会可通过性的构造与持续保 持。 其一，净空、身体尺度和相对运动如何确定相容安排，并为受阻疏通提供空间依 据？其二，空间、成员和运动改变后，哪些关系应保留、重建或解除，如何接续后续 交会？ WP 以实时宽度构造空间相容分配，以锚定路线和紧迫交会更新维持接续；实 验评估通行、持续到达与计算代价。下文先建立空间相容条件，再据此组织规划与多 代理协同。

# 5. 实验与结果 

本章从交会过程、持续到达效率和计算代价三个方面评价 WP 。交会回放与运行 状态统计用于考察目标更新、成员接入及作业占用变化后的运动接续；与四种代表性 方法的比较用于评价完整观察时窗内的目标到达量、行程用时和计算负担。 

5.1 案例与实验场景 

本文以某大厦标准层的楼层布局和施工作业需求构造多代理终身路径规划计算场 景。预制装配式与现浇结构混合体系中，垂直构件的分项作业同时产生运动目标与空 间占用。不同工种的作业人员往返于材料堆场与作业位置，并在相邻构件之间转移。 各人的行程随任务产生，却共同经过入口和连接通道；作业操作空间又压缩邻近通行 范围，使交会位置与可用宽度随施工状态改变。 62

# 6. 讨论 

作业启停改变局部净空，改线又影响后续交会，使当前通过与后续接续相互依 赖 。 局 部 冲 突 求 解 、 宽 度 引 导 、 空 间 分 离 和 依 赖 维 护 已 有 研 究 基 础 [7 –

14,18,25,28,29] 。WP 的创新在于以实际交会需要连接这些处理：在交会处依据实时空 间建立共同通过关系，在相邻交会之间保留贪婪引导的连续路线，随空间和参与者变 化 更 新 受 影 响 安 排 。 相 对 于 以 网 格 位 置 、 分 区 边 界 或 采 样 连 接 组 织 运 动 的 表 达 

[6,18,19] ，这一架构明确了共同约束在何处生成、怎样接续以及何时更新，并可继续 利用区域拓扑和几何路径求解。 

空间相容判据为这种组织方式提供了几何依据。在所述局部空间条件下，定理 1

给出定点换位的充要宽度条件；定理 2 给出组合换位必须覆盖的分离方向，分别指导 通道构造和交会位置迁移。多代理在共享栅格上按式 (34) 协调通过位置，以式 (35) —

(36) 接入各自锚定路线，改线后重新预测，实际运动统一满足式 (1) 。因此，局部空间 条件不仅用于选择一次交会的位置，也参与后续通过关系的保持与重建。 

共同通道同时联系时间让行与受阻疏通：分离位置保留通过空间，让行错开到达 时刻，顺行协调将前方阻挡者递推纳入同向运动。通道轴线上的横向分离由式 (26) 保证，有限步清出依赖式 (38) 的出口接纳与实际进展。两至五人交会及占用变化后的记 录展示了通过和清出的接续；完整 WP 运行中，近停占导航代理步 0.393% ，同一代 理、同一目标行程内最长连续近停为 1.0 s 。这些结果支持完整方法的运动接续表现， 各机制的独立收益尚需区分。 

5—10 代理的完整运行比较中， WP 平均目标到达量较最佳对照提高 25.2% —

52.2% ；更多到达伴随已完成行程平均时长的缩短，二者相互印证。其平均计算耗时低 于 K-CBS 滚动适配，支持 WP 在所测场景中兼顾持续到达与计算代价。这些系统结果 为交会处共同协调与交会间路线自由相结合提供了计算证据。 

本文由此形成的贡献，是从实时可用空间构造局部通过关系、并沿连续运动维护 这些关系的规划原则。几何判据给出构造依据，共享通道与锚点接续使局部安排参与 多方协同，紧迫更新和实际清出决定关系的保持与解除，共同支撑了对作业与运动空 间耦合过程的处理。这为其他兼有任务占用与持续转移需求的工程场景提供了方法依 据。 

# 7. 结论 

本文针对作业与运动空间耦合条件下局部交会可通过性的构造与持续保持，提出 了 WP 方法。创新在于以预测身体冲突组织共同决策，将实时空间相容分配、连续路 线接续与受阻疏通纳入按需生成、持续更新的规划架构。定点换位的充要宽度条件与 组合换位的必要方向条件提供了通道构造和位置迁移的几何依据；共享通道与有序锚 点连接多方交会，紧迫更新保留有效关系并重建受影响部分。由此形成的贡献是以局 部通过关系组织连续群体运动的规划原则，将共同协调与贪婪路线自由相结合，扩展 了终身路径规划对作业占用变化与持续运动需求的联合处理。 

楼层计算实验记录了作业占用变化后的路线接续及多方交会清出，完整 WP 运行 中的近停比例较低、持续时间较短。 5—10 代理的 200 s 完整运行比较中， WP 各配置 平均目标到达量较最佳对照提高 25.2% —52.2% ，相较 K-CBS 滚动适配的平均计算耗 时较低。这些结果为该架构在工程生产场景中的运动协调提供了计算证据；局部疏通 仍受相应几何与执行条件约束，各机制的独立效应及跨场景适用性有待受控检验。 71
