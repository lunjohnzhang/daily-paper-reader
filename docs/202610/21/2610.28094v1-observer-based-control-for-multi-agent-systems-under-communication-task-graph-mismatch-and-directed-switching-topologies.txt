# Observer-Based Control for Multi-Agent Systems under Communication-Task Graph Mismatch and Directed Switching Topologies 

Tommaso Zaccherini, Siyuan Liu, and Dimos V. Dimarogonas 

Abstract —This paper addresses decentralized estimation and control of heterogeneous multi-agent systems communicating over directed switching topologies. The objective is to satisfy co-operative tasks that require coordination among agents according to a time-invariant task graph. To address the potential mismatch between the communication and task graphs, each agent is equipped with a k-hop Prescribed Performance State Observer (k-hop PPSO) to estimate the states of agents up to k communica-tion hops away using only information exchanged with its 1-hop neighbors. A novel prescribed performance funnel construction is developed to account for the switching communication topology, and a sufficient lower bound on the dwell-time is derived to guarantee the prescribed performance specifications under topology variations. Further analysis shows that, if the desired control objective is achieved with exact knowledge of the required nonlocal states and the resulting closed-loop system is uniformly set-ISS with respect to the state estimation error, then replacing these states with their estimates guarantees convergence to a neighborhood of the desired set. The size of this neighborhood can be made arbitrarily small by appropriately selecting the observer performance bounds. A simulation study demonstrates task satisfaction under directed switching topologies. 

Index Terms —Multi-agent systems, Observer-based control, Directed Graphs, Switching Topologies, Communication-task graph mismatch, Decentralized control and estimation. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# THE increasing use of autonomous robotic systems in demanding and large-scale environments has motivated a shift from centralized to distributed architectures, where multiple agents coordinate using locally available information. Compared with single-agent systems, multi-agent systems (MAS) exploit distributed sensing, computation, and actuation to accomplish complex tasks cooperatively, with applications including formation maintenance [1], distributed sensing [2], and autonomous exploration [3]. Distributed MAS control typically assumes that the in-formation required by each agent is available from its communication neighbors [4]–[8]. Task-decomposition methods adapt tasks to the communi-cation structure, but may require additional optimization or apply only to distance-based tasks [9]–[11]. Alternatively, information-reconstruction methods exploit distributed sens-ing and state estimation to recover unavailable information from spatially distributed measurements [12]–[14]. The re-sulting estimates support observer-based controllers for dis-tributed coordination objectives [15]–[24]. However, many information-reconstruction methods are developed for linear or single-double integrator dynamics [12], [16], [22], [23], and nonlinear observer-based control approaches are often tailored to specific objectives such as consensus [17], [24], leader-following [23], or formation control [20]. These limitations leave a gap in addressing general task-dependent coordination of heterogeneous nonlinear MAS, where the behaviors of multiple agents may be coupled through nonlocal information dependencies. To accommodate nonlocal information dependencies with-out task decomposition, our recent work [25]–[27] intro-duced distributed and decentralized k-hop Prescribed Per-formance Observers (k-hop DPPOs) for nonlinear MAS. These observers enable each agent to estimate the states of non-neighboring agents that are two or more hops away using only 1-hop communication, while guaranteeing prescribed bounds on the resulting state estimation errors. The above results, however, rely on a time-invariant, undirected commu-nication topology, under which the communication paths used to propagate the k-hop estimates remain fixed and bidirec-tional. This assumption limits the applicability of the k-hop DPPOs to communication networks in which links are fixed and information exchange is symmetric. In more general MAS, communication links may instead be asymmetric and vary over time, resulting in directed and time-varying topologies. To reconstruct nonlocal states despite topology switching and bounded disturbances, this paper develops a decentralized 

k-hop prescribed performance state observer ( k-hop PPSO) for heterogeneous MAS over directed and switching communica-tion graphs. As in the undirected case, the proposed observer enables each agent to estimate the states of agents located two to k hops away, while keeping the estimation errors within predefined performance bounds specified at the design stage. Directed communication graphs, however, generally have nonsymmetric Laplacian matrices, which fundamentally arXiv:2610.28094v1 [eess.SY] 20 Oct 2026 2

change the structural properties of the error dynamics used to establish prescribed-performance guarantees. Moreover, topol-ogy switching changes the communication paths along which information propagates, resulting in time-varying k-hop neigh-borhoods. As a result, agents may join or leave a given agent’s 

k-hop neighborhood as the communication topology switches, causing the set of estimated agents to change over time. Since the estimation vector is defined over this set of agents, its dimension changes accordingly, resembling the behavior of Open Multi-Agent Systems (OMAS). Thus, in order to define the vector of estimates consistently as agents enter and leave the estimated set, we draw inspiration from the OMAS literature [28]. Beyond the observer design, we establish the applicability of the proposed observer to general control objectives through a set-ISS characterization of the closed-loop system. Specifi-cally, we show that if the closed-loop dynamics are uniformly set-ISS with respect to the state estimation error, replacing nonlocal states with their estimates preserves convergence to a neighborhood of the desired set, whose size can be made arbitrarily small through an appropriate choice of the observer performance bounds. To the best of our knowledge, this work presents the first decentralized k-hop Prescribed Performance State Ob-server and associated observer-based control framework for heterogeneous MAS over directed switching communication topologies, with applicability to general control objectives. The contribution of the paper is summarized as follows:  

> •

Compared with the observer-based approaches in [17], [23], [24], which target specific coordination objectives, the proposed observer is independent of the particular task. It also explicitly prescribes the transient perfor-mance of the state estimation error, yielding a priori bound on the worst-case state estimation error at every time instant. This generality is particularly relevant for applications in which task satisfaction depends on guar-anteed bounds on the estimation uncertainty throughout the transient, as in [27].  

> •

Unlike [20], which considers formation control based on a prescribed-performance observer for single-integrator MAS communicating over an undirected time-invariant graph, the proposed k-hop PPSO is developed for hetero-geneous MAS under directed switching communication topologies. Moreover, rather than reconstructing aggre-gate formation information through a dynamic average consensus scheme, the proposed k-hop PPSO reconstructs the states of nonlocal agents required by the task. The choice of k determines the maximum communication-hop distance over which states can be reconstructed, allowing the observer to support task dependencies beyond each agent’s immediate communication neighborhood.  

> •

A novel prescribed-performance funnel construction is developed to account for topology switching in the 

k-hop state observer. Unlike existing PPC approaches for switched systems [29], [30], which either relax the pre-scribed funnels online at switching instants or construct an over-approximating funnel a posteriori, the proposed method constructs auxiliary funnels online based on the worst-case variation of the k-hop neighborhood. Under suitable feasibility and minimum dwell-time conditions, these funnels ensure that the original prescribed per-formance specifications remain satisfied across topology switches. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion II introduces the notation, preliminaries, and problem for-mulation. Section III defines the disagreement vectors among the agents’ estimates and derives their dynamics. Section IV presents the proposed k-hop PPSO and the conditions under which the original prescribed performances are satisfied under switching topologies. Section V presents the feedback control structure and establishes the conditions under which the k-hop estimation-based feedback controller guarantees convergence to the team objective. Section VI demonstrates the effective-ness of the proposed approach through simulation results, and Section VII concludes the paper with final remarks and directions for future work. II. IV. P RESCRIBED PERFORMANCE OBSERVER UNDER SWITCHING TOPOLOGIES 

As discussed in Section II-C, changes in the dimension of the state estimation error vector ˜xi may introduce discontinu-ous jumps at switching instants. Consequently, the estimation-error dynamics become hybrid, requiring the estimation errors to be analyzed both during continuous evolution and across switching instants. This section establishes conditions ensuring that the estimation errors preserve the desired prescribed performance guarantees despite the switching communication topology. 

A. Prescribed-performance function design 

Although the prescribed performance functions introduced in Section II-D guarantee the desired transient and steady-state behavior during continuous evolution, switching instants may introduce discontinuities in the estimation errors. defined in (7), thereby violating the prescribed performance constraints in (10) immediately after a switch. This paper develops a decentralized k-hop PPSO for het-erogeneous multi-agent systems communicating over directed switching topologies. The proposed observer enables the re-construction of nonlocal agent states using only 1-hop com-munication while guaranteeing predefined transient bounds on the state estimation errors. To account for topology switch-ing, a novel prescribed performance funnel construction is introduced, and feasibility conditions and a lower bound on the dwell-time are derived to preserve the original prescribed performance specifications across topology switches. Further-more, a set-ISS analysis establishes that, for control objectives achieved with exact nonlocal state information, replacing these states with their estimates preserves convergence to a neigh-borhood of the desired set, whose size can be made arbitrarily small through the observer performance bounds. Future work will focus on characterizing the topological properties of the communication graph that enable an edge-selection mechanism to dynamically reconfigure the commu-nication topology so as to satisfy the prescribed-performance constraints. Furthermore, we will investigate conditions guar-anteeing observer and control convergence under intermittent loss of communication paths required to estimate nonlocal states. APPENDIX
