# Dispersive Tree Agreements for Multi-Robot Coordination 

Shashank A. Deshpande, Blake T. Edwards, and Jonathan P. How 

Abstract —Multi-robot coordination of nonlinear systems is naturally formulated as joint-space optimal control subject to constraints over groups of robots. Existing methods that approach joint optimality remain largely centralized, offline, and confined to collision-free motion planning, while decentralized, real-time counterparts target safe navigation without pursuing the joint optimum. This paper develops a decentralized, real-time planner that approaches the joint optimum as an anytime process. We extend a recent single-robot framework that searches dispersive trees of forward-propagated commands for differentially flat systems to coordinate a multi-robot team near-optimally. We first show that the inter-robot coupling can be effectively replaced by some local constraints, under which locally grown dispersive trees contain a near-optimal agreement. It is this structure that the robots pursue by decentralized column generation, negotiating rents on the shared space that guide the search of each robot’s tree. The negotiation settles on an agreement whose near-optimality is certified at quiescence. Our protocol, WarpWeaver, implements this negotiation as an asynchronous real-time loop over a receding horizon, and demonstrates effective closure to quiesced agreements even when real-time deadlines cut the negotiation short. In simulation, WarpWeaver reduces total travel time by up to 22% 

against centralized offline planners on the Dynobench suite and by up to 37% against a decentralized real-time baseline, and nearly doubles the success rate of a task-specific planner on graph connectivity. Hardware experiments on six robots planning onboard demonstrate real-world implementability. 

Index Terms —Multi-robot systems, path planning for multiple mobile robots or agents, distributed robot systems, optimization and optimal control, planning, scheduling and coordination. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

This paper addresses the coordination of a team of robots with differentially flat nonlinear dynamics, formulated as optimal control in the joint space of the team subject to constraints over groups of robots. We seek control inputs whose cost lies near the joint optimum, via distributed computation across the robots in real time, to maintain robustness to changes in the individual objectives of the robots as well as in the dynamics of the environment. Existing approaches to this problem divide broadly into centralized, offline planners with guarantees of asymptotic optimality, and real-time decentralized planners that work with simplified dynamics, without pursuing the joint optimum. A range of centralized planners derive from multi-agent path finding [1] and handle pairwise conflicts in a high-level search on top of low-level single-robot planners running model predictive control [2], sampling-based planning [3], or search

These planners provide asymptotic guarantees of completeness or optimality, and focus on motion planning with pairwise separation. Decentralized planning, on the other hand, comes with real-time advantages such as robustness to changes in the individual objectives or in the environment. Decentralized trajectory optimization involves high-frequency replanning of safe splines around the incumbents communicated by peer robots, while often simplifying the robots’ dynamics [9–14]. Consensus-based distributed optimization instead lets the robots negotiate, with gradient-based local optimization driven by penalties on mutual disagreement [15, 16]. Coordination across these methods, however, often settles in local optima, without pursuing the joint optimum. In summary, real-time multi-robot coordination deals with multiple sources of complexity. First, planning and control at the level of a single robot must respect the nonlinear dynamics of its control system. In addition, these control trajectories must coordinate to satisfy various constraints over groups of robots while evading the many local minima of the joint problem. Finally, the algorithm that addresses these challenges must arXiv:2610.31540v1 [cs.RO] 22 Oct 2026 2

decentralize across the robots under limited communication and maintain robustness to communication delays within the team. While existing approaches handle this complexity in part, this paper develops a decentralized framework that tackles each of its sources in unison. We employ dispersive forward tree search [17], which finds near-optimal control inputs for differentially flat systems, for single-robot planning and control. To solve coordination, we first show that the coupling among the robots can be absorbed into suitable constraints on each robot alone, so that tractably sized dispersive trees grown locally by the robots jointly contain a near-optimal agreement. It is this structure that the remainder of our development pursues. We formulate the selection of this joint agreement as an integer linear program and approach its solution by column generation [18, 19], in which the dual of its linear relaxation sets rents on the shared space. Under these rents, the search for a new column becomes a single-robot problem that each robot solves near-optimally with its own dispersive forward tree. Notably, this column generation procedure is effectively decentralized, to distribute the computational load of the process across the robots while limiting communication within the team to a manageable amount. We show that the resulting process implements an anytime negotiation whose agreement is certified near-optimal once it reaches quiescence, up to the resolution of the search and a gap that the agents compute online. This development culminates in WarpWeaver, the real-time multi-robot planner that we propose, which runs the negotiation asynchronously across the team over a receding horizon and remains robust to bounded communication delays. Our experiments thereby demonstrate that this negotiation, driven by decentralized column generation, is a pattern well suited to real-time multi-robot coordination, as the agreements that it commits within real-time deadlines come close in cost to those that it reaches when given time to quiesce. In summary, this paper makes the following contributions. 

• Joint optimal control as distributed dispersive forward search. We cast a broad class of multi-robot coordination problems as joint-space optimal control of differentially flat nonlinear systems, and prove that suitably constrained, locally grown dispersive forward trees contain a near-optimal agree-ment (Proposition 1). 

• Decentralized column generation. We pursue this structure by decentralized column generation under limited communica-tion, in which rents on the shared space guide the tree search of each robot, and prove that the negotiated agreement is certified near-optimal at quiescence, relative to the joint solutions robust at the search resolution (Proposition 4 and Theorem 2). 

• Real-time protocol. We develop WarpWeaver, an asyn-chronous protocol that runs decentralized column generation over a receding horizon onboard each robot, to coordinate the team in real time under bounded communication delays, whose agreements within real-time deadlines come close in cost to quiesced ones. 

• Evaluation and hardware experiments. We benchmark WarpWeaver in simulation against centralized offline, decen-tralized real-time, and task-specific planners. It reduces total travel time by up to 22% against centralized offline planners on Dynobench and by up to 37% against a decentralized real-time baseline. Further, on a graph connectivity task, it nearly doubles the success rate of a task-specific planner. We further deploy it on a team of six robots, each planning onboard in real time. In this section, we present a brief review of existing literature on multi-robot planning and control, along with related techniques relevant to our work. 

A. Sampling and Search for Multi-Robot Motion Planning 

Sampling-based planning extends to multi-robot motion plan-ning in the composite configuration space of the team [7, 8]. For geometric paths, search over the tensor product of individual roadmaps has been shown to be asymptotically optimal [8], and near-optimal at a finite sample size [20]. While it is unclear how this decomposition carries over to robots with continuous dynamics, kinodynamic tree search in the composite state space has been shown to be probabilistically complete [21]. Decentralized implementations of composite search have also been demonstrated, with robots replanning in turn by merit-based token passing [22] or exchanging probability distributions over their plans [23]. Kinodynamic extensions of multi-agent path finding on graphs [1] retain its two-level structure, a high-level search that coordinates the team on top of low-level local planners. The low-level graph search is then replaced by a range of methods spanning sampling-based tree search [3], model predictive control [2], and search over graphs of motion primitives joined up to a bounded discontinuity [4–6]. These implementations are predominantly centralized and offline, and guarantee solution properties ranging from probabilistic or resolution completeness to asymptotic optimality. 

B. Trajectory Optimization for Multi-Robot Motion Planning 

Centralized trajectory optimization coordinates the team within a single program [24, 25], whose size grows with the number of robots. Scalable variants involve a discrete coordi-nation layer, such as priority-based search over mixed-integer programs for tracked reference paths [26] and multi-agent path finding over Bézier curves [27]. Decentralized trajectory optimization segregates the program across the robots, each of which replans a trajectory around the incumbent commitments of its peers. These commitments enter as collision penalties in gradient-based optimization [9], or as separating constraints 3

robust to imperfect communication [10, 11]. Distributed model predictive control [12, 13] follows a similar pattern over short horizons, with linear safe corridors [14] preserving feasibility. These planners run in real time at scale, typically on simplified models of the dynamics. Instead of iterative replanning around incumbent commit-ments, consensus-based distributed optimization lets the robots negotiate. Each robot keeps local copies of the shared variables and optimizes them under its nonlinear dynamics. Penalties on mutual disagreement, updated by the alternating direction method of multipliers, drive the team toward agreement on these variables [15, 16]. 

C. Coordination Beyond Inter-Robot Separation 

Several tasks of utility constrain groups of robots beyond pairwise separation, such as maintaining a connected commu-nication network [28] or keeping robots tethered to each other. Existing work typically addresses these tasks by deploying dedicated mechanisms. For instance, connectivity maintenance is commonly enforced by potential functions that keep the algebraic connectivity of the communication graph positive [28], a design that Realm [29, 30] extends to line-of-sight constraints derived in real time from onboard point clouds. Our method is able to address coordination beyond collision avoidance by specification of hard constraints on the joint-space dynamics, as we demonstrate by implementing connectivity maintenance in simulation and tethering in hardware experiments. 

D. Column Generation and Decentralized Pricing 

Column generation is a canonical technique for solving large linear programs by generating their variables on demand [18, 19]. Dual prices from a restricted master program guide the search for new columns, with a Lagrangian bound on the gap [31] and branching for integrality [32]. This technique has been applied to multi-agent path finding on graphs by branch-and-cut-and-price [33], and to multi-robot routing over space-time positions with pricing by resource-constrained shortest paths [34]. Column generation has been shown to distribute effectively across robots, with local pricing and an exchange of columns among neighbors, for task assignment [35]. We formulate joint optimal control as a search over the dispersive forward trees of the robots [17] and distribute this search across the team via column generation, running asynchronously in real time with a certificate of near-optimality at quiescence, relative to the joint solutions that are robust at the search resolution. V. D ISPERSIVE TREE AGREEMENTS FOR NEAR -O PTIMAL 

COORDINATION 

In this section, we develop Dispersive Tree Agreements and the protocol that negotiates them, WarpWeaver. WarpWeaver, as implemented, provides a decentralized anytime approach to a near-optimal solution of the JOCP (11). We begin with an overview of our mathematical results, followed by their presentation and the protocol as implemented. We defer the proofs of these results to Appendix A. 

A. Overview 

Our presentation begins with an existence result in Sec-tion V-B, in which we show that there exists a near-optimal 

agreement on agent-local dispersive trees, each pruned at a resolution that keeps its growth computation tractable. This result motivates the development of our decentralized protocol. We begin this development in Section V-C by restricting the problem of finding an optimal agreement to an integer linear program (ILP), over the command sequence sets ATi of all agents, at a chosen resolution of space and time. Subsequently, in Section V-D, we develop our solution scheme for the ILP based on column generation. We show that the search for a near-optimal agreement can be decomposed into an iterative loop that involves solving the linear relaxation of the ILP restricted to a small pool of the agents’ command sequences, or columns , and a decentralized column generation procedure that enlarges the pool until no agent finds an im-proving column. We prove that, whenever this process reaches quiescence, the agents select from the pool an agreement that is near-optimal relative to the solutions of the JOCP that are robust at the search resolution, up to a gap that the agents compute online.

D. Decentralized Column Generation 

As formulated, the ILP (21) holds a binary decision variable 

λi,c for each command sequence c of each agent i, and is thus of enormous size. Column generation [18, 19, 32] is the canonical method to address integer programs with too many variables to enumerate. The procedure involves solving the linear relaxation of the ILP over a small pool of variables, or 

columns , and enlarging the pool with columns that improve the relaxation under prices derived from its dual. For multi-robot coordination problems with our structure, we show that column generation can be effectively decentral-ized across the agents due to the separable structure of the corresponding ILP, in which the agents interact only through the aggregator and the joint predicates. This allows each agent to solve a local problem akin to the single-agent problem (15), priced by its interactions with its peers, on its onboard computer. E. Protocol and Real-Time Implementation 

We now present the WarpWeaver protocol, which implements decentralized column generation among the agents as a real-time negotiation process in a receding-horizon manner. In Algorithm 1, we state the negotiation from the perspective of agent i, and Fig. 3 depicts the same. Each agent runs an identical loop onboard and communicates by broadcasting generated columns as well as endorsing joint solutions over the joint pool. In Algorithm 1, a joint solution is globally endorsed 

once every agent has published or endorsed it, and the best such solution has the least cost Φ, with ties broken by a common rule. An iteration is quiescent once all agents hold the same pool Q,compute ˆJi ≥ 0 under the common optimal solution of DP( Q),and endorse a common solution of JCP( Q). Since quiescence cannot be guaranteed within the real-time deadlines, the agents commit at the pick time tn to the best globally endorsed solution, whose cost is certified by Proposition 5(ii) below only if the iteration is quiescent. An agent sends no message after time tn − δcomm , even within a pass of the loop begun before it. The pick margin τ leaves time to commit before the committed windows begin at time (n + 1) ∆ s. VIII. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we formulated a broad class of multi-robot coordination problems as joint-space optimal control subject to constraints over groups of robots, and extended a recent single-robot framework of dispersive forward tree search to approach its joint optimum. We showed that suitable constraints on each robot can effectively replace the inter-robot coupling, so that dispersive forward trees grown locally by the robots jointly contain a near-optimal agreement. This structure was pursued by decentralized column generation, in which the robots search their trees under common prices and negotiate an agreement certified near-optimal at quiescence. This de-velopment culminated in WarpWeaver, a real-time protocol that runs the negotiation asynchronously over a receding horizon onboard each robot. In simulation, WarpWeaver was shown to match or outperform state-of-the-art centralized offline, decentralized real-time, and task-specific planners, with total travel times up to 22% on Dynobench and 37% lower than the former two and nearly double the success rate of the latter. Notably, we find that WarpWeaver demonstrates effective closure to quiesced agreements even when real-time deadlines cut the negotiation short, implying that decentralized column generation is well suited for real-time multi-robot coordination. In real-robot experiments, we used a team of six unicycle robots to demonstrate its real-time implementability for kinodynamic planning around obstacles and through a doorway, and additionally with pairs of robots tethered to each other. A limitation of our approach is that a coordination task is only as tractable as its integer linear restriction. Constraints that tie many robots tightly together, such as the maintenance of a rigid formation, admit few joint solutions among the individual trees and yield programs whose relaxations are weak, where differential methods that exploit the local geometry of the constraint may be better suited. Incorporating such differential information into dispersive search remains a direction for future research. 15

Both parts follow iteration n of Algorithm 1 line by line. (i) Completeness and consistency. Initialization (line 3): Every agent initializes its pool with the initial agreement, which every agent endorses by (c). The initial agreement is thus globally endorsed at time tn.

tn − δcomm . By (a), every such message reaches every agent before time tn.
