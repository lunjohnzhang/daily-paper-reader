# Consistent Distributed Invariant EKF SLAM with Point and Object Features 

Mengya Xu, Xianjia Yu, Ha Sier, Shoudong Huang, Tomi Westerlund 

Abstract — Multi-robot SLAM allows robots to improve their estimates by exchanging map information, but repeated ex-changes create unknown correlations between local estimators. Covariance intersection (CI) can fuse estimates without track-ing these correlations. In right-invariant extended Kalman filter (RI-EKF) SLAM, however, point features obtain their invariant errors through the robot rotation, so a neighbor’s map estimate is expressed in its own error coordinates and is correlated with its pose, and cannot be fused by CI directly. This paper presents Joint-CI, a unified fusion method for distributed RI-EKF SLAM with point, object, and mixed point–object maps. Using a neighboring robot’s local posterior and an inter-robot observation, a joint estimate of the receiving robot’s pose and the common features is constructed in the receiving robot’s error coordinates, retaining the robot–feature and feature– feature correlations. Partial-state CI then fuses this estimate with the receiving robot’s local posterior. We show that the unified formulation and the fusion preserve the six unobserv-able directions associated with global rotation and translation, and that communication and fusion preserve first-order co-variance consistency given consistent local posteriors. Monte Carlo simulations and a real-world dataset-derived experiment show that Joint-CI improves estimation accuracy while main-taining consistent, mildly conservative uncertainty. The code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/ Joint-CI-for-Distributed-RI-EKF-SLAM-E14B/ .

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multi-robot SLAM improves coverage and estimation ac-curacy by allowing robots to exchange locally acquired map information. Representative systems such as DDF-SAM [1], Kimera-Multi [2], and Swarm-SLAM [3] employ distributed or decentralized nonlinear optimization over factor-graph or pose-graph representations. These approaches provide powerful batch or incremental optimization capabilities, but repeatedly solving a growing optimization problem can im-pose computational and latency costs on lightweight robotic platforms. Filtering-based methods provide a complementary solution by recursively updating the current posterior as new measurements and communicated estimates arrive. Point features are generic and efficient to detect and match, and have therefore been widely used in both single-robot and multi-robot SLAM. Advances in machine learning have also increased interest in high-level features such as objects, which provide persistent semantic identities and strong geometric constraints [4], [5], [6], [7]. Practical sys-tems have further demonstrated the value of mixed map representations, in which low-level point features and high-level object features are maintained together [8], [9], [10]. For filtering-based SLAM, estimation consistency is essential because underestimated uncertainty can degrade both state estimation and uncertainty-aware downstream decisions [11]. Conventional EKF SLAM can become inconsistent because linearization at the estimated states may introduce spurious information along unobservable directions [12], [13], [14]. Right-invariant EKF (RI-EKF) formulations address this is-sue by defining the estimation error through the symmetry of the state space, which preserves the appropriate unobservable directions. They have been developed for both point-feature SLAM [15], [16] and object-feature SLAM [17]. Distributed filtering introduces an additional difficulty because previous information exchange creates generally unknown cross-correlations between local estimators. Co-variance intersection (CI) [18] provides a conservative fusion rule without explicitly tracking these correlations and has been applied to SLAM [19] and decentralized cooperative localization [20], [21]. Partial-state CI extends this idea to estimates containing different or overlapping subsets of a common state [22], [23], [24]. Distributed invariant filters have also applied CI to pose estimation on Lie groups [25], [26], without estimating a shared map. For object-level multi-robot SLAM, Li et al. [27] use per-object invariant errors, representing each object as an independent SE (3) 

component, so that neighboring robot poses and common object estimates can be combined separately. Despite these advances, fusing map information between RI-EKF SLAM estimators remains nontrivial. Exchanging only pose estimates discards the information contained in the shared map, and the per-object invariant errors in [27] rely on object orientations and therefore do not directly cover point features. In RI-EKF SLAM, point features, which carry no orientation of their own, obtain their invariant errors through the robot rotation [15], [16], and the object formulation in [17] adopts the same coupling, allowing point, object, and mixed maps to share one error representation. In this coupled formulation, exchanging map features raises two issues. First, each robot’s feature errors are defined relative to its own rotation error, so a neighbor’s feature covariance cannot be combined directly with the receiving robot’s posterior. Second, a neighbor’s pose and feature errors are correlated through its own SLAM estimation, and in the coupled formulation they additionally share the same rotation error. A communicated covariance that ignores these correlations is not guaranteed to be consistent. This paper presents Joint-CI, a single construction that ad-dresses both issues for distributed RI-EKF SLAM with point, object, and mixed point–object maps. Joint-CI re-expresses the communicated robot pose and common features in the receiving filter’s error coordinates as one correlated partial state, derives its complete first-order covariance from the neighboring posterior and the inter-robot observation, and then applies standard partial-state CI. The main contributions arXiv:2610.31221v1 [cs.RO] 22 Oct 2026 are: 1) We derive a unified construction of the communicated robot–feature partial state and its complete first-order co-variance in error coordinates compatible with the receiving filter, for point, object, and mixed maps. 2) We show that the unified local RI-EKF formulation and the subsequent fusion preserve the global gauge directions. Given consistent local posteriors, we further establish first-order covariance consistency of the communicated estimate and subsequent CI fusion. 3) We evaluate estimation accuracy, statistical consistency, communication and computational costs, and operation in multi-neighbor networks through Monte Carlo simulations and a real-world dataset-derived experiment. A. Experimental setup 

The three scenarios in Fig. 1 examine complementary operating conditions. The General scenario uses two robots to provide a controlled comparison across map types under intermittent communication. The High-overlap scenario tests whether consistency is maintained when frequent communi-cation and extensive shared map information cause repeated information reuse. The Five-robot scenario provides a more complex and diverse communication setting to test whether the fusion construction remains effective in a multi-neighbor network. An additional real-world dataset-derived scenario uses Sequence 7 of the YCB-Video dataset [28] to evaluate the same backend using recorded camera motion and object geometry, as illustrated in Fig. 2. All three simulation scenarios contain 50 features in a 

100 m × 100 m environment, with each robot following a 2000-time-step trajectory. In mixed maps, a fixed randomly selected half of the features are represented as objects and the remainder as points. A 30 m feature-observation range and a 20 m communication range are used throughout. The common noise covariances are Φ = diag(0 .02 2I3, 0.52I3),

Ψq = 0 .52I3, Ψo = 0 .52I6, and Ψkl = 0 .05 2I6, with rotational and translational components expressed in radians and meters, respectively. Two robots within communication range reciprocally exchange messages, using independent inter-robot observations in the two directions. Both updates at a communication event are computed from the pre-communication posteriors, preventing immediate reuse of information within that event. To avoid redundant configurations, the High-overlap and Five-robot scenarios use the mixed map as a representative of the unified formulation, which reduces to the point-only case when mo = 0 and the object-only case when mp = 0 .The object map is retained separately to enable comparison with the object-specific baseline. 

1) Compared methods: The comparison includes five methods: 1) Local runs the coupled RI-EKF SLAM estimator without inter-robot fusion. 2) Pose-CI communicates and fuses only the virtual pose of the receiving robot using partial-state CI. 3) Joint-CI (ours) communicates the virtual pose and common features together with their complete covariance in compatible coordinates. 4) DInCIKFc-C is our controlled implementation of the object-level distributed invariant filter DInCIKFc proposed in [27]. 5) DIn-Local uses the local estimator underlying DInCIKFc-C without inter-robot fusion. DInCIKFc-C retains the state representation and fusion structure of DInCIKFc [27], in which the communicated covariance is block-diagonal, assembled from separately constructed robot and object blocks. For a paired comparison, it uses the same ground truth, observation availability, and physical noise realizations as Joint-CI. Noise covariances are mapped into the respective tangent coordinates using the corresponding first-order Jacobians. Historical accumulation of object estimates used in [27] is disabled so that all fusion methods process the same communication events, separating the state and covariance constructions from fusion schedul-ing. Because Joint-CI and DInCIKFc-C also use different local estimators, DIn-Local is included to isolate the effect of inter-robot fusion within DInCIKFc-C. 

2) Evaluation criteria: For each scenario and map type, 

100 Monte Carlo trials are conducted. Within each trial, all methods use the same ground truth, measurements, noise realizations, visibility sets, and communication events. The evaluation considers estimation accuracy, statistical consis-tency, communication payload, and fusion time. Root-mean-square error (RMSE) measures the accuracy of the robot and the applicable feature components. The normalized estimation error squared (NEES) and 95% cov-erage assess statistical consistency. NEES is computed in each estimator’s own error coordinates. A value close to one indicates that the reported covariance agrees with the observed estimation error. Joint-CI achieves this consistency by communicating the complete covariance of the correlated robot–feature partial state, whose payload grows quadratically with the number of common features but is independent of the local map size. Since any principal submatrix of a consistent covariance remains consistent (the Loewner order is preserved under congruence), any subset of common features can be selected while preserving first-order covariance consistency, with Pose-CI as the special case of the empty subset. This enables payload reduction through feature selection. The fusion time of Joint-CI remains comparable to that of the baselines.

2) Results: Table II evaluates the proposed construction in multi-neighbor settings. Across the Five-robot and dataset-derived experiments, Joint-CI reduces the average RMSE relative to Local while maintaining consistent, mildly conser-vative uncertainty estimates. Pose-CI increases the average RMSE while its uncertainty remains conservative, indicating a loss of information rather than overconfidence. A plausible cause is that each pose-only fusion discounts the entire local posterior, including the map, whereas new information enters only the pose. DInCIKFc-C achieves larger RMSE reductions relative to DIn-Local, but its larger NEES and lower coverage indicate overconfident uncertainty estimates. In the Five-robot experiment, Joint-FastCI closely matches direct Joint-CI, showing that the compatible joint messages also support two-stage neighbor aggregation. For the mixed map, Joint-CI exhibits the same overall behavior, supporting the same construction when point and object features are represented in a unified state. The main cost of Joint-CI is its larger communication payload. VI. C ONCLUSION 

This paper presented Joint-CI for distributed RI-EKF SLAM with point, object, and mixed feature maps. A direct error mapping constructs a correlated robot–feature estimate with a covariance compatible with the receiving filter, after which standard partial-state CI can be applied without track-ing inter-robot correlations. The analysis establishes preser-vation of the gauge directions and first-order covariance con-sistency of communication and fusion given consistent local inputs.
