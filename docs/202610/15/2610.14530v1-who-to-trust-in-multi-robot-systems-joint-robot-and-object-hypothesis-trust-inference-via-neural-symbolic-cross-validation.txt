# Who to Trust in Multi-Robot Systems: Joint Robot and Object Hypothesis Trust Inference via Neural-Symbolic Cross-Validation 

Ruiyang Wang, Hao-Lun Hsu, Haocheng Meng, Songqiao Xie and Miroslav Pajic 

Abstract —Multi-robot systems (MRS) offer significant ad-vantages over single-robot platforms by enabling information sharing and coordination for different tasks in complex en-vironments. However, reliable cooperation critically depends on inferring trust over both information sources and their reported hypotheses, particularly under partial observability and potential adversarial interference. Existing approaches frequently depend on pre-established trust relationships or side-channel information, which may not be available or reliable in realistic MRS deployments. This work introduces NeST-Bayes , a neural-symbolic framework that jointly infers robot and hypothesis trust using only inter-robot observation consistency, without requiring preassigned robot trust, reputation information, or side-channel identity signals. NeST-Bayes infers instantaneous trust evidence scores for robots and object hypotheses by reasoning over ego-centric relational graphs constructed from cross-robot observa-tion consistency. These evidence scores are accumulated over time through Bayesian updates of Beta-distributed trust beliefs to maintain temporally consistent trust estimates. Extensive evaluations show that NeST-Bayes achieves the highest mean object precision and robot recall across all benchmark settings, and the highest robot precision and object recall in most settings, compared to trust-based methods. Under aggressive optimized attacks, it achieves 98.8%/93.8% robot precision/recall and 98.3%/95.4% object precision/recall. In an occlusion-rich Webots warehouse, it maintains 92.5%/92.0% robot precision/recall and 97.3%/85.0% object precision/recall. This shows joint improve-ments in the reliability and coverage of retained information. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

By enabling coordinated decision-making and information exchange among multiple robots, multi-robot systems (MRS) achieve performance gains over single-robot platforms in per-ception, exploration, coverage, and planning. Seminal works [1], [2] established the value of cooperation in distributed robot teams, while more recent efforts improved perceptual robust-ness through multi-view sensing [3] and enhanced search effi-ciency via information sharing and coordinated planning [4]. While multi-viewpoint aggregation overcomes individual sensing limitations, real-world MRS must operate under partial observability and sensing uncertainty [5], unreliable commu-nication [6], and restricted fields of view [7]. More critically, shared estimation and planning become vulnerable in con-tested environments and scenarios, where adversarial robots may inject/utilize misleading information; e.g., Sybil attacks distort collective objectives through false identities [8], and Byzantine behaviors corrupt distributed diffusion and state estimation [9], [10]. Any mechanism aggregating inter-robot information should therefore infer trust over both the robots providing reports and the hypotheses they produce. Existing trust inference approaches in MRS typically rely on assumptions that limit their applicability in realistic settings. Early works [11]–[13] treat trust as a pre-established or reputation-based quantity, while more adaptive methods [14], [15] infer it from hand-designed models or specifications of nominal behavior. Recent resilient hypothesis-testing meth-ods [16], [17] exploit side-channel information that is often unavailable. Complementary sensor-fusion approaches reject inconsistent measurements without explicitly estimating the reliability of their sources [18], [19]. Bayesian methods such as MATE [20] already use observation consistency to jointly estimate agent and track trust, with each estimate informing the other. However, their mappings from consistency checks to trust evidence rely on hand-designed rules. A critical limitation is the lack of methods to learn how typed consistency and contradiction relations can be combined, to infer robot and object hypothesis trust under potential adversarial reporting. Consequently, in this work, we introduce NeST-Bayes , a Neural Symbolic Trust inference framework that learns rela-tional evidence to jointly estimate robot and object-hypothesis trust from cross-robot observation consistency, with Bayesian temporal evidence accumulation through updates to Beta-distributed trust beliefs. For each robot, we construct an ego-centric heterogeneous graph whose typed edges encode obser-vation agreement and contradiction using geometric and field-of-view (FoV) checks. A triplet encoder initializes node repre-sentations from their symbolic relation profiles, and relation-specific graph attention propagates evidence across robot and hypothesis nodes to produce instantaneous trust scores. These scores are accumulated as fractional pseudo-counts in Beta distributions to maintain temporal trust beliefs. 

NeST-Bayes requires no preassigned robot trust or side-channel identity signals at inference time. Evaluations under noisy sensing, normal and optimized adversarial reporting, increased adversarial ratios, and occlusion-rich Webots simula-tions show that NeST-Bayes generally achieves the highest pre-cision while also leading in recall among trust-based methods. Component ablation studies show that relational propagation improves recall while maintaining high precision. In summary, the main contributions of this work include:  

> •

A neural-symbolic trust inference framework that com-bines symbolic relation profiles with heterogeneous graph attention to jointly infer robot and object-hypothesis evidence, integrating the learned scores with Bayesian temporal evidence accumulation.  

> •

An extensive evaluation against No Trust , Na¨ ıve Bayesian , and MATE baselines under sensing noise, normal and optimized adversarial reporting, increased adversarial ratios, and occlusion-rich Webots simulations, together with component ablations demonstrating how symbolic relation initialization and typed relational prop-agation contribute to precision and recall. arXiv:2610.14530v1 [cs.RO] 10 Oct 2026 II. R ELATED WORK 

A. MRS Vulnerabilities 

MRS are sensitive to sensing noise and partial observability [21], communication uncertainty [22], [23], and malicious interference [24]. False data injection can destabilize co-ordination and navigation [25], and adversarial perception attacks inject spurious measurements that disrupt coopera-tive decision-making [19]. Byzantine behaviors in distributed estimation and learning have also been studied [26], [27], where even a few compromised robots degrade performance by injecting false positives or omitting valid observations. These results motivate principled trust mechanisms that operate under limited information and unreliable sensing, without assuming nominal-mode behavior. 

B. Trust in MRS 

Trust estimation in MRS remains an open problem. Exist-ing approaches derive trust evidence from explicitly defined behaviors or task specifications [15], [28], or exploit side-channel information that effectively fingerprints the robot identity [16], [17]. More recent methods [20], [29], [30] cross-validate observations across robots, but rely on hand-designed rules for trust updates that, as we show, can be brittle when adversaries are numerous or cross-robot validation is sparse. NeST-Bayes instead learns how symbolic consistency and contradiction relations combine through heterogeneous message passing, producing joint robot and object-hypothesis evidence for temporal trust updates. 

C. Neural-Symbolic AI 

Neural-symbolic methods combine the interpretability of symbolic reasoning with the representational flexibility of neural networks [31], [32], and have proven effective for logical inference [33], probabilistic logic programming [34], and relational reasoning [35]. In robotics, they have succeeded in task planning [36], visual and relational reasoning [37], [38], and manipulation [39], [40]. Neural Logic Machines (NLMs) [41] learn multi-step relational reasoning by com-posing symbolic predicates over a shared entity domain. Trust inference in MRS instead involves heterogeneous robot and object-hypothesis entities connected by typed observation and consistency relations. Relation-aware graph neural networks with heterogeneous message passing [42]–[44] provide asparse substrate for this setting. NeST-Bayes is conceptually related to NLM-style relational reasoning, but operates over ego-centric heterogeneous graphs rather than using the explicit predicate operators of an NLM. III. We introduce NeST-Bayes , a trust inference framework based on ego-centric relational graphs encoding cross-robot observation consistency (Fig. 1). At each step, a centralized Trust Aggregation Module (TAM) constructs Gi(t) for each robot ri from ri’s own and neighboring reports and applies a shared neural-symbolic model to reason through each graph, accumulating ego-robot evidence in a system-wide registry while returning validated track evidence to ri, whose track beliefs are maintained onboard. The TAM could equally be replicated onboard each robot; we centralize it to obtain a single system-level trust estimate per robot, leaving the full decentralized deployment to future work.

D. Ablation Studies 

We evaluate each ablation over 100 scenarios, evenly split between normal and optimized adversaries, with the latter equally sampling conservative, moderate, and aggressive poli-cies. Table III shows that the full model achieves the highest mean robot precision and recall among learned variants (97.2% and 95.5%), together with the highest object precision (99.0%) and 94.8% object recall. Compared with a Ho-mogeneous GAT, which collapses all relation types into a single edge type, typed relations improve robot precision and recall by 10.4 and 16.8 percentage points, respectively, and object precision by 12.0 points, showing the value of dis-tinguishing agreement from contradiction. This paper presented NeST-Bayes , a neural-symbolic frame-work that jointly infers robot and object-hypothesis trust from cross-robot observation consistency. By encoding observation agreement and contradiction as symbolic relations in ego-centric heterogeneous graphs and accumulating the resulting neural evidence through Bayesian updates of Beta-distributed trust beliefs, NeST-Bayes operates without prior trust assign-ments, side-channel information, or hand-designed nominal behavior specifications. Comparisons against Na¨ ıve Bayesian ,which accumulates identical symbolic evidence, demonstrate the benefits of learned relational reasoning for both precision and recall, including under occlusion-rich conditions. Compo-nent ablations show that heterogeneous relational propagation improves the coverage of legitimate robots and real objects. Across the benchmark settings, NeST-Bayes achieves the high-est mean object precision and robot recall among trust-based methods, while also leading in robot precision and object recall in most settings.
