# Robust Multi-Agent Pursuit with Preemptive Communication via Reachability-Based Control Barrier Functions 

Madison Book and Justin Bradley 

Abstract — Safe multi-agent coordination typically assumes what real-world, deployed, resource-constrained robots cannot provide: continuous, perfect state sharing. Robust control strategies must be complemented with robust communication strategies to deliver resilient deployments. In this work, we propose a unified control/communication strategy that provides safe control to guarantee inter-agent safety under intermittent communication and demonstrate its effectiveness in a pursuit-evasion scenario. Each agent computes the reachable set of neighboring agents based on the last state information received, then solves for a safe control input based on control barrier functions to ensure agents avoid collision. We prove that inter-agent collision avoidance is guaranteed provided communication staleness remains bounded. As the reachable set inflates between state transmissions, each agent reasons about when to preemptively transmit its state information to neighboring agents based on novel metrics characterizing 

uncertainty risk and kinematic risk . We implement and simulate a variety of pursuit-evasion scenarios and show that preemptive communication reduces messages by 77.5% compared to periodic and event-triggered baselines with zero collisions. 

I. INTRODUCTION Multi-agent safety guarantees based on Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) typically assume continuous state infor-mation [1]. This assumption fails the moment bandwidth, power, or latency issues arise. On small, resource-constrained UAVs, this failure is the norm rather than the exception, leading to event-triggered safe control methods that rely on continuous state monitoring to trigger communication when deemed necessary [2]. In this work, we invert the usual communication trigger: rather than transmitting when an agent’s own state crosses a threshold, agents transmit preemptively, when neighbors need the information to remain safe. An alternative line of work models communication as discrete events [3] but treats control and communication as separate design problems leading to fragile real-world deployments [4]. In reality, control must account for the uncertainty growth with time under stale information. Together, these limitations suggest a need for safety-aware communication that incorporates onboard constraints while ensuring safety. Rather than traditional event-triggered mechanisms [5], which rely on self-state thresholds to transmit state, agents should transmit state when it is needed to preserve safety guarantees. This reframes communication as a safety mechanism.

We address this by combining reachability analysis with CBFs. Reachability analysis over-approximates the set of states an agent could occupy since its last communicated state [6]. CBFs enforce safety constraints by finding control inputs that ensure the state never leaves a safe set [7]. Related to this work, they have been applied to pursuit-evasion [8], [9] and multi-agent navigation [10], [11]. CBFs have also been combined with reachability analysis [12], [13]. But these efforts have left open the opportunity to co-design CBFs with communication in a safety-preserving manner. As reachable sets expand with time, prolonged gaps in communication may lead to the quadratic programming (QP) controller becoming infeasible. To address this, we propose a preemptive communication mechanism where an agent transmits its state when it predicts that neighboring agents don’t have enough information to remain safe. The result is agents that communicate with neighbors as needed, reducing overall communication bandwidth while still providing safety guarantees. Remark 1. Theorem 1 guarantees safety provided 

ωij (xi, t k) ≥ 0 can be enforced at each time step, keeping the QP (17) feasible. QP feasibility is threatened as τij grows (i.e., the reachable set inflates, reducing control authority). The objective of the proposed communication strategy is to preemptively transmit state information to preserve QP feasibility while minimizing unnecessary communication. 

In the proposed decentralized pursuit-evasion scenario, pursuers must coordinate and maintain safety guarantees under intermittent and stale neighbor state information. Traditional communication strategies involve agents transmitting state information at fixed intervals or self-state changes. These methods do not account for cooperative agents that are in physically risky situations or do not have enough information about a teammate to operate safely. To address this, we propose a preemptive communication strategy, where pursuer 

i ∈ P transmits its state to j̸ = i ∈ P when the estimated risk between them exceeds a threshold value. Agent i transmits its state to a neighbor j when i predicts they are at kinematic risk of collision with j or that j doesn’t have enough information about i to operate safely. Define the relative quantities pij = pi(t) − ˆpj (t), vij =

vi(t) − ˆvj (t), uij = ui(t) − uj (tji ), where ˆpj (t) and ˆvj (t)

are computed using ξji and τji . Define dij = ∥pij ∥ and unit direction nij = pij /d ij . To determine when to communicate, each pair of pursuers (i, j ) compute a preemptive risk score defined by 

ρij = 1 − (1 − ρk)(1 − ρu), (19) where ρu, uncertainty risk , quantifies uncertainty due to staleness, and ρk, kinematic risk , quantifies physical collision risk. This ensures that the estimated risk ρij is high when either uncertainty or kinematic risk is high, signaling agent i

to transmit state information. Pursuer i transmits its current state to j when 

ρij ≥ P or τij ≥ T , (20) where P ∈ [0 , 1) is the risk threshold and T is the maximum inter-communication interval. Smaller values of P result in more frequent, conservative communication, while larger values reduce communication at the expense of increased uncertainty and physical risk. T serves as a heartbeat mechanism to bound staleness regardless of risk and ensure that communication failures between agents can be detected. Inter-agent safety requires a finitely bounded T . Both P

and T can be tuned according to system constraints and performance. The uncertainty risk ρu measures pursuer i’s perception of pursuer j’s uncertainty about pursuer i. As staleness grows, this uncertainty inflates the reachable set used by j’s safety controller, eventually threatening QP feasibility. To quantify this, we define uncertainty risk as 

ρu = ri(τij )

A. Discussion of Results 

Table II presents a comprehensive performance comparison across three pursuit-evasion scenarios, including 8 pursuers and 8 evaders (8P8E), 12 pursuers and 6 evaders (12P6E), and 16 pursuers and 4 evaders (16P4E), over 100 trials each. Simulations focused on situations where pursuers were actively closing on evaders. Performance is evaluated along four axes: total messages transmitted, messages per agent per second, inter-pursuer collisions, and total capture time. The results consistently demonstrate that the proposed preemptive communication strategy achieves collision-free operation and competitive task performance at a fraction of the communication cost of all baseline methods. 

1) Communication Efficiency: The proposed preemptive mechanism achieves the lowest communication overhead of any strategy that enforces safety, across every scenario tested. Compared to the full communication strategy, the preemp-tive strategy sends 29.03 × less messages/agent/second and reduces overall message volume by 96.5% while maintaining comparable safety and capture performance. The preemptive mechanism also outperforms the periodic baseline mechanisms across all scenarios. The preemptive strategy reduces overall message volume by 64.1% compared to the 1 Hz strategy and 90.77% compared to the 5 Hz strategy. The preemptive strategy sends 3.26 × and 10.96 ×

fewer messages/agent/s against the 1 Hz and 5 Hz strategies, respectively. Compared to the event-triggered mechanism, the preemptive strategy sends 4.78 × fewer messages/agent/s, which reduces overall message volume by 79.43%. Across all scenarios and baseline communication strategies, the preemptive mechanism reduces total message volume by 82.2%. Compared to the periodic and event-triggered mechanisms, the preemptive strategy reduces total message volume by 77.5%. This reduction is not uniform over time. The preemptive communication strategy concentrates communication when agents are at risk of collision or predict that neighbors need information to operate safely. This effectively reframes communication as a mechanism for safety, where agents transmit their state when they predict their neighbors need information to operate safely.

3) Total Capture Time: In all cases, total capture time between the preemptive strategy and the communicating baselines are within a fraction of a second. This indicates that the 82.2% reduction in message volume incurs no significant penalty in the total capture or task completion time. 

4) Scalability: The advantages of the preemptive strategy compound with scale. Full and periodic strategies scale with agents, as their message rates are governed by the number of agent pairs and communication interval. The event-triggered strategy scales similarly, as more agents operating in a space leads to a higher frequency of significant self-state changes. In contrast, the preemptive mechanism’s communication rate is governed by the situational risk, resulting in more favorable scaling as the number of agents in a system grows. 

5) Summary: The results demonstrate that our preemptive communication mechanism achieves the same performance as other baseline communicating methods, including safety and capture time, while significantly reducing the communication required. Across all scenarios and baseline communication strategies, the preemptive strategy reduces total message volume by 82.2%, further indicating that safety-aware com-munication can significantly reduce overall communication while maintaining provable safety guarantees. VI. CONCLUSION This paper presented a preemptive communication strategy, where agents transmit state when they predict neighbors need information to operate safely. When combined with a reachability-based HOCBF-QP controller to enforce inter-agent safety under intermittent state information, the pre-emptive communication strategy maintained zero inter-agent collisions while reducing the total message volume by 82.2% compared to baseline communication strategies.
