# Safe Multi-Robot Collaborative Transport Using Density Functions 

Jagannath Prasad Sahoo, Sriram S. K. S. Narayanan, and Umesh Vaidya 

Abstract — This paper presents a hierarchical density-based model predictive control framework for safe collaborative ma-nipulation by multiple quadrupedal robots. The framework enables a team of robots to push a shared object to adesired pose using only the initial and goal poses, without requiring a precomputed reference trajectory. A centralized box-level MPC optimizes contact forces while enforcing acontrol-density constraint for goal convergence and obstacle avoidance. Each robot then solves its own distributed robot-level whole-body MPC, under a stated shared-information assumption, to track its moving contact location while ac-counting for static obstacles and the time-varying positions of neighboring robots. The approach is evaluated in MuJoCo using whole-body contact dynamics for two and three Unitree Go2 quadrupeds collaboratively pushing rigid objects through narrow passages. Comparisons with matched Control Barrier Function and RRT* based tracking baselines demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed density-based formulation for push-only, force- and torque-coupled manipulation tasks. Im-plementation videos are available at https://jaggu2606. github.io/go2-density-mpc-pushing/ .

I. Introduction Quadrupedal teams can support exploration, inspection, and search-and-rescue [1], [2], where coordinated, collision-free motion is essential in cluttered environments [3], [4]. Tasks such as debris removal and material transport may also require teams to manipulate objects beyond a single robot’s capacity. Collaborative pushing requires the robots to coordinate their contact forces while avoiding collisions with one another, the object, and surrounding obstacles [5]. This is especially challenging in push-only manipulation, where the contact forces couple the object’s translation and rotation. Collaborative pushing has been approached through plan-ning, optimization-based control, and learned coordination. Sampling-based planners such as RRT* generate collision-free paths, but these plans must still be converted into feasible object motions and robot actions [6]. For contact-rich loco-manipulation, a hierarchical MPC for a single quadruped jointly optimizes contact forces and configuration while coordinating with whole-body locomotion control, using a prescribed object trajectory [7]. Related methods address uncertain object mass and friction through adaptive force regulation [5], and unmodeled payloads through CLF-constrained MPC [8]. These approaches demonstrate coordinated pushing, but do not provide formal guarantees of collision safety for the robots and shared object during execution. Formal safety constraints have also been incorporated into robot control. Control barrier functions provide conditions for forward invariance of safe sets and have been used in MPC [11]; distributed barrier-based controllers further address safety and convergence in multi-agent navigation [12]. These methods offer formal guarantees under their stated assumptions, but do not address the coupled problem of optimizing multiple push-only contact forces on a shared object while coordinating robot-level motion around moving contact targets and neighboring robots. Density functions provide a complementary dual-space formulation of safety and convergence, based on conditions on the evolution of trajectory density [13]. Related ideas have been applied to formation stabilization [14], safe navigation and data-driven planning [15]–[17], and learned transfer-operator models for off-road and legged locomotion [18], [19]. Control density functions have been used to formulate convex and robust safety constraints for quadratic-program controllers [20], [21], and have been applied to safe motion planning and control of robotic systems [22]–[24]. These formulations are primarily developed for continuous-time systems. More recently, control density functions have been incorporated into MPC with tunable safety margins [25]. However, existing density-based safety methods have focused primarily on single-agent systems and do not directly address predictive safety in multi-agent settings. This paper presents a hierarchical density-constrained MPC framework for collaborative manipulation by multiple quadrupedal robots, consisting of a single centralized box-level MPC coupled with 𝑁 distributed robot-level MPCs. A box-level MPC selects the contact forces that drive the shared object toward a desired pose while accounting for static obsta-cles. Each robot then solves its own robot-level whole-body MPC locally to track its moving contact location and enforce additional safety constraints with respect to obstacles and neighboring robots, under the shared-information assumption stated in Section II. Remark 3: Theorem 2 converts the continuous-time density-divergence condition used in [21] into a one-step discrete-time condition. At each prediction stage, the input is held constant over the finite sampling interval 𝑇 𝑠 (zero-order hold), defining the one-step flow map. Integrating the continuous-time condition over this interval yields the stated Perron–Frobenius inequality. Thus, the fixed input in the theorem is local to each one-step map; it does not assume a constant input over the full closed-loop trajectory. In MPC, the density condition is enforced pointwise using the input chosen at each stage and is re-imposed whenever the object-level and robot-level controllers re-solve.

Remark 4 (Active Constraint Set for 𝑁 > 2 agents): 

For the two-robot team, Eqn. (13) involves a single other robot and is instantiated directly. The first optimal input 𝑢 𝑖 (𝑘 ) = 𝑢 ★𝑖,𝑘 | 𝑘 is applied through the whole-body controller. At each sampling instant, the controller measures the object and robot states, solves the object-level MPC, and generates interaction-force references and predicted contact targets. This suggests that phase locking helps coordinate contact loading, although broader tests are needed to determine its benefits. This may be more consequential for a shared payload carried atop the robots, where gait-induced relative motion could affect payload stability. Limitations and Future Work. Validation is limited to simulated box and T-shape pushing with two and three robots, fixed contacts, and planar safety envelopes. Larger teams require evaluation of computational scalability, while arbitrary object geometries and complex or dynamic environments require richer collision representations beyond the current bounding-size margin. Future work will quantify minimum robot-envelope clearances, account for estimation and geo-metric uncertainty near small clearance margins, and validate contact-force tracking and real-time execution on hardware. VI. Conclusions We presented a hierarchical density-constrained MPC framework for multi-quadruped collaborative pushing with-out a prescribed object trajectory. Object-level contact-force optimization is coupled with robot-level tracking and density-based obstacle and inter-agent constraints. MuJoCo exper-iments with box and T-shape pushing demonstrated goal attainment with positive recorded object-footprint clearances. Compared with matched CBF MPC, density MPC reduced completion time by 22 .5% and 6 .3% in the two scenarios, while RRT* based tracking MPC failed to complete either task. The current validation is limited to simulated two- and three-robot teams, fixed contacts, and planar safety envelopes; future work will address computational scalability, dynamic environments, uncertainty, and real-time hardware validation.
