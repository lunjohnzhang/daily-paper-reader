# Communication-Free Obstacle Localization from Aggregate Wrench Measurements in Leader–Follower Cooperative Transport 

Amin Kashiri, Aditya Mohan, and Yasin Yazıcıo˘ glu 

Abstract — We consider obstacle localization for a team of robots cooperatively transporting a rigid payload without explicit inter-robot communication. A leader robot directs the payload’s motion, while follower robots assist and react to locally detected obstacles. The leader measures the followers’ aggregate wrench, i.e., the combined force and torque they exert on the payload, but cannot directly distinguish their individual reactions. We design a follower control law that allows the leader to recover obstacle locations from these measurements. Each follower resists motion toward nearby obstacles, resulting in a piecewise-linear relationship between the payload’s transla-tional and angular velocity and the aggregate wrench. Changes between adjacent linear regions reveal an obstacle’s bearing and distance and identify the responding follower. We give sufficient conditions for exact recovery at a fixed payload configuration and develop an adaptive probing procedure in which the leader applies translational and rotational inputs to the payload to obtain the required measurements. We demonstrate the performance of the proposed method in simulations. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

When a team of robots carries a shared payload with-out communicating, the payload is their only coordination channel. In leader–follower schemes, a leader that knows the destination steers the payload while each follower reinforces the motion measured at its own grip, so the team transports a rigid body with no communication at all [1], [2]; the principle has been implemented on robots [3] and extended to flexible payloads [4]. Where communication or a shared model is available, transport is instead posed as decentralized opti-mization [5], [6], [7], [8] or adaptive control over a broadcast common state [9]. In these works, obstacle information is either absent or supplied directly to the robot that plans around it. A follower, however, may sense an obstacle that the leader cannot, and without communication it can convey this only through how it pushes on the payload. Such interaction has been used for inference: followers infer the motion intended by a leader that knows the obstacles [10], a leader recovers obstacles from the input of the single follower in a two-robot team [11], and inverse safety filtering infers

With several followers, the leader instead senses one aggregate wrench, three numbers regardless of team size, in which all reactions are summed, so no single measurement reveals which follower responded or to what. In this paper, we show that the leader can separate these reactions by actively probing the payload. We design a follower law under which each follower resists only motion carrying it toward a nearby obstacle. The map from payload twist to aggregate wrench then becomes piecewise linear, and the change between adjacent linear regions encodes one obstacle’s bearing and distance together with the follower that detected it. Recovering piecewise-linear maps from queries is well studied for hybrid systems [13] and ReLU networks [14]; here, the structure is designed into the team so that every region boundary has a physical meaning. Our contributions are: (i) a communication-free follower law that makes individual obstacle reactions separable in the aggregate wrench; (ii) sufficient conditions under which measurements at a fixed payload configuration uniquely de-termine every detected obstacle and its responding follower; and (iii) an adaptive probing procedure that selects the leader’s test motions to obtain these measurements, evaluated in simulation. In this work, we considered obstacle localization for a leader–follower team of robots cooperatively transporting a rigid payload without explicit inter-robot communication. Specifically, we investigated how the leader can localize ob-stacles outside its sensing range by measuring the aggregate wrench exerted on the payload by the followers. We proposed a reaction law under which each follower resists only motion toward nearby obstacles. We showed that this law makes the map from the payload’s twist to the followers’ aggregate wrench piecewise linear, with a rank-one change whenever a single follower-obstacle pair starts or stops resisting, and gave conditions under which each obstacle location and its responding follower are recovered in closed form from observations in adjacent cells. We also designed a probing procedure for the leader to search for such observations by comparing neighboring samples and splitting the intervals where several pairs switch. Simulation results demonstrated the performance of the proposed method. Future work will extend the proposed probing procedure to formally guarantee the collection of observations enabling exact recovery. We also intend to test our method on real robots.
