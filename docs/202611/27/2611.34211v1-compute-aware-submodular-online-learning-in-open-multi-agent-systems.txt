Abstract 

This paper studies policy learning for cooperative task allocation in open multi-agent systems, where submodular utilities model diminishing marginal returns due to overlapping agent contributions. While standard methods evaluate every candidate action to update the policy, this may incur an excessive computational cost online. Hence, we impose a budget on these evaluations and analytically study the effect of query budget on online learning. Our algorithm samples a prescribed number of agent-action pairs and applies inverse-probability weighing to their marginal gains, yielding an unbiased gradient estimate of the expected stage utility for a Kullback-Leibler (KL) mirror-ascent update. We derive a 1/2-approximate regret bound that elegantly splits into a tracking error between optimal and learned policies, quantified by their KL divergence, and a query-dependent feedback term. Experiments on cooperative information coverage show that our approach retains high performance with significantly less computation online. 

Keywords: Online learning, submodular optimization, multi-agent system, open system. 

1. Introduction 

Multi-agent task allocation refers to the problem where cooperative autonomous agents are dispatched to engage with given tasks. Sub-additive agent contributions give rise to so-called diminishing returns: each agent’s individual (or marginal) contribution to global performance decreases when more agents engage with a target. This is common in robotics where diminishing returns model, e.g., overlapping camera fields-of-view in cooperative coverage (Tzoumas et al., 2016). Submodularity offers an elegant framework to formally capture such overlaps and derive provable suboptimality gaps if the feasible set has added structure in the form of a matroid, which includes common cardinality and partition constraints (Nemhauser et al., 1978; Calinescu et al., 2011).

The intersection between submodularity and data-driven coordination policies is still under-explored, especially in multi-agent systems. Online coordination methods use knowledge about submodular marginal gains (Xu et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2025b,a), while bandit methods leverage feedback on selected actions (Zhang et al., 2019; Wan et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023a). Related to submodular data-driven optimization, previous works by the authors study submodular multi-agent reinforcement learning; Liu et al. (2026b) designs policy gradient tailored to submodular rewards, while Liu et al. (2026a) learns tabular softmax policies via Kullback-Leibler (KL) mirror ascent. While these studies leverage submodularity to evaluate the marginal gains of alternative actions and iteratively improve the policy, they assume no constraint on the learning agent feedback, i.e., all candidate actions can be evaluated at each policy update. However, this assumption may fail in online learning; if the physical learning device (e.g., a mobile robot or smart sensor) carries limited compute, it cannot timely evaluate all marginal gains corresponding to a large number of actions or agents, introducing a latency bottleneck for real-time operation. Moreover, open systems, where agents may join or leave during operation (Deplano et al., 2026), exacerbate this issue; each agent must select its action while accounting for possible changes in the team. Budgeted feedback has been studied in label-efficient prediction (Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2005), reinforcement learning (Efroni et al., 2021), and nonstationary bandits (Li and Li, 2025). However, these works limit access to labels or rewards rather than counterfactual marginal evaluations used to update the policies, while we focus on the latter issue which is central to compute-aware learning. 

Contribution. To address this challenge, we extend SubMAPL (Liu et al., 2026a) to online learning with budgeted marginal feedback. Instead of evaluating every candidate action, we impose a given number of marginal queries to estimate the gradient of the expected utility. This reduces the number of marginal evaluations at each round while allowing the policy to adapt as the agents and feasible actions change. Our contributions are summarized as follows. • We propose a policy-aware query allocation strategy that distributes a given number of marginal evaluations across agent-action pairs at each learnig round. Unlike methods that restrict reward observations (Efroni et al., 2021; Li and Li, 2025), we allocate counterfactual marginal queries for policy updates. In particular, we derive and characterize an optimal allocation rule that minimizes an upper bound on the second moment of the policy gradient estimator. • We develop an online submodular policy learning method with a given number of queries at each round. Our method constructs an unbiased gradient estimator using only the selected marginal evaluations, enabling online learning with limited compute in real time. A fixed-size sampling procedure ensures that the query budget is always satisfied, updating policies online as agents and feasible actions change. • We establish a 1/2-approximate dynamic regret bound for our method. Compared with recent studies on multi-agent online learning (Zhang et al., 2025b,a), our bound highlights the distinct contributions of feedback budget and of time-varying agent set due to open systems. Numerical experiments on cooperative coverage showcase performance close to the compute-unaware full-feedback baseline despite a substantial reduction in marginal queries. 

Organization. We introduce the multi-agent submodular online learning problem in Section 2. In Section 3, we recap SubMAPL, which we extend to budgeted online learning in Section 4. In Section 5, we establish a provable regret bound and propose a strategy to sample counterfactual feedback that minimizes it. We report simulations results in Section 6 and conclude in Section 7.

7. Conclusion 

This paper presented a KL mirror-ascent method for task allocation in open multi-agent systems. Deterministic rounding and inverse-probability weighting of counterfactual samples enforce a pre-scribed budget and preserve unbiased gradient estimation. We established a regret bound separating the contributions of open system and query budget, respectively, and proposed a query allocation that minimizes it. Experiments on cooperative coverage report essentially unchanged performance at a fraction of full-feedback queries, supporting online learning with cheap compute. Future work will consider noisy marginal evaluations, neural policies, and deeper compute evaluation.
