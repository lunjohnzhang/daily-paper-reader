# LSRP*-LA: Scalable and Anytime Multi-Agent Path Finding for Large Agents with Asynchronous Actions 

Dachuan Liu 1, Shizhe Zhao 1, and Zhongqiang Ren 1,†

Abstract — Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) seeks collision-free paths for multiple agents from their start locations to their goal locations while minimizing path costs. Most MAPF algorithms assume that each agent occupies a single vertex and that the actions of all agents are synchronized. These assumptions may limit the use of MAPF planners in practice. Existing planners that get rid of both assumptions either handle only a few agents or cannot improve their solutions. Recently, a planner called LSRP* was proposed, which is scalable and anytime with asynchronous actions, but still limited by that each agent occupies a single vertex. This paper develops a new planner called LSRP*-LA for MAPF for large agents with asynchronous actions (LA-MAPF-AA), where an agent can occupy several cells. LSRP*-LA is scalable and complete, and it keeps improving solution quality after the initial solution is obtained until the runtime budget depletes. We analyze the properties of LSRP*-LA and test it against several baselines with up to 800 agents on various maps. LSRP*-LA can handle twice as many agents as baseline methods and its solution cost is about half that of recent synchronous planners for large agents. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) seeks collision-free paths for multiple agents from their start locations to their goal locations while minimizing the path costs. It arises in applications such as logistics and automated warehouses. MAPF is NP-hard to solve to optimality [1], and exist-ing planners trade solution quality for scalability: optimal planners such as conflict-based search (CBS) [2], rule-based planners such as PIBT [3], which handle thousands of agents, and anytime planners, which keep improving a first solution while time remains [4], [5]. Most of these MAPF planners rely on two assumptions: (i) the point-agent assumption, where every agent occupies a single vertex of the graph, and (ii) the synchronous-action assumption, where every action of the agents, either wait or move, takes one time step, and all agents start and end their actions at the same integer time steps. In practice, robot fleets may satisfy neither of the two assumptions. In the car plant of Fig. 1(a), automated tugger trains and Smart Transport Robots (STRs) share one hall. A tugger train is several times longer than an STR, so it occupies several cells and can block several STRs at the same time (Fig. 1(b)). Tugger trains and STRs also move at different speeds, so they do not start and finish their moves at the same time. Some existing work drops one of the two assumptions but keeps the other. The rule-based planner LSRP [8] scales to hundreds of agents, and its extension LSRP* [9] is in addition complete and anytime. In MAPF for large agents (LA-MAPF) [10], an agent occupies several cells, and even finding a feasible solution is NP-hard [11]. Several variants of MAPF have been developed to address this problem, such as generalizing CBS [12], Multi-Dependency PIBT (MD-PIBT) [13], and Layered LA-MAPF [14]. These planners assume synchronous actions. Only a few planners drop both assumptions. CCBS [15] plans in continuous time for agents with a geometric body that traverse edges of different lengths, and AOC-CBS [16] handles heterogeneous fleets and keeps improving its solu-tion while time remains. These planners build on CBS and aim at optimal solutions. Their runtime grows quickly with the number of conflicts, and large agents cause far more conflicts, leading to poor scalability. Prioritized planning with safe-interval path planning (PP-SIPP) handles robots of different sizes and speeds and scales to hundreds of robots [17], but it is incomplete and cannot improve its solution. Different from these planners, this paper develops LSRP*-LA, a planner that drops both assumptions while being able to scale to a large number of agents with anytime solution refinement. LSRP*-LA builds upon LSRP* [9], which does not consider large agents but can handle many point agents by employing a rule-based procedure to complete joint actions quickly, and a search over joint actions in the style of LaCAM [18] to make the planner complete and anytime. However, extending LSRP* to large agents is challenging.

First, a large agent may be blocked by several agents at once, and each of them may need several actions to get out of its way. Planning one action of a large agent thus couples many agents over several actions, and these agents have to be planned together in their joint space, whose size grows exponentially with agents number. Second, due to asynchronous actions, the later actions of a push start at different times, and other agents are planned in between. Such an agent sees only the current footprints of the agents in the push, so it may move into the cells that one of their later actions will occupy. Synchronous planners for large agents (such as [13]) bypass this challenge by deciding the actions of all agents together at each time step. To address these challenges, LSRP*-LA builds upon the search process of LSRP*, and checks collisions between the footprints of the agents. First, a pusher agent pushes all agents that block the pusher within an action, as in MD-PIBT [13], and each pushed agent plans a short sequence of actions, called an evacuation , to leave the pusher’s way. Second, a pre-execution check tests each planned action for collisions right before it starts. With these techniques, we show that LSRP*-LA is complete and guaranteed to eventually find an optimal solution given sufficient runtime. In our tests, we compare LSRP*-LA against several base-lines, including LSRP [8], CBS-AA [7] extended to large agents, PP-SIPP [17], and the synchronous planners MD-PIBT [13] and Layered LA-MAPF [14], using various maps from a popular MAPF benchmark [19]. The results show that LSRP*-LA can handle up to 40 times as many agents as the baselines. Specifically, on den312d, our LSRP*-LA can solve instances with up to 800 agents, twice as many as LSRP alone, whereas CBS-AA and PP-SIPP solve no instance with more than 20 and 200 agents. The anytime search in our LSRP*-LA refines the cost of the initial solution by up to about 34% within the runtime budget. LSRP*-LA often finds nearly optimal solutions, as its solution costs are at most 2% higher than that of CBS-AA-LA, an optimal planner, on small scale problems. Planning with asynchronous actions is also important, as the solution costs of LSRP*-LA are about half of that of the synchronous planners (MD-PIBT [13], Layered LA-MAPF [14]) which ignores asynchronous actions. II. R ELATED WORK 

Planning for MAPF-AA. Loosely Synchronized Search (LSS) [6] and CBS-AA [7] seek optimal solutions and handle tens of agents. Loosely Synchronized Rule-based Planning (LSRP) [8] lets an agent push the agent that blocks it and scales to hundreds of agents, but it cannot improve its solution. LSRP* [9] makes LSRP complete and anytime with a search inspired by LaCAM* [5], [18]. LaCAM* keeps only the cheapest path to each joint configuration, which does not suffice with asynchronous actions. LSRP* therefore searches over joint actions together with their start and end times, and compares two paths by both the cost and the arrival times of the agents. These planners assume that each agent occupies a single vertex. 

Planning for large agents. CBS has been extended to large agents [10], [12]. Multi-Dependency PIBT (MD-PIBT) [13] lets one agent push several agents, and Layered LA-MAPF [14] splits an instance into smaller ones. These planners assume synchronous actions. HetPIBT [20] extends PIBT to robots of different sizes and speeds, but it is incomplete and cannot improve its solution. III. P ROBLEM DEFINITION

C. Search Algorithm 

LSRP*-LA searches as LSRP* does (Fig. 5). It keeps the search nodes in a stack Open , which initially holds the root 

n0 with a0 and T (n0) = {θ0}. It repeats four steps. (1) It pops the top search node n of Open . (2) It removes the first action node θ from the frontier T (n) and appends the children Ch LA (θ). (3) It generates one successor nnext of n:the busy agents keep their actions, the agents in Itree (θ) take the actions in Atree (θ), and the rule-based procedure plans the other idle agents. (4) It pushes n back if T (n)̸ = ∅,and then pushes nnext with T (nnext ) = {θ0}. Since nnext is on top of Open , the search goes deep and quickly reaches a goal node. The incumbent n∗ is the cheapest goal node found so far. Once it exists, a popped search node n with 

f (n) ≥ g(n∗) is pruned. The search returns n∗ when Open 

is empty or time runs out.

LSRP*-LA keeps the guarantees of LSRP* [9]. 

Theorem 1 (Completeness and optimality) . LSRP*-LA ter-minates and finds a solution for every solvable instance. If it runs until Open is empty, the solution that it returns is optimal. Proof sketch. The theorem holds for LSRP* [9]. LSRP*-LA differs from LSRP* only in how the action tree detects conflicts and in the rule-based procedure. So it suffices to show that the following two properties still hold. (i) The leaves of AT (a) are exactly the valid successors of a. A child of an action node θ adds a move only if the agent’s swept area, shares no cell with Rtree (θ), so every leaf is valid. (ii) The rule-based procedure terminates and returns a valid successor. It terminates, because a pusher is planned and only unplanned agents are pushed: no agent appears twice in a chain of pushes, so the recursion of A SY PUSH -LA is at most N deep. The successor is valid, because a move starts at the current event τ only if it collides neither with the cells occupied by the planned agents nor with the footprint of an unplanned agent. So the assigned moves do not collide with each other. Moreover, no move enters the footprint of an agent that is still unplanned, so this agent can always wait at its vertex, and every idle agent receives an action. C. Solution Quality 

We compare the SOC of the final solutions with a 60 s time limit. Each plot in Fig. 7 shows the SOC ratio of LSRP*-LA over a baseline; lower is better. The purple curve (vs. LSRP-LA) drops below 100% on all maps, showing that LSRP*-LA finds solutions up to about 25% cheaper than LSRP-LA. The green curve (vs. CBS-AA-LA) stays within 2% of 100% on the small instances that CBS-AA-LA solves. As CBS-AA-LA extends an optimal planner, this shows that LSRP*-LA has high solution quality. The blue curve (vs. PP-SIPP-LA) is close to 100% for small N and rises to about 112% for larger N . It shows that LSRP*-LA obtains solution quality comparable to PP-SIPP-LA. 

D. Benefit of Asynchronous Planning 

To solve LA-MAPF-AA, a synchronous planner must let every step last as long as the slowest action. Against MD-PIBT and Layered LA-MAPF executed with durations of 5, LSRP*-LA costs about half of their SOC (25–78%, Fig. 8). This comparison favors LSRP*-LA, since part of the gap comes from the durations of 5 alone: even for a single agent, LSRP*-LA costs about half. To check whether the plans of LSRP*-LA are also better, we compare the planners on the problem that the synchronous planners are designed for. We set all durations to 1, which turns the instances into LA-MAPF instances (Remark 2), so the synchronous planners no longer lose time to the durations of 5. LSRP*-LA is still never more expensive on average (Fig. 9): its SOC ratio is 65–99% against MD-PIBT and 38–100% against Layered LA-MAPF. Hence the gain of LSRP*-LA does not come from asynchronous actions alone; its plans are also cheaper. Fig. 9. SOC ratio of LSRP*-LA over the synchronous planners in the synchronous setting. E. Effect of Evacuation 

We compare LSRP*-LA with and without evacuation on Berlin 1 256 and maze-128-128-10 with N = 40 and N =100 (Fig. 10). Without evacuation, a pushed agent makes a single move and is pushed again and again, so the first solution costs 1.3 to 9.0 times as much. After 60 s, the SOC without evacuation is still 110–900% of the first solution with evacuation, whereas with evacuation it falls to 50–95%. VII. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

This paper extends LSRP* to LSRP*-LA, a scalable, complete and anytime planner for MAPF with large agents and asynchronous actions. Its evacuation lets pushed agents make way for large agents, and it scales to hundreds of agents with high-quality solutions.
