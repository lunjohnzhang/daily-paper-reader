## Abstract 

World action models have demonstrated promising perfor-mance in robot policy learning, yet their high inference la-tency remains a critical bottleneck for real-world deploy-ment. Recent approaches have sought to improve inference efficiency by reducing test-time future-frame imagination or accelerating action prediction. However, these methods of-ten leverage representations from all video world model lay-ers without explicitly considering their varying relevance to action prediction, leading to redundant computation and suboptimal representation utilization. In this work, we pro-pose TurboWAM, a fast world action model that efficiently aggregates structured world representations conditioned on action predictions. Specifically, TurboWAM introduces a structured world representation aggregation module that adaptively fuses representations from different world model

## 1. Introduction 

World Action Models (WAMs) [2, 12, 14, 22, 23, 26] have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robot policy learning. Typically, WAMs integrate future visual genera-1arXiv:2610.33941v1 [cs.CV] 24 Oct 2026 tion with action prediction into a unified framework, allow-ing robots to anticipate how the physical world evolves dur-ing interaction. Despite their promising performance, the high computational cost of WAMs remains a critical bottle-neck for real-world deployment. In particular, WAMs often rely on large-scale video generative models that require it-erative denoising to predict future observations, resulting in substantial computational overhead and inference latency. This limits their applicability to real-time robotic control, where efficient and responsive action prediction is essential. Recently, several methods [13, 15, 18, 23, 27] have been proposed to improve the inference efficiency of WAMs for robot policy learning. These methods primarily address the computational bottleneck by reducing test-time future imagination, accelerating action prediction, or developing more efficient model architectures. Despite their encourag-ing results, several limitations remain. First, these methods often process or aggregate features from all world model layers without explicitly considering their varying relevance to the ongoing action prediction. However, different lay-ers may contribute unequally to action prediction, and their importance can vary across denoising timesteps. Conse-quently, indiscriminately processing all layers introduces redundant computation and leads to suboptimal representa-tion utilization. Second, existing approaches typically lack explicit mechanisms to regulate how action supervision in-fluences the optimization of different world model layers. Without such mechanisms, the learned world representa-tions may not be sufficiently aligned with the requirements of action prediction, limiting policy performance. To resolve these limitations, in this work, we propose TurboWAM, a novel fast world action model that efficiently aggregates structured 1 world representations conditioned on action predictions. Following [18, 23], TurboWAM pre-serves video co-training during training while eliminat-ing future visual prediction during inference. Specifically, we introduce a structured world representation aggregation module that adaptively aggregates representations from dif-ferent world model layers according to their relevance to action prediction at each denoising timestep. Unlike exist-ing methods that rely on fixed or action-independent rep-resentation fusion, our approach dynamically adjusts the contribution of different layers based on the current action denoising state. Furthermore, by restricting inference to a limited number of world model layers and caching reusable representations for efficient aggregation, TurboWAM sub-stantially reduces redundant computation while preserving informative world representations for action prediction. ## 2. Related Work 

World Action Models. World Action Models (WAMs) [12, 22, 24, 25, 29] and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models [3, 4, 10, 11] have emerged as two prominent paradigms for robot policy learning. While VLA models typically predict actions directly from visual observations and language instructions, WAMs leverage visual generative models, such as video generation mod-els [20], to incorporate knowledge of physical dynamics into action prediction. By jointly modeling future visual observations and robot actions, WAMs learn world rep-resentations that capture how the environment evolves during interaction [2, 12, 14, 22, 23]. Representative approaches, including DreamZero [22], Motus [2], Cosmos Policy [12], and LingBot-VA [14], have demonstrated the potential of leveraging predictive world modeling for robot control. Despite their promising performance, these methods often incur substantial computational overhead due to the expensive process of future visual generation, limiting their applicability to real-time robotic control. Low-Latency World Action Models. Recent efforts have explored various strategies to reduce the inference la-tency of WAMs. Fast-WAM [23] eliminates explicit future visual generation during inference while retaining video co-training during training, enabling efficient action prediction without test-time future imagination. Faster-WAM [18] ag-gregates key and value representations from all video world model layers using learned fusion weights and employs a single-DiT-block action head to reduce action prediction la-tency. ARPS [8] aggregates intermediate world model fea-tures into a compact state for the action expert to reduce inference overhead and employs future-representation su-pervision to enhance world representation learning. Other methods explore model compression and lightweight archi-tectures [13, 15], as well as sparse video-action interac-tions [27], to improve inference efficiency. Despite these advances, existing methods do not explicitly explore adap-tive world representation learning conditioned on the evolv-ing action denoising state and still leave room for improve-ment in the efficient utilization of world representations. In contrast, our TurboWAM introduces structured world rep-resentation aggregation that dynamically adjusts the contri-butions of different world model layers conditioned on the current action denoising state. Additionally, our structured action supervision explicitly modulates gradients from ac-tion supervision across different world model layers. By re-ducing the number of world model layers executed during inference and caching reusable representations for efficient aggregation, TurboWAM further reduces inference latency.

Framework Overview. An overview of TurboWAM is shown in Figure 2. During training , we encode image ob-servations using a VAE encoder and process language in-structions and proprioceptive states using their respective encoders to obtain latent representations. Noise is then added to the future-observation latents, which are fed into the video world model Fw for denoising conditioned on the current-observation latents, language instructions, and proprioceptive states. Meanwhile, we extract the key and value tokens corresponding to the current observation from the first m layers of the video world model and use them directly for structured representation aggregation. Follow-ing [18], we employ a single DiT block as the action head, which takes noisy action latents as queries and the aggre-gated world representations as keys and values for attention-based action prediction. At a given action denoising step, the noisy action latents condition the structured representa-tion aggregation module, which produces the fused world representations used for action prediction. During back-ward propagation, structured action supervision modulates gradients from the action loss to the selected world model representations, while the video loss follows ordinary back-propagation. During inference , TurboWAM predicts ac-tions from the current observation, language instruction, and proprioceptive state without performing future imagi-nation. Only the first m world model layers are executed once per control decision, and their key and value represen-tations are cached for reuse throughout action denoising. The structured representation aggregation module updates the fusion weights at each denoising step based on the cur-rent noisy action latents, whereas structured action supervi-sion is not applied during inference. Thus, TurboWAM re-duces inference latency through efficient representation ag-gregation while maintaining competitive task success rates. 

3.2. Structured World Representation 

Observation and Motivation. In WAMs, the world model produces hierarchical representations across multi-4ple layers, capturing different levels of abstraction and tem-poral dynamics. Fast-WAM [23] employs a fixed one-to-one correspondence between world model layers and ac-tion head layers without aggregating world representations across layers. This rigid layer-wise coupling incurs substan-tial computational overhead and limits the flexibility of uti-lizing hierarchical world representations. Faster-WAM [18] instead employs a docking interface to fuse world represen-tations from all world model layers for action prediction. However, Faster-WAM still requires processing all world model layers during inference and does not explicitly adapt its learned fusion weights to the evolving action denoising state. As shown in Figure 3(a), the docking interface of Faster-WAM assigns larger fusion weights to intermediate world model layers than to early and late layers. This sug-gests that intermediate-layer representations are particularly relevant to action prediction. In addition, Figure 3(b) shows that spatial feature relationships between the reference lo-cation (close to the gripper) and other regions vary across world model layers, suggesting that different layers capture different aspects of the spatial information relevant to action prediction. Moreover, Figure 3(c) shows that the influence of individual world model layers on action prediction varies across denoising steps, with relatively small variations at early steps and more pronounced variations at later steps. These observations suggest that world model layers con-tribute unequally to action prediction and that their relative importance can change along the denoising trajectory. Mo-tivated by these observations, we propose a structured rep-resentation aggregation module that adaptively combines representations from selected world model layers according to the current action denoising state.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate TurboWAM on three widely used robot ma-nipulation benchmarks, namely LIBERO [16], LIBERO-Plus [7], and RoboTwin2.0 [5], and additionally conduct ex-periments on a real-world robot platform involving several manipulation tasks. LIBERO consists of four task suites, namely Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long, each containing 10 tasks with a total of 500 demonstrations. We train Tur-boWAM on all four suites and report task success rates over 2,000 evaluation trials across 40 tasks. LIBERO-Plus ex-tends LIBERO by introducing seven categories of pertur-bations, including camera viewpoints, robot initial states, language instructions, lighting conditions, background tex-tures, sensor noise, and object layouts, to evaluate the ro-bustness and generalization capabilities of robot policies. We evaluate the zero-shot generalization of TurboWAM on 10,030 LIBERO-Plus instances by directly applying the models trained on the original LIBERO suites without fur-ther adaptation. We report task success rates across all seven perturbation categories. RoboTwin2.0 is a bimanual robot manipulation benchmark comprising 50 tasks. Fol-lowing [18, 23], we train TurboWAM using 2,500 demon-strations from clean scenes and 25,000 demonstrations from randomized scenes across all 50 tasks. We report task suc-cess rates over 100 evaluation trials per task under both clean and randomized conditions. For real-world robot ex-periments, details of the experimental setup and results are provided in the supplementary material.

4.3. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods 

Compared Methods. We compare TurboWAM against state-of-the-art WAM methods, including Motus[2], LingBot-VA[14], Cosmos Policy[12], Efficient-WAM[13], Light-WAM[15], Faster-WAM[18], and Fast-WAM[23]. We also include representative VLA-based methods (X-VLA[28] and π0.5[4]) for reference. The inference latency of all compared methods is measured on the same single data-center GPU with approximately 140 GB of memory under identical environmental settings. 

Result Analysis. As shown in Table 1, TurboWAM achieves competitive task success rates with substan-tially reduced inference latency on both LIBERO and RoboTwin2.0. Specifically, on LIBERO, TurboWAM achieves the highest average success rate of 99.0% and the lowest inference latency of 49.7 ms among the com-pared methods. On RoboTwin2.0, TurboWAM maintains strong task performance, achieving an average success rate of 90.5% with the lowest inference latency of 47.1 ms. We further evaluate the zero-shot generalization of TurboWAM on LIBERO-Plus. As shown in Table 2, TurboWAM achieves competitive performance across the seven pertur-bation categories compared with state-of-the-art methods. Notably, these results are obtained by directly evaluating the models trained on the original LIBERO suites without any additional adaptation. This demonstrates the robust-ness and generalization capability of TurboWAM under di-verse perturbations and environmental variations. In addi-tion, Figure 4 presents qualitative comparison of task exe-cution among TurboWAM, Fast-WAM, and Faster-WAM. We observe that TurboWAM completes the manipulation tasks both quickly and accurately, whereas Fast-WAM and Faster-WAM either take longer to complete the tasks or fail in some scenarios. 4.4. Ablation Study 

Structured Representation Aggregation. Table 3 stud-ies the effectiveness of the proposed structured representa-tion aggregation module. In addition to the default struc-tured representation aggregation, we investigate three alter-native strategies: (1) using the structured representation ag-gregation without the learned static logit; (2) directly av-eraging world representations across all layers; (3) adopt-ing the DoT aggregation strategy from Faster-WAM [18]. As shown in the table, the proposed structured representa-tion aggregation consistently outperforms these alternative strategies. Specifically, removing the learned static logit term leads to a slight performance degradation. Directly averaging world representations leads to substantially lower task success rates. Replacing the proposed aggregation with the DoT aggregation strategy within our TurboWAM frame-work results in inferior performance. These results validate the effectiveness of our design.

## 5. Conclusion 

In this work, we present TurboWAM, a novel fast world action model that reduces inference latency while main-taining competitive task success rates. TurboWAM intro-duces a structured world representation aggregation mod-ule that adaptively aggregates relevant world representa-tions conditioned on the evolving action denoising state. Also, TurboWAM introduces structured action supervision that adaptively modulates gradients from action supervi-sion across different world model layers. Extensive exper-iments show that TurboWAM achieves significantly lower inference latency while maintaining competitive task suc-cess rates compared with state-of-the-art methods. 8Limitations and Future Work. Despite its promising performance, TurboWAM still has several limitations. First, it may face challenges in scenarios requiring complex spa-tial reasoning and intricate object interactions. Incorporat-ing geometric priors from spatial foundation models [9, 21] or semantic knowledge from vision-language models [1] could be promising directions to address these challenges. Second, further improving task success rates while preserv-ing low inference latency remains an important research di-rection. Future work will explore these directions to further enhance the robustness and efficiency of TurboWAM.
