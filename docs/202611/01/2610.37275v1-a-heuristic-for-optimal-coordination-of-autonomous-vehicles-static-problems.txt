# A heuristic for optimal coordination of autonomous vehicles: static problems 

Saugat Shahi, Filippo Fabiani and Mario Zanon 

Abstract —We consider the coordination of autonomous ve-hicles at unsignalized intersections, where trajectory planning and crossing-order decisions are intrinsically coupled, thereby making their simultaneous optimization intractable in practice. To this end, we propose a heuristic based on a hierarchical structure consisting of a series of nonlinear programs (NLP) in the lower level, which optimizes trajectories of each vehicle for a given crossing order, and a high-level heuristic to design new crossing schedules that exploits sensitivity information from the lower-level NLPs. Extensive numerical simulations across a broad range of traffic scenarios demonstrate that our strategy achieves substantially improved scheduling with respect to first-in-first-out ordering while preserving computational tractability. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The coordination of autonomous vehicles at unsignalized intersections is a crucial problem in transportation systems of the future, where the replacement of traffic rules with optimized coordination promises significant improvements in terms of travel time, fuel consumption, and throughput [1]– [3]. This problem requires one to simultaneously perform (a) trajectory planning, and (b) collision-free ordering of vehicles. Existing approaches address this challenge by using differ-ent strategies, see, e.g., the survey papers [4]–[7]. In particular, mixed-integer formulations [8]–[11] encode the crossing order through binary variables and optimize it jointly with the trajectories. While such formulations capture the coupling between order and motion explicitly, the number of binary variables however grows combinatorially with the number of vehicles, thereby hindering scalability to realistic scenarios. To alleviate the complexity of mixed-integer approaches, some techniques resort to heuristics in order to decide the crossing order. For instance, rule-based policies fix the order through predefined priorities, e.g., derived from right-of-way conventions [12] or from the kinematic state of each approach-ing vehicle [13]. Reservation-based schemes, instead, grant conflict-free space-time slots on a first-come basis through a central manager [14], [15]. These approaches are computa-tionally light and provably collision-free, but they decide the crossing order from simple fixed criteria that do not account for vehicle dynamics or the individual cost of each vehicle. Consequently, these heuristics may perform very poorly in scenarios where the vehicles have heterogeneous costs. The formulation we adopt builds upon the work of [3], [16]– [21], which casts intersection coordination as a constrained op-timal control problem and excludes collisions through prece-dence constraints based on the times at which vehicles enter and leave the intersection. This approach is flexible, as it offers the opportunity to include energy-optimal objectives [18], [22], vehicles that turn inside the intersection [23], as well as networks of adjacent intersections [24]. Closely related for-mulations are the convex conflict-resolution model of [25], the cooperative receding-horizon scheme of [26], the safety-and-liveness framework of [27], and the parallelized distributed MPC of [28]. Nevertheless, these formulations all share the same limitation, namely they assume a fixed crossing order, decoupling the scheduling decision from motion planning and leaving part of the achievable performance unexplored. Decoupling scheduling and motion planning motivates the interest in schedule-refinement heuristics, which attempt to improve an initial schedule based on some information given by the motion planning task. In [29], the order is selected by solving a mixed-integer quadratic approximation of the coor-dination problem, and in [30] vehicle priorities are reassigned within prioritized distributed model predictive control. Unlike available methods, we propose a coordination scheme that refines the crossing order using sensitivity infor-mation from the motion planner. For a fixed order, a single NLP jointly optimizes the trajectories and the entry and exit times of all vehicles. A higher-level heuristic then refines this order using the dual variables of the NLP: the Lagrange multipliers of the precedence constraints are the first-order sensitivities of the cost associated with each ordering decision. Since this information is local, each candidate swap is retained only if the cost is actually reduced. The resulting heuristic explores only the most promising part of the permutation space and solves a handful of NLPs, in contrast to schemes based on integer variables, whose computational cost grows combi-natorially with the number of vehicles. Extensive experiments over a broad set of traffic scenarios show that our approach recovers most of the optimality gap of first-in-first-out (FIFO) scheduling. The extension of this approach to make it suitable for dynamic environments in which vehicles keep arriving at the intersection and leaving it is studied in the companion paper [31]; and a real-time algorithm is proposed in companion paper [32].

III. S CHEDULE -REFINING HEURISTIC 

A popular heuristic consists in fixing the schedule based on a FIFO approach, i.e., by ordering the vehicles based on their arrival time in the absence of the other vehicles. This approach is very simple, computationally inexpensive, and would arguably deliver a very good solution in case Ji = Jj

for all i, j . However, in the general case, the costs differ, and this approach can yield schedules that are far from optimal, as we will also illustrate later in our numerical simulations. 3

An alternative heuristic solves a quadratic approximation of (5) to compute a schedule [29]. Even though solvers for quadratic mixed-integer programs are becoming increasingly efficient, the problems they solve are combinatorial and still do not scale well as the number of vehicles becomes large. We therefore propose a heuristic based on local information about optimality while avoiding computationally demanding algorithms. A rejected pair is excluded only until a swap is accepted, i.e., the next candidate is the pair with the next-largest multiplier for the same schedule. Unlike algorithms for mixed-integer optimization, both Heuristic 1 and 2 greedily explore the space of permutations by targeting the pairs with the largest expected impact on the cost. 4

This drastically reduces the computational burden, at the price of obtaining possibly suboptimal solutions. These two variants share the same basic mechanism and differ only in the policy used to update the admissible swap set, and their performance will be compared through simulations in Section V-C. IV. E. Comparison against rule-based and reservation-based heuristics 

Finally, we compare the proposed heuristic against represen-tatives of the two classes of computationally light coordination schemes discussed in the introduction, over the same 100 randomized 6-vehicle scenarios considered in Section V-C. Rule-based policies fix the crossing order through predefined priorities, independently of the vehicles’ objectives [12]. As a representative of this class, we evaluate a static right-of-way convention in which main-road approaches take prece-dence over secondary ones, with vehicles ordered by arrival within each class. The reservation-based baseline implements the first-come-first-served scheme of [14]: a central manager processes vehicles in arrival order and grants each one the earliest conflict-free occupancy slot no earlier than its free-flow crossing interval, and each vehicle then plans its trajectory individually so as to comply with the granted slot. In both cases, the resulting schedules are evaluated with the objective of (6), so that all methods are directly comparable. Figure 11 reports the total cost across all scenarios. Both baselines are consistently outperformed: on average, the right-of-way rule is approximately 2.7× and the reservation-based scheme approximately 6× more expensive than FIFO, as cost-blind or-dering and rigid slot allocation, respectively, penalize precisely the vehicles with the largest weights. We remark that these schemes target throughput and provable collision avoidance rather than the objective of (6), and are considerably cheaper to evaluate; the comparison thus quantifies the performance they forgo when vehicles have heterogeneous objectives. The proposed heuristic attains the lowest cost in all 100 scenarios, with an average cost reduction of 51 .2% with respect to FIFO. Although the proposed heuristic is orders of magnitude faster than the mixed-integer formulations, its runtime is not yet compatible with real-time operation, since the schedule is recomputed from scratch for a fixed set of vehicles and every candidate swap requires a full NLP solve. Both aspects can be improved: the schedule can be updated incrementally as ve-hicles arrive, within a receding-horizon scheme, and the NLP re-solves can be replaced by sensitivity-based approximations that exploit the bi-level structure of the problem. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We have presented an optimization-based framework for co-ordinating autonomous vehicles at unsignalized intersections. Having formulated the overall control problem as a nonlin-ear program, we have proposed a Lagrange-multiplier-based heuristic to address the combinatorial nature of the vehicles crossing order scheduling. By exploiting sensitivity informa-tion available for free from the KKT conditions, our technique refines vehicle crossing orders through targeted swaps, avoid-ing exhaustive enumeration or combinatorial optimization. Evaluated against brute-force search, FIFO scheduling, and mixed-integer formulations on extensive numerical sim-ulations, our scheme has demonstrated several advantages. Specifically, it usually requires significantly lower computa-tional time compared to a mixed-integer setup and brute-force search, while achieving a comparable performance in terms of cost minimization, and improving crossing order over the basic FIFO approach. The proposed architecture then enables efficient approximation of optimal schedules while preserving computational tractability, making it suitable for real-time intersection coordination. Future work will extend the framework in two directions that build on each other. First, the heuristic can be embedded in a receding-horizon scheme in which vehicles enter the coordination area over time, so that the crossing order is updated incrementally at each arrival rather than recomputed from scratch. Second, the computational cost of the swap evaluations can be reduced by exploiting the bi-level structure of the problem and the parametric sensitivities of the vehicle trajectory problems, with the aim of executing the reordering within the sampling time.
