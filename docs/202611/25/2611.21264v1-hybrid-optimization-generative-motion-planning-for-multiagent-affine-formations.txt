# Hybrid Optimization-Generative Motion Planning for Multiagent Affine Formations 

Zhonggang Li, Hongyu Zhou, and Raj Thilak Rajan 

Abstract — Formation maneuver control enables a multiagent system to cooperatively navigate through constrained environ-ments in collective motion. While various formation controllers exist, they rely heavily on high-quality and dynamically feasible reference trajectories provided a priori, making formation motion planning (FMP) essential. To address the computational challenges for high-dimensional joint trajectories faced by con-ventional constrained optimization-based solutions, this paper introduces a hybrid optimization-generative framework with a focus on affine formations, which provide the necessary spatial flexibility. By formulating the planning task as a constrained Maximum-a-Posteriori estimation, we decouple multiagent ob-jectives to leverage pre-trained single-agent diffusion priors for dynamic feasibility, obstacle avoidance, and smoothness. We then interleave the generative process with projected gradient descent (PGD) to strictly enforce affine geometric constraints with safety guarantees. Simulations demonstrate our framework generates highly smooth, collision-free plans that are readily trackable by common distributed controllers, and outperforms state-of-the-art planners in various metrics. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Formation maneuvering is an essential task for multi-robotic systems, aiming to navigate a swarm of robots safely through a potentially cluttered area while maintaining a desired range of geometries. This is often desired in applica-tions such as cooperative object transport [1], satellite inter-ferometry [2], sensing and coverage [3], [4], etc. Recently, deformable formations [5], [6], [7] have received increasing attention due to the advantages of flexible geometries for complex environments, over conventional rigid body control [8]. In particular, affine formations [6], [9], [10], includ-ing translation, rotation, scaling, and shear, have become a popular expressive class. Predominant formation control literature solves stabilization and tracking of maneuvers assuming given references, i.e., the trajectories are assumed to be provided a priori and are inherently collision-free and dynamically feasible. Moreover, in common distributed control [6], followers rely solely on local information. Conse-quently, if the planned trajectories deviate from a strict affine transformation, the formation cannot be resolved locally, leading to severe tracking degradation. Hence, enforcing affine geometry is essential for such distributed controllers.

Generating a full set of high-quality reference trajectories subject to complex geometry constraints is difficult. Practical approaches typically decouple the problem hierarchically by pairing a simple global path planner (typically artificial po-tential field (APF) or A*) for the swarm centroid or a leader with a reactive local controller to resolve collisions and geometry [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]. For instance, [12] plans for a formation that allows rotation and anisotropic scaling of the geometry, and it uses APF to traverse a feasible global path for the centroid with coarse shape planning, followed by a model predictive controller (MPC) that refines the shape geometry and resolves collision avoidance at runtime. This paradigm shifts the heavy sensing and computational burden of collision avoidance and geometric maintenance entirely onto the local controller. Consequently, the required complex online solvers can easily overwhelm the limited onboard resources, such as on lightweight or space robots. More fundamentally, this approach is bottlenecked by the quality of the reference trajectory, making it difficult to apply common tracking controllers such as [6], which will inevitably lead to suboptimal maneuvers or tracking failure. An alternative paradigm to mitigate complexity is to plan within a low-dimensional space parameterized by the formation’s macroscopic geometric variables [1], [16], [17]. Since geometric coupling intrinsically binds the agents, a 2D affine transformation allows the entire swarm’s configuration to be fully described by just 6 degrees of freedom (DOF). Leveraging this reduction, [1], [17] optimize trajectories strictly within the 7-DOF 3D similarity transformation space, while [16] restricts planning to a 3-DOF 2D rigid body trans-formation. However, planning exclusively within the geo-metric parameter space is highly susceptible to disturbances. Small perturbations in the macroscopic parameters are often significantly amplified when mapped to the individual agents, resulting in severe non-smoothness and dynamic infeasibility in the actual trajectory domain. As such, these geometric ab-stractions must be paired with advanced auxiliary space-time smoothers in the trajectory domain. For example, [17] devel-ops a spatiotemporal polynomial optimization framework to guarantee smooth, dynamically feasible flight, embedded in the precomputed geometric references. Recently, learning-based motion planning has received increasing attention, such as reinforcement learning-based FMP [18]. In particular, generative models such as dif-fusion models [19], [20] have been successfully applied to robot motion planning [21], [22], [23]. The idea is to internalize the complex, non-convex motion constraints into learned structural priors through a large amount of arXiv:2611.21264v1 [cs.RO] 16 Nov 2026 data. Then, during inference, we sample from this learned prior with mild intervention from conventional optimization using gradient descent for task-specific objectives. Compared with conventional approaches, this optimization-generative method is computationally tractable, with better smoothness and reachability in cluttered settings [22]. However, this approach is currently limited to single-agent planning, as multiagent trajectory data is much more difficult to collect for an arbitrary number of mobile agents. As such, pioneering works for general multiagent motion planning with diffusion models [24] mostly focus on higher-level priority coordina-tion based on single-agent priors. Moreover, diffusion-based motion planning for multiagent flexible formation maneuvers remains unexplored. In this work, we propose a diffusion-based motion plan-ning framework specifically for multiagent affine formations. We formulate the FMP task as a constrained Maximum-a-Posteriori (MAP) estimation, which naturally translates to a hybrid constrained optimization with generative priors. By decoupling the high-dimensional multiagent objectives, we can leverage pre-trained single-agent diffusion priors to fulfill intra-agent requirements (such as smoothness and obstacle avoidance), while resolving inter-agent interactions, i.e., the formation, through projected gradient descent (PGD). Addi-tionally, a safety distance among agents can be guaranteed by configuring the affine geometry parameters in the projection step. Our proposed approach avoids the need for complex reactive controllers or additional spatiotemporal trajectory smoothers as in conventional methods. The generated trajec-tories are smooth, strictly affine, and executable by common distributed tracking controllers. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The general problem formulation and single-agent motion planning with diffusion is introduced in Section II. Then the proposed FMP framework with formation projection and safety guarantees is discussed in Section III. Finally, we validate our algorithm and the quality of the planned trajectories in comparison with state-of-the-art methods in Section IV. We then conclude the discussion with insights into the future directions in Section V. Throughout the paper, we adhere to the following notation conventions for mathematical clarity.

C. Results and Discussions 

We first show the process of the proposed diffusion-based planner in Fig. 2, where the trajectories are gradually de-noised from random Gaussian initialization to clean samples. Observe that at initial steps, e.g., Step 10, there are still collisions with the obstacles, but the trajectories are nudged away from the obstacles as a mutual effort of the diffusion priors and the cost gradients. Next, we show a few more examples of the proposed solutions and the state-of-the-art for both the planned and controller-tracked trajectories in Fig. 3. The proposed solu-tion does not lead to deterministic results, as can be seen in the top row of Fig. 3, where the swarm could either pass from the left or right corridor given different random-ness. This multi-modality is favored in motion planning for reachability and avoiding local minima. From a trajectory quality perspective, the proposed planner and [17] yield visibly smoother and more natural trajectories compared to [12], which relies on a coarse artificial potential field (APF) planner. From a formation constraints perspective, the proposed method admits rigorous affine transformation of the nominal template, and [12] can also maintain its desired geometry, i.e., rotation with anisotropic scaling. However, [17] trades off the formation geometry for maneuverability across the passages, as formation is never strictly enforced in their framework. From a tracking control perspective, the planned trajectories overlap with the controlled trajectories after initial convergence from random positions, which shows trackability for the proposed planner. This work proposes a multiagent motion planning frame-work under affine formation constraints. The planning mixes projected gradient descent (PGD) and diffusion priors under a MAP formulation, where the diffusion prior is learned from the single-agent trajectory data and is shared across the agents for individual objectives, and PGD tackles the inter-agent interactions while maintaining feasible formations. Numerical results indicate our planner yields smooth and dynamically feasible trajectories under strict affine formation constraints with safety guarantees. The planner also admits moderate computation complexity which is tractable as the swarm size scale.
