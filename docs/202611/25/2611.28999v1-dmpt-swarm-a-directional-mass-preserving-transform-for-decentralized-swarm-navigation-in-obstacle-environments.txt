# DMPT-Swarm: A Directional Mass-Preserving Transform for Decentralized Swarm Navigation in Obstacle Environments 

Jacob Lee 1, Samuel Sutphin 1, Haroon Malik 2, Chang Liu 3, Pingping Zhu 1,2∗

Abstract — This paper presents a decentralized microscopic control framework for swarm navigation in obstacle environ-ments, given a precomputed macroscopic trajectory represented by Gaussian mixture models (GMMs). To generate obstacle-aware reference locations without inter-agent communication, we first employ affine transport between multivariate normal (MVN) distributions under the Wasserstein-2 metric. We then propose a directional mass-preserving transform (DMPT) to incorporate obstacle effects by modifying radial transport while preserving directional structure. Based on the transformed ref-erences, a local artificial potential field (APF)-based controller is applied for navigation. Each agent operates independently, re-ducing communication and computational burden. Simulation results demonstrate improved efficiency and obstacle avoidance compared to conventional APF-based methods. 

I. INTRODUCTION Robotic swarm intelligence has emerged as a promising research area with applications in path planning, coverage, formation control, and navigation, ranging from autonomous drone swarms to large-scale fleets of mobile sensing systems. As the number of agents in a robotic swarm (RS) system increases, scalability and communication efficiency become critical challenges. Existing swarm control methods are generally categorized into centralized and decentralized approaches. In centralized approaches, a global controller governs all agents by rep-resenting the swarm through a macroscopic state, typically modeled as spatial distributions such as Gaussian mixture models (GMMs) [1]–[5] or their variants, e.g., skew-normal mixture models (SNMMs) [6]. These methods effectively address scalability at the macroscopic level. However, they require extensive data sharing among agents and the central unit, making them dependent on reliable, high-bandwidth communication, which is often impractical in real-world applications such as outdoor exploration or security-sensitive missions. In contrast, decentralized approaches enable agents to make decisions individually or within local neighbor-hoods, thereby reducing communication requirements. Most existing distribution-based swarm control frame-works adopt a two-layer hierarchical structure, consisting of macroscopic planning and microscopic control. At the macroscopic level, the swarm is represented by time-varying distributions, and planning is conducted in the probabilistic space to generate a desired trajectory of distributions. At the microscopic level, individual agents are controlled to follow this distribution. Although control inputs are applied at the agent level, such methods remain fundamentally cen-tralized because the microscopic control laws depend on global distribution-level objectives and require knowledge of other agents’ states to minimize distribution discrepancies. This implicit coupling introduces significant communication overhead and limits scalability. To address these challenges, this paper develops a fully decentralized microscopic control framework for swarm nav-igation in obstacle-populated environments, given a pre-computed macroscopic distribution trajectory from existing centralized planners [1]–[6]. The key idea is to decouple the global distribution-following objective into independent local control tasks without requiring inter-agent communication. First, we employ an affine transform between multivari-ate normal (MVN) distributions, which corresponds to the optimal transport map under the Wasserstein-2 metric in obstacle-free environments, to generate reference locations for each agent. Next, we propose a novel directional mass-preserving transform (DMPT) to incorporate the effects of obstacles. The DMPT modifies the transport along radial directions while preserving directional structure, enabling ef-ficient approximation of obstacle-aware skewed distributions. Finally, a local artificial potential field (APF)-based control law is designed to drive each agent toward its reference location while avoiding collisions. In the proposed framework, each agent computes its control input independently and locally, without requiring communication with other agents or a central unit. This significantly reduces computational complexity and commu-nication burden compared to existing microscopic control strategies. The main contributions of this paper are summa-rized as follows: 1. Directional Mass-Preserving Transform: We propose a novel transform for approximating obstacle-induced skew-normal distributions and generating obstacle-aware reference locations for individual agents. 2. Decentralized Microscopic Control: We develop a arXiv:2611.28999v1 [cs.RO] 21 Nov 2026 fully decentralized control framework that eliminates inter-agent communication while maintaining distribution-level coordination. 3. Validation and Performance Evaluation: We validate the proposed DMPT and demonstrate the effectiveness of the control framework through numerical simulations. II. CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORKS This paper presents a decentralized microscopic control framework for swarm navigation in obstacle environments using a directional mass-preserving transform. By combin-ing affine transport under the Wasserstein-2 metric with the proposed DMPT, obstacle-aware reference locations are generated without inter-agent communication. A local APF-based controller then enables fully decentralized execution. The proposed framework decouples global distribution track-ing into independent local control problems, significantly reducing communication and computational burden. Simu-lation results demonstrate improved efficiency and energy performance compared to conventional APF-based methods. The current framework is developed under the relatively strong Assumption 1 , in which the obstacle-avoidance direc-tion of each agent is constrained to align with the direction toward the mean. This assumption improves tractability, but may limit the flexibility of the proposed transform in more complex environments. A promising direction for future research is to develop a dynamic DMPT in which the transport direction can adapt to the motion of individual agents and the local obstacle configuration. Future work will also investigate extensions to dynamic environments with online updates, theoretical guarantees on convergence and stability, and validation on real robotic platforms. VIII.
