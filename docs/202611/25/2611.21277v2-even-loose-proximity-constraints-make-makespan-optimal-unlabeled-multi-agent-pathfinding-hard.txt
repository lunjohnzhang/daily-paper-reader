Abstract 

We study r-Connected Unlabeled Multi-agent Pathfind-ing ( rCUMAPF), a variant of MAPF where interchangeable agents have a proximity range r ∈ N, and agents must form a single group in a given graph G through limited-range com-munication at all times. The problem arises in fundamental applications such as self-reconfiguration and platooning, while standard MAPF does not capture proximity constraints. For the problem of finding a makespan-optimal solution, prior work shows NP -hardness for r = 1 and polynomial-time solvability when r is sufficiently large; the complexity for the intermediate case 2 ≤ r is largely unknown. To narrow this gap, we show that finding a solution to rCUMAPF with makespan at most 

2 is NP -hard for 2 ≤ r = O(|V |1−ε), where 0 < ε ≤ 1

is a fixed constant and |V | is the number of vertices. As a consequence, we show that the labeled analogue of rCUMAPF is also NP -hard under the same condition. 

## 1 Introduction 

Research on Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) has attracted considerable attention in the last decade, driven by the growing demand for modern cooperative multi-robot systems. MAPF aims to find a set of collision-free paths on a given graph 

G = ( V, E ), or a plan , from initial positions to target positions for multiple agents. Standard MAPF assumes discrete time, allows each agent to remain at its current vertex or move to an adjacent vertex at each time step, and requires plans to avoid the following two types of collisions : (i) vertex collisions, where more than one agent occupies the same vertex v ∈ V

at the same time, and (ii) swap collisions, where two agents traverse an edge e ∈ E at the same time. In response to diverse requirements, recent work proceeds to study variants of MAPF (see, e.g., (Stern et al. 2019; Zahrádka et al. 2026)), since a prescribed standard formula-tion may not always capture constraints required in practical applications, such as safety (Atzmon et al. 2020; Hönig et al. 2018), uncertainty (Ma, Kumar, and Koenig 2017), and lim-ited inter-agent communication range (i.e., integrity) (Maoudj and Christensen 2024). This paper also studies one of the variants, namely the proximity-constrained variant, since such constraints arise in collaborative work in space (Liu et al. 2026), autonomous exploration at disaster sites (Tranzatto et al. 2024), and scenarios involving agents with a finite sensing range (Carli, Salaris, and Giordano 2024). For r ∈ N,the proximity constraint requires the occupied vertices to induce a connected subgraph of Gr at every time step, where 

Gr joins vertices at distance at most r in G. We say r is the 

proximity range . One can see that this proximity constraint naturally captures the requirement that any two agents can exchange information through multi-hop communication. Given that finding optimal solutions for labeled MAPF is 

NP -hard regarding various metrics (Yu and LaValle 2013b), our complextiy study focuses on the unlabeled variant, which admits a polynomial-time makespan-optimal algorithm (Yu and LaValle 2013a), where makespan denotes the length of the plan. In the unlabeled setting, every pair of agents is interchangeable, and the positions of agents are given by a set of vertices. Together with the proximity constraint, we study r-Connected Unlabeled Multi-agent Pathfinding (rCUMAPF), which seeks a plan between given initial and target vertex sets with minimum makespan. See Section 2 for a formal definition of our problem. Although the importance of rCUMAPF is underscored by a wide range of applications—such as autonomous pla-tooning (Martínez-Díaz et al. 2021) and programmable mat-ter (Hinnenthal, Liedtke, and Scheideler 2024)—only limited complexity results are known for this problem. For the ex-tremal cases of makespan optimization, Fekete et al. (2023) show that deciding whether there is a plan for 1CUMAPF with makespan at most 2 is NP -complete, and the case r = D(G),where D(G) is the diameter of G, can be reduced to unla-beled MAPF (Yu and LaValle 2013a), which is known to be solvable in polynomial time. Moreover, existing suboptimal algorithms (Fekete et al. 2023; Suzuki and Okumura 2026) only consider 1CUMAPF, and they cannot be applied for 

r ≥ 2, which evidently reveals the challenge of extending the results to the general case. Reflecting these results, a natural question arises from the viewpoint of computational complexity: does rCUMAPF for the case 2 ≤ r < D (G)

admit a polynomial-time algorithm, or is it NP -hard?

## 2 Preliminaries 

2.1 Notation 

Let G be a simple, undirected, unweighted, and finite con-nected graph. We write the vertex set and the edge set of G

as V (G) and E(G), respectively. For a vertex v ∈ V (G), the 

open neighborhood is NG(v) = {w ∈ V (G) | vw ∈ E(G)}.The maximum degree of G is ∆( G) = max v∈V (G) |NG(v)|.For a vertex subset S ⊆ V (G), we denote the subgraph induced by S as G[S]. We use [i, j ] to denote the set 

{i, i + 1 , . . . , j − 1, j } of consecutive integers for i ≤ j.The distance dist (u, v ) is the number of edges in a shortest 

u–v path. A path P = ( pi)ki=0 has length k and vertex set 

V (P ) = {pi | i ∈ [0 , k ]}.

## 3 Related Work 

Beyond classical MAPF, practical settings motivate additional requirements, including alternative collision definitions (Li et al. 2019; Surynek, Kumar, and Koenig 2019; Suzuki, Tamura, and Okumura 2026) and constraints on configura-tions (Li et al. 2020; Tateo et al. 2018; Hönig et al. 2018). Among these, several studies consider constraints motivated by limited communication between agents and integration: Tateo et al. (2018) study communication constraints in a model where physical movement and communication are represented separately; Li et al. (2020) introduce a soft constraint for main-taining a specific formation; and Otaki et al. (2020) address a platooning scenario with an objective that considers air resis-tance. Recent work also considers planning with graph-based proximity constraints, as follows. For rCUMAPF, Fekete et al. (2023) show that deciding whether a plan with makespan 2 ex-ists for 1CUMAPF is NP -complete, even on empty 2D grids, and provide polynomial-time constant-factor approximation algorithms for such grids. For rCMAPF, Fioravantes et al. (2026) provide parameterized complexity results, and Fekete et al. (2024) establish makespan lower bounds and provide constant-factor approximation algorithms for restricted cases. In contrast, our work considers the unlabeled setting with an arbitrary proximity range r.

4.3 Algorithmic consequences. 

There is a known algorithm for deciding whether a given instance admits a valid plan of length at most 1 in polyno-mial time (Fekete et al. 2023). A plan of makespan at most two is a polynomial-size certificate whose validity can be checked in polynomial time. Thus, Theorem 1 establishes a sharp complexity boundary for rCUMAPF: the bounded-makespan variant of rCUMAPF is polynomial-time solvable for makespan at most one and NP -complete for makespan at most two. Equivalently, it is NP -hard to compute an interme-diate configuration Q such that (S, Q, T ) is a valid plan, or to correctly report that no such configuration exists. This holds even when ∆( G) ≤ 3, for every integer r ≥ 2 satisfying 

r = O(|V (G)|1−ε). The hardness result yields some conse-quences regarding the hardness of approximation, hardness for sublinear proximity ranges, hardness for rCMAPF, and running-time lower bounds.

Every formula produced by these transformations has O(n) variables and clauses. Consequently, the latter re-stricted satisfiability problem on n variables cannot be solved in 2o(f (n)) time either. r = max {2, ⌈n(1 −ε)/ε ⌉} . The resulting graph has |V (G)| =Θ( rn ) = Θ( n1/ε ), and hence n = Θ( |V (G)|ε). Hence, there is a constant c > 0 such that c|V (G)|ε ≤ n, where n is the number of variables in the instance of 3-SAT. For this constant 

c, a 2o(f (c|V (G)|ε)) -time algorithm for computing an optimal plan would decide this restricted satisfiability problem in 

2o(f (n)) time by testing whether the returned makespan is at most 2, contradicting the assumption.
