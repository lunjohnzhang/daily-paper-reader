# Sampling at Scale: Distributed Multi-Agent Trajectory Optimization 

Minchan Jung 1, Dimitrios S. Georgiou 1, Augustinos D. Saravanos 2 and Evangelos A. Theodorou 1

Abstract — Multi-agent trajectory optimization poses two cou-pled challenges: effectively exploring non-convex landscapes and scaling favorably to large teams of agents. Sampling-based methods are natural candidates for the exploration challenge but scale poorly with problem dimension. Moreover, their off-the-shelf integration into distributed optimization introduces a fundamental trade-off: sampling either fails to address the constraint-induced non-convexity or its search space grows with neighborhood size. We address this gap by introducing a Distributed Sampling-based Trajectory Optimization (DS-TO) architecture built on a splitting mechanism tailored to sampling. Under this splitting, each agent samples only in its own control space, independent of neighborhood size, while still remaining exposed to both the constraint-induced non-convexity and the decisions of other agents. The resulting algorithm is fully decentralized, requiring only neighborhood communication, with all local updates executed in parallel across agents. Efficient variations, including parallel Jacobi updates within each agent’s subproblem and single-step con-straint linearization, further reduce computation. Experiments on 2D and 3D tasks with up to 1024 agents demonstrate improved exploration and constraint satisfaction over state-of-the-art derivative-based distributed optimization, and superior scalability to existing distributed sampling methods. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Cooperative trajectory planning is fundamental to the safe and efficient operation of robot teams in shared en-vironments, such as aerial swarms navigating clutter [1], autonomous vehicles traversing intersections [2], and ware-house fleets transporting goods [3]. In such settings, robots must plan trajectories that optimize task performance subject to their dynamics, actuation limits, and collision avoidance with obstacles and other robots. These requirements render planning non-convex, coupling decisions through nonlinear dynamics across the horizon and inducing competing local minima among routes and passing orders. At the same time, scale compounds the difficulty: the computational cost of coordinating these coupled trajectories grows rapidly with team size. Meeting these requirements calls for methods that jointly address scalability and non-convexity. Coordination has been pursued through multi-agent path finding [4], reactive feedback methods [5], learned policies [6], and dynamic games [7]; however, these approaches do not jointly optimize the team’s trajectories. In contrast, trajectory optimization (TO) plans all agents’ trajectories through a joint optimal control problem, yet solving that

Dis-tributed approaches address this bottleneck by decomposing the full problem into local agent-level subproblems solved in parallel and coordinated through neighborhood commu-nication. Some of the most prominent ones build on the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) [8], a well-established distributed optimization framework. Such methods have enabled distributed model predictive control [9], [10], collaborative manipulation [11] and legged loco-motion [12], as well as distributed TO architectures scaling to thousands of agents [13], [14]. Nevertheless, these methods typically rely on derivative-based local solvers, remaining prone to initialization and poor local minima. Sampling-based derivative-free methods such as model predictive path integral (MPPI) [15] and the cross entropy method (CEM) [16] are natural candidates for such non-convex landscapes. Although such methods are widely used in the single-agent setting [17], [18], their sample efficiency deteriorates as the search space grows, fundamentally limit-ing their extension to large-scale multi-agent problems. Some notable attempts include ADMM schemes that sample joint neighborhood trajectories [19], [20], belief propagation for distributed inference [21], and decentralized MPPI account-arXiv:2611.31561v1 [cs.RO] 23 Nov 2026 ing for neighbors through predicted trajectories [22]. The effectiveness of distributed sampling schemes hinges on two factors: the dimension of each local search and the safety information available to evaluate rollouts. On one side, jointly sampling ego and neighboring controls [19], [20] exposes the sampler to all constraints but inflates the search dimension. On the other hand, splittings that isolate agent dynamics [13], [23] would keep the sampled dimension small but hide the constraints inside auxiliary copies, leaving the sampler to explore a surrogate objective blind to the true safety landscape. Some decentralized MPPI schemes [24] avoid the trade-off entirely, but only by abandoning coordination through a joint problem, treating neighbors as fixed predictions rather than co-optimizing agents. The core challenge is therefore to retain direct exploration of the non-convex safety landscape and joint trajectory coordination while sampling only each agent’s own controls. We propose Distributed Sampling-based Trajectory Optimization (DS-TO) , an ADMM-based distributed sampling-based TO framework that retains constraint-aware exploration while sampling only each agent’s own controls. The proposed formulation separates dynamically feasible primal trajectories from local copies subject to safety con-straints, with global consensus variables enforcing consis-tency between the two. Soft constraint penalties in the primal objective let stochastic optimizers explore the non-convex safety landscape directly, while the local copies coordinate constraint satisfaction without entering the sampled search, keeping its dimension independent of neighborhood size. Instantiating the primal solver with MPPI, our experiments demonstrate strong constraint satisfaction, improved explo-ration in non-convex scenarios, and favorable scaling relative to state-of-the-art distributed TO baselines (Fig. 1). B. First Block: Agent-wise Local Subproblem 

In the first ADMM block, each agent independently min-imizes Li over its primal and local-copy variables (Zi, ˜Zai ),while keeping the global and dual variables fixed. The opti-mization variables remain coupled through the soft penalty terms βhc(hij,t ) in Φi. Jointly solving for these variables cannot exploit distinct structures: the primal variables are constrained only by the dynamics, whereas the local copies are subject to hard constraints but timewise independent. We therefore use a block-coordinate update to separate updates, yielding subproblems tailored to their respective structures. The block-coordinate update can follow either a Jacobi or Gauss–Seidel (GS) scheme; we use Jacobi by default, allowing both blocks to use previous iterates and perform the computationally intensive updates in parallel within each agent. The ADMM penalties maintain coupling between the two blocks through the shared consensus variables. We describe the solver choices and approximations used in DS-TO. These choices are specific to our implementation, and alternatives can be used within the same framework. 

A. MPPI for Constraint-Aware Exploration 

We employ MPPI to solve (11), sampling each agent’s control sequence T × nu and generating dynamically fea-sible rollouts in parallel. The soft penalties βg c(gi,t ) and 

βhc(hij,t ) guide dynamically feasible exploration of the non-convex constraint landscape. Penalty evaluations can be performed in parallel across samples and constraints without requiring any constraint derivatives. Note that a convergence guarantee accounting for the inexact stochastic MPPI updates lies beyond the scope of this work. 

B. Constraint Linearization and Relaxation 

The local copy variables are warm-started from the global consensus variables, which also serve as the proximal targets in (12) and carry information from the preceding primal up-date through (13). This motivates normalizing the local-copy penalties to preserve the influence of explored primal trajec-tories. Although (12) decomposes across time, fully solving each nonlinear subproblem requires multiple iterations. We instead linearize the constraints and omit the interagent soft penalty βhc(hij,t ), yielding a small QP independent of the current primal update and relinearize at the next ADMM iteration. Since the omitted penalty and its gradient vanish at consensus, this does not alter the stationarity conditions. Since global consensus may violate the constraints, lin-earization around consensus variables can yield an infeasible subproblem. We therefore introduce slack variables initial-ized from the current violations to ensure a feasible relaxed subproblem. We do not warm-start the local solver dual variables, avoiding bias toward the previous active set. V. E XPERIMENTS

We proposed DS-TO, a highly parallel and fully decentral-ized framework for multi-agent trajectory optimization. DS-TO combines sampling-based optimization in each agent’s control space with locally optimized views of interacting agents’ trajectories, which are reconciled through decentral-ized consensus of ADMM. This separates constraint-aware exploration from multi-agent coordination without requiring a centralized optimizer. Across the considered tasks, DS-TO maintained high solution feasibility in challenging non-convex multi-agent problems and scaled to large locally connected teams, including a team of 1024 drones. While this work focuses on MPPI as the local solver, the framework remains well suited to other sampling-based methods. Future work will explore integrating more ad-vanced training-free [28] or learning-based diffusion/flow-based samplers [29] to further enhance performance.
