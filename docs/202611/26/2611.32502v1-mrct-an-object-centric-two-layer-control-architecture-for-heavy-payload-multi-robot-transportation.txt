MRCT: An Object-Centric Two-Layer Control Architecture for
Heavy-Payload Multi-Robot Transportation
Ning Wang1, Yoshihiko Nakamura2, Tianwei Zhang1,3∗
Abstract—Cooperative transportation of heavy payloads by
heterogeneous mobile manipulators requires coordinated payload
motion, force distribution, and compliant physical interaction.
This paper presents MRCT, an object-centric two-layer control
architecture for multi-robot transportation. At the object level,
a model predictive controller (MPC) generates a coordinated
contact-force reference for the payload. At the robot level, whole-
body impedance control (WBIC) modules on individual robots
realise the reference through a confidence-based blending of
velocity-tracking and impedance torques. The blending mecha-
nism emphasises tracking when the joint-velocity error is small
and increases compliance when tracking degrades because of
abrupt reference changes or physical disturbances. Simulation and
hardware experiments with a heterogeneous team comprising a
single-arm and a dual-arm mobile manipulator transporting a
near-rated table payload demonstrate accurate object tracking,
regulated contact forces, and stable payload orientation. Parameter
sensitivity studies, obstacle-avoidance transportation, and step-load
disturbance experiments further demonstrate the effectiveness of
MRCT for reliable cooperative transportation under the evaluated
conditions.
I. INTRODUCTION
Cooperative transportation by multiple robots enables the
handling of heavy or bulky objects beyond the capability of a
single platform. This capability is important in logistics, manu-
facturing, and service robotics, where payload motion, contact
forces, and robot configurations must be coordinated under
actuation and safety constraints. Heavy-payload transportation
is particularly challenging because the controller must regulate
object motion, distribute interaction forces among robots with
different capabilities, and remain effective under modelling
errors, contact uncertainty, and external disturbances [1].
A central challenge is balancing trajectory tracking with
physical interaction safety. Robots must remain sufficiently
compliant to prevent impulsive contact, force amplification, and
possible structural damage, while retaining enough stiffness to
support the payload and preserve its pose during motion [2].
This trade-off becomes more difficult for heterogeneous mobile
manipulators, whose different morphologies, actuation limits,
and contact configurations complicate whole-body motion co-
ordination and force realisation. Practical transportation tasks
further require operation under obstacle constraints, manual
reference changes, and varying payload loads.
Existing work addresses cooperative transportation through
task and motion planning [3], payload-aware trajectory opti-
misation [4], coupled object-level control [5], and distributed
or leader–follower coordination strategies [6], [7]. However,
many approaches either treat the mobile base and manipulator as
separate subsystems, rely on specialised coordination structures,

This paper presents MRCT, an object-centric two-layer con-

An object-level MPC first computes a contact-force reference
whose resultant defines a payload wrench reference. The re-

robot controllers, which independently execute local WBIC
modules. A confidence-based blending mechanism uses the
instantaneous joint-velocity tracking error to interpolate between
tracking and impedance torques: it prioritises precise motion
execution when tracking is reliable, and increases compliance
when abrupt references or physical disturbances cause larger
tracking deviations. This design permits a common payload-
level objective while allowing heterogeneous robots to respond
locally to contact conditions. Code will be released upon accep-
tance. The contributions of this paper are as follows:
• An object-centric two-layer architecture, termed MRCT,
that combines object-level MPC planning with robot-level
WBIC execution for heavy-payload transportation by het-
erogeneous mobile manipulators.
• A confidence-based torque blending strategy that inte-
grates velocity-tracking and impedance torques, enabling a
smooth trade-off between tracking accuracy and compliant

II. RELATED WORKS
Cooperative transportation has been studied through multi-
robot task and motion planning, payload-aware trajectory opti-
misation, and coordinated manipulation. For example, Motes et
al. [3] formulate multi-robot task and motion planning using
hypergraphs, while Kennel-Maushart and Coros [8] consider
payload-aware trajectory optimisation for non-holonomic mo-
bile multi-robot manipulation with tip-over avoidance. These
methods provide valuable task-level planning capabilities, but
often treat the mobile base and manipulators separately, which
can limit the whole-body coordination needed for force-sensitive
heavy-payload transport.
More integrated cooperative control approaches employ de-
centralised MPC, leader–follower architectures, or capability-

Compliant interaction is often achieved using admittance or
impedance control. Admittance control adapts motion according
to measured external forces but can be affected by feedback
delays during fast contact transitions [11], [12]. Impedance
control instead specifies a force–motion relationship and is
particularly suitable for fast local compliance in stiff physical
interactions [13]. Whole-body impedance control extends this
idea by coordinating full-body robot motion while regulating
task-space compliance [14]. It has been applied successfully
to legged and wheel-legged robots operating under uncertain
contact conditions [15], [16].

III. METHODS
Fig. 2 illustrates the proposed two-layer control framework
for heterogeneous cooperative transportation. The system oper-
ates on two levels. At the object level, a shared MPC module
receives the object’s current pose Pobj and desired trajectory
Pobj,d. It computes coordinated contact-force references and
desired contact-point poses for the next control step. This
information is broadcast to all robots, providing a unified global
objective.
At the robot level, each agent independently executes a
WBIC scheme for the contact point(s) it manages. The robot
first acquires its own state (base pose and joint angles), e.g., via
SLAM, motion capture and encoders, then computes the current
end-effector pose through forward kinematics. The resulting
pose error is fed simultaneously to two parallel controllers.
Specifically, a whole-body MPC generates desired joint veloci-
ties ˙q and base velocity v. The base velocity v is sent directly
to the base controller, while ˙q is passed to a joint-level PD
controller to produce a velocity-tracking torque τPD. At the
same time, an impedance controller uses the same pose error to
compute a compliance torque τimp.
These two torque components, τPD and τimp, are then inte-
grated via a confidence-based weighting mechanism to yield a
combined joint torque τd. The final joint torque command τ is
obtained by adding a feedforward term from the robot’s dynam-
ics and a compensation term τfd for the object’s transportation
force. This control logic is consistent across heterogeneous
platforms. For a single-arm robot, one contact point is managed.
For a dual-arm robot, the left and right arms are controlled
independently by separate WBIC instances, generating distinct
torque commands τl and τr.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATIONS
This section evaluates the proposed control framework in
simulation and on physical hardware. The hardware platform
consists of a heterogeneous robot team comprising a single-arm
and a dual-arm mobile manipulator. The single-arm robot is built
on an AGV base equipped with an Elfin 5 arm (payload capacity
5 kg), while the dual-arm robot uses an AGV base with two Elfin
3 arms (payload capacity 3 kg per arm). The transported object
is a rigid table of dimensions 1200 mm × 600 mm and weight
9.89 kg, and additional step-load disturbances are introduced
using two 500 g bottles placed on the tabletop. The grasping
configurations are shown in Fig. 3. Notably, neither robot
can complete the transportation task independently; successful
execution requires coordinated cooperative transportation under
the proposed object-level MPC and WBIC architecture.
To showcase the performance of the system in executing the
cooperative transportation task, three main evaluation metrics
are considered: position tracking accuracy, contact-force regu-
lation, and payload orientation stability.
Experiment I: Manual Trajectory Control Trajectories are
generated via joystick input to evaluate system responsiveness
and the performance under different control parameter settings.

A. Result of Experiment I
To analyze the impact of control parameters on system
performance, sensitivity analysis experiments are conducted in
the MuJoCo simulation environment. The study focuses on
both the fusion weight parameter and the impedance control
parameters by varying key configurations, and evaluates the
resulting tracking errors and contact force responses under
different conditions.
First, a sensitivity analysis is performed on the fusion weight
parameter σ in Eq. (10). The parameter σ is defined as a
proportion of the maximum joint velocity, and five different
values are selected for evaluation. The corresponding results
under different fusion settings are summarized in Table I.
Table I reports the sensitivity of MRCT to the blending scale
σ. Small σ values increase the impedance contribution, whereas
larger values retain the tracking contribution over a broader
error range. The results show a clear tracking–force trade-off,
and the intermediate setting used in the subsequent experiments
provides the most balanced performance.
Based on this, a sensitivity analysis is further conducted on
the impedance model parameters in Eq. (6). Multiple combina-
tions of (Kimp, Dimp) are evaluated, and the resulting tracking
errors and contact forces are summarised in Table II.
Fig. 4 illustrates a representative hybrid trial. The robots
track joystick commands to lift, translate, and rotate the table
while maintaining smooth object motion without visible os-
cillations or slippage. Position errors remain within 0.045 m
in all Cartesian directions and yaw errors within 0.105 rad,
while pitch and roll stay within ±0.018 rad and ±0.021 rad,
respectively. The contact force profiles in Fig. 5 show that forces
remain within approximately ±50 N for the single-arm robot

and ±25 N for the dual-arm robot during transportation, with
the impedance controller dissipating transient peaks and keeping
non-persistent spikes below 50 N. These results indicate that the
hybrid strategy achieves a favourable balance between trajectory
accuracy and interaction safety, combining the tracking benefits
of whole-body control with the force-limiting advantages of
impedance.
B. Result of Experiment II
Experiment II evaluates the proposed framework on physical
hardware using a predefined obstacle-avoidance trajectory. Five
real-world trials were conducted with the hybrid WBIC–MPC
controller. As summarised in Table III, the system maintains
accurate position tracking and orientation stability across all
trials, with performance metrics comparable to the simulation
results of Experiment I. The slightly improved tracking accuracy
relative to the joystick-driven case is mainly due to the smooth,
predefined trajectory, which avoids the abrupt command changes
inherent to manual operation.
Figure 6 shows a representative obstacle-avoidance trial.
The robots approach, lift, transport, and return the table while
following the desired path around four obstacles. Throughout the
execution, positional errors remain within 0.045 m in all Carte-
sian directions and yaw errors do not exceed 0.105 rad. The
payload maintains excellent stability, with pitch and roll angles
constrained within ±0.018 rad and ±0.021 rad, respectively.
These results confirm that the proposed control strategy transfers
reliably from simulation to real hardware and supports precise,
stable cooperative transportation in cluttered environments.
C. Result of Experiment III
To evaluate the robustness of the proposed framework,
Experiment III introduces step-load disturbances during coop-
erative transportation. Building on the joystick-based manual
control setup of Experiment I, two 500 g bottles are placed
on the table surface near the dual-arm robot at different times
during the task. As summarised in Table IV, the system
maintains stable performance under these dynamic conditions:
peak position tracking errors remain below 0.052 m with mean
errors under 0.019 m, while contact forces along the x-axis are
bounded within 54.22 N, 37.73 N, and 39.81 N for the three
contact points. The table’s orientation stability is preserved with
pitch and roll peaks not exceeding 0.020 rad and 0.035 rad,
respectively, indicating effective disturbance rejection.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper introduced MRCT, an object-centric two-layer
control architecture for heavy-payload cooperative transporta-
tion by heterogeneous mobile manipulators. MRCT combines
object-level MPC planning with local WBIC execution, where a
confidence-based torque blending mechanism balances velocity
tracking and compliant interaction. Simulation studies charac-
terised the effects of the blending and impedance parameters
on tracking and contact forces. Hardware experiments with a
single-arm and a dual-arm mobile manipulator demonstrated
accurate table transportation with regulated contact forces and
stable orientation during joystick-guided motion, obstacle avoid-
ance, and step-load disturbances.
