# Multi-Robot Bearing-only Pose Estimation via Angle Rigidity* 

J. Francisco Presenza 1, Leonardo J. Colombo 2, Ignacio Mas 3, and Juan I. Giribet 3

Abstract — This letter proposes a novel distributed bearing-based pose estimator for time-varying multi-robot systems. The method uses angles computed from body-frame bearings to estimate the robots’ positions in R3 without knowledge of their orientations. The orientations in SO(3) are recovered from the estimated positions, the bearings, and the bearing derivatives. The proposed observer only requires the (directed) sensing topology to be angle-rigid , a weaker condition than the commonly used ones like bearing rigidity. Local uniform exponential stability of the proposed observer is established under the assumption of persistently exciting motions for a subset of robots. Simulations are presented and discussed to evaluate the scheme’s effectiveness and practicality. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Distributed pose estimation using only inter-agent mea-surements is a fundamental problem in multi-robot systems. Over the past decade, extensive research has focused on 

bearing-based methods, which exploit line-of-sight direction measurements between robots. This interest is largely moti-vated by the fact that bearings can be extracted from low-cost visual sensors with minimal processing. Bearings are typically measured in each robot’s body frame, and to use them for position estimation, they are typically transformed into a common frame. To this end, the corresponding robot orientations in SO(3) are required. However, absolute orientation estimation is often difficult in practice, as commonly used magnetic sensors are highly susceptible to disturbances and may be unavailable in some scenarios. Alternatively, some strategies employ inter-agent relative rotation measurements to estimate the orientations; see, for example, [1], [2]. Nevertheless, measuring relative rotations is challenging in practice, as it often requires substantially more processing, calibration, or additional in-frastructure, such as fiducial markers. For these reasons, many works have proposed exploiting body-frame bearing measurements for orientation estimation. Early approaches relied on the infinitesimal bearing rigidity

On the other hand, [4], [5], [6], [7], operate on SO(3) but still exhibit limited applicability, especially under time-varying sensing networks. This is partly because these methods depend on carefully designed sensing topologies, involving (i) cascaded interaction graphs, i.e., robot i only uses information from robots j < i , and (ii) reciprocal bearing measurements, i.e., 

i measures j if and only if j measures i. Additionally, a key limitation of existing bearing-based methods, including [3], [4], [5], [6], [7], is that each robot must continuously measure at least one (or two) bearing(s) for its orientation in 

S1 (SO(3) ) to be observable. This sensing requirement limit the robots’ visual sensors from being used for other tasks, such as mapping, target tracking, etc. More recently, angle rigidity has emerged as a promising avenue for network localization; see [8], [9], [10]. Angle measurements, which are obtained from the inner product of measured bearings, are independent of the robots’ orienta-tions. Hence, positions can be estimated without knowledge of orientation. Due to this, angle rigidity imposes milder conditions on the sensing topology than bearing rigidity; see [11]. Specifically, angle-based localization relaxes the requirement on the minimum number of measured bearings per robot. However, several shortcomings prevent their use in real-world applications. For example, most of them are only applicable in R2, or, to provide global convergence, they require special graph topologies, such as triangular in R2 and tetrahedral in R3. In addition, existing angle-based methods do not provide orientation estimation, which is required—or at least highly beneficial—in many robotic applications. This work introduces a novel distributed method for es-timating time-varying SE(3) poses of multi-robot systems using only body-frame bearing measurements. The observer leverages a position estimator based on the angles computed from bearings, whose output, together with the bearings and their velocities, is used for orientation estimation. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first approach achieving bearing-only pose estimation without requiring (i) reciprocal bearings, (ii) all robots to obtain bearing measurements, and (iii) additional sensing capabilities, such as inter-agent rotations. In addition, by leveraging rigidity theory, the proposed method is well-suited to time-varying networks, as distributed methods for maintaining rigidity are well studied in the literature; see [11] and references therein. Motivated by the structure of the proposed observer, we define Vs := {i ∈ V : |O i| ≥ 2}, and Vf := V \ V s. The first group, the angle-sensing robots , consists of those that obtain angles from bearing measurements, while the second group, the angle-free robots , consists of those that measure one or no bearings.
